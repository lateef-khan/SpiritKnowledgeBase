> Keep your bill of sale. **Twenty four (24) months** from the date on the bill of sale or **eighteen (18) months** from the date of factory shipping as determined by the serial number establishes **the warranty period** should service be required.

**Twenty-four months, and this is the only Spirit manual in this knowledge base that says so.** Every other Spirit treadmill, elliptical, bike, climber and stepper prints the same sentence with **twelve (12) months** in the first slot. On an MS300 the sentence gives two years.

**It also says "the warranty period", not "the labor warranty period".** Every sibling manual narrows the sentence to labor. Here it is unqualified, so on its face the sentence dates the whole warranty rather than one column of it. **The manual never says which.** Quote it as printed rather than deciding for the customer.

**Neither figure agrees with the table above it.** The table gives parts and labor three years and the frame lifetime (`ms300-2021-warranty-coverage-periods`), and this paragraph names twenty-four months and eighteen months. Nothing in the book reconciles the two, and nothing says which governs when the bill of sale and the serial number disagree. Say both.

**The eighteen-month fallback is the same as everywhere else.** With no sales receipt the clock runs from the original factory shipping date, read off the serial number - the opening paragraph of the warranty page states it too, in the same words the rest of the range uses.

**What the clinic should do is keep the paperwork.** Keep the bill of sale, and if service is performed, obtain and keep all receipts. The manual adds that this written warranty gives specific legal rights and that other rights vary from state to state.

**This fact is printed in two places.** The eighteen-month fallback is in the opening paragraph on the first warranty page; the twenty-four-month figure is in the Service paragraph three pages later, and only there.
