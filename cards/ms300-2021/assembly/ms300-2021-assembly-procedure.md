Numbers in parentheses are assembly-drawing item numbers. Read each step and study its
drawing before beginning it - the manual's own opening instruction.

## Step 1 - pedals, seat latch handle and handle bar

- Slide the seat carriage (20) all the way back first. Slide the **handle bar assembly
  (17)** onto the receiving tubes of the seat frame (20) and secure it with **two 3/8" x
  3-1/4" bolts (140)**, installed **from the inside hole** of the receiving tube, **four
  flat washers (170)** and **two nylon nuts (190)**.
- **Install the safety cover (108) and the seat belts (233) onto those bolts before you
  put the washers and nuts on.** They cannot be fitted afterwards.
- Install **four 3/8" x 2" bolts (132)** from the top side of the tubes with **four 3/8"
  flat washers (170)** and **3/8" nylon nuts (190)**.
- Attach the seat back **gas shock (109)** to the seat back angle adjustment bracket of
  the handle bar assembly with **one 5/16" x 1-1/4" bolt (135), two 5/16" flat washers
  (173) and one 5/16" nylon nut (193)**.
- Assemble the **swivel seat latch handle (22)** to the seat assembly with **two M6 x
  40mm bolts (129), four 6mm curved washers (185) and two 6mm nylon nuts (187)**.
- Plug the hand pulse connectors from the handle bars into the mating connectors of the
  seat carriage.
- Assemble the **rubber isolators (82)** and **pedals (28)** to the pedal foot plates
  with **six M5 Phillips screws (181) per side**.
- Press the **four stabilizer end caps (31)** into the stabilizer tubes; a rubber mallet
  may help.

## Step 2 - console mast and transport wheels

- Install the **transport wheels (77)** with **two 5/16" x 1-3/4" bolts (152)** and
  **5/16" nylon nuts (193)**.
- Slide the **console mast cover (74)** onto the **console mast (2)** the right way
  round, or it will not clip in place later.
- Snake the computer cable through the mast and slide the mast onto the receiving
  brackets **without pinching the cable**.
- Fasten the mast with **two 3/8" x 2-1/2" bolts (154)**, 3/8" split washers and **two
  3/8" nylon nuts (190)**, then **two 8mm x 16mm bolts (153)** and curved washers (184)
  through the front and rear holes in the mast.

## Step 3 - seat back and bottom cushion

- Slide the **seat back assembly (94)** into the seat back angle adjustment bracket and
  secure with **two 3/8" x 1-3/4" bolts (197), 3/8" washers (167) and 3/8" nuts (190)**.
- Assemble the **seat cushion (93)** onto the seat frame with **four M6 bolts (122)**.

## Step 4 - console, arms and the release levers

- Connect the computer cable into the back of the console and install the **console
  (29)** onto the mast with **four 5mm x 12mm screws (143)**.
- Install the **left and right arms (3 & 4)** with **3/8" x 3/4" bolts (243)** on the
  sides of the arms and **3/8" curved washers (218)** on the front and rear. **Tighten
  these very securely so the arms do not loosen during use.**
- Fit the release levers to the handle bar (17) with the Allen screws. **Set them so the
  patient can reach them comfortably.** They are labelled left and right:
  **the left lever, with the grey cable, is the fore/aft seat adjustment; the right
  lever, with the black cable, is the seat back angle.**

## What this build has that no other Spirit machine does

**Seat belts (233) and a safety cover (108)**, fitted in step 1 onto the handle bar
bolts, and **release levers set to the patient's reach** rather than to a fixed
position. Both come from the medical template this book was made from.

## What the build never does

No step fits a levelling glide - all six arrive on the machine; see
[the levelling card](../../shared/assembly/spirit-rehab-stepper-assembly-levelling-six-glides.md).
There is no first-power-on step in the chapter; the setting-up page earlier in the book
says the MS300 takes any A.C. supply from 100 to 240 volts, 50 to 60 Hz, with the input
at the front. The book prints no transfer board, no strap set and no removable seat -
the wheelchair anchor and the removable seat belong to the 7.5S and 8.5S, not to this
machine.
