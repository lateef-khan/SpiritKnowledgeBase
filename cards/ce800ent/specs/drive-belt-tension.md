**About 185 to 210, measured with a sonic belt tension meter.**

Set it with the **nut on the J bolt** of the idler wheel assembly, after the belt is back on the
drive pulley.

**The manual prints the unit as "185Khz~210Khz". That is a misprint.** A sonic belt tension meter
reads the belt's plucked frequency in **hertz**, not kilohertz; a kHz belt frequency is physically
impossible. Read the figure as **185 Hz to 210 Hz**. The manual gives no other tension figure and
no feel test, so this is all you have.

**The CE800 (2020) service manual prints the same sentence, misprint and all**: *Adjust
the nut which on the J bolt to adjust the tension of the belt to the value (About
185Khz~210Khz by using sonic belt tension meter)*, in its own idler-wheel step. So the
generator CE800-2021 and the mains-powered CE800ENT share this one figure, set at the
same J bolt with a 13 mm open-end wrench
(`ce800-2021-maintenance-belt-drops-off-or-slips-j-bolt-nut-13-mm`). The 2016 CE800
book prints 190 Hz plus or minus 10 instead - a different figure from a different book,
tabled with the rest of the family on
`spirit-elliptical-specs-drive-belt-tension-figure-by-service-manual`.

Do not carry a figure over from a neighbouring machine. The CVC800 climber wants 190 Hz plus or
minus 10, and the CU900ENT bike wants **180 to 210 newtons** - a force, not a frequency, despite
looking almost like this number.
