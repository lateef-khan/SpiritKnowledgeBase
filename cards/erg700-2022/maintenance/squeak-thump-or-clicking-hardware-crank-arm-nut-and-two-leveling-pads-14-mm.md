**This is the ERG700 rower's noise rule, with three reasons. The elliptical version blames dirty rear rails and
the bike version blames the pedals** - `xterra-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking-from-loose-hardware-dirty-rails-or-leveling`,
`xterra-sb-maintenance-thump-clicking-or-wobble-from-loose-pedals-or-crank-arms`.

General Maintenance item 2, as printed:

> If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops the main cause is most likely one of three reasons:
>
> I. **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts that were installed during
> assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger wrench than the one
> provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. I cannot stress this point enough; **90% of calls to the
> service department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware.**
>
> II. **The crank arm nut needs to be retightened.**
>
> III. If squeaks or other noises persist, **check that the unit is properly leveled. There are 2 leveling pads
> on the bottom of the rear stabilizer, use a 14mm wrench (or adjustable wrench) to adjust the levelers.**

Item 1 on the same page is the sweat-path wipe-down
(`xterra-maintenance-wipe-the-sweat-path-with-a-damp-cloth-after-each-workout`). Nothing else on cleaning,
lubrication or rail care is printed for this machine.

## The crank arm line is bike boilerplate

Reason II is the sentence Dyaco prints on its bike pages. The ERG700 is an air-and-magnetic rower with a handle
strap; its assembly chapter fits stabilizers, track, seat and pedals and names no crank arm, and the book has
no parts list to show one. Spirit's CRW800, which shares this frame family, prints the same paragraph with
**two** reasons and no crank arm nut (`crw800-2021-maintenance-two-remedies-and-no-crank-arm-nut`). Follow
reasons I and III on this machine; if a customer asks where the crank arm nut is, the honest answer is that
the sentence was carried over from a bike.

The 14 mm figure is the one hard number on the page: two leveling pads, under the rear stabilizer, turned with
a 14 mm or adjustable wrench. The ERG160, ERG180 and ERG220 books also level at the rear stabilizer but print
no wrench size (their leveling is the assembly section's).
