**Belt figure: 180 +/- 10 Hz on a belt tension gauge.** The CU800 and CU800ENT print 180 to 205 Hz for the same J-bolt and the same three screws; the 4.0R recumbent twin prints 180 +/- 10 Hz too. Set this machine to this figure.

1. Remove the **chain covers (L) (R)**.
2. Use a **13 mm wrench** to loosen the **M8 x 7T nut** on the **J-bolt** until the belt is loose enough to take apart.
3. Use a Phillips head screwdriver to unscrew **3 pcs of M6 x 7T Phillips head screws** on the **idle wheel arm**, and the **belt** can be taken off. To release the idle wheel arm, unscrew **3 pcs of M6 x 15 Phillips head screws** with **3 pcs of Ø1/4" split washers** and **3 pcs of 1/4" x 13 x 1T** (flat washers).
4. To refit the belt and idle arm, use the Phillips head screwdriver to secure the **generator flywheel** with 3 pcs of M6 x 15 Phillips head screws together with 3 pcs of Ø1/4" split washers and 3 pcs of 1/4" x 13 x 1T flat washers. Turn the **drive pulley** to run the belt onto the pulley, hook the J-bolt on the main frame and use the 13 mm wrench to tighten the M8 x 7T nut on the J-bolt until the belt has acceptable tension. **Use a belt tension gauge to make sure the frequency is within 180 +/- 10 Hz.** Last, turn the drive pulley and make sure the drive belt sits in the centre without falling off the pulley.

**Two defects carried over from the CU800 text.** Step 3 calls the idle-arm screws "M6 x 7T" and then "M6 x 15" - 7T is a nut thickness, not a screw length - and its washer line has no part name until step 4 supplies "flat washers". The parts list carries **three M6 x 15 mm Phillips head screws (56)**.

The book's own Q&A sends a slipping belt to "procedures 8-7" to tighten the same nut and a fallen belt to "procedure 8-7" - the procedure is numbered 9-7; there is no chapter 8-7. That adjustment and the idler-alignment advice for a belt that keeps coming off are the maintenance section's.
