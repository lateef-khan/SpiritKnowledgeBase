Chapter 10 Q&A, `Belt Drop`, the whole answer:

> 1. Replace the belt by following procedure 8-7 to install the Belt back to unit then test operation of the motion with rotating the cranks forward and backward then see if it works well? Please try these ways to adjust the idler wheel:
>
> There are some root causes can make driving belt coming off the pulley, but **mostly is the pivot of idler wheel wasn't welded properly and caused idler wheel doesn't align with Pulley/Flywheel.** Another possibility is **the Pulley was not installed properly and it caused pulley and idler wheel out of alignment.** Both rely on assembler to adjust the angle of idler wheel/pivot then rotate the cranks awhile on assembling line to make sure the driving belt won't come off.

**The cause named first is a factory one.** A belt that will not stay on after a correct refit is blamed on the idler wheel's pivot - a weld that left the wheel out of plane with the pulley and flywheel - and the fix offered is to adjust the angle of the idler wheel/pivot and spin the cranks until the belt stays put. The second cause is the pulley itself seated crooked.

**The procedure reference is wrong.** The answer says *procedure 8-7*; the belt and idler wheel replacement is **9-7** in this book (chapter 8 is Error Messages / Troubleshooting). The same slip is printed in the `Belt Slippage` answer above it, which sends you to 8-7 to tighten the M8 x 7T nut on the J-bolt - that re-tensioning is a maintenance procedure, not a fault, and is carded under `section: maintenance`.

**No idler pivot appears in the CU800 answer to the same symptom.** The CU800 2020-book moves the belt one groove and then replaces the drive pulley, bearing housing or flywheel (`cu800-2021-errors-belt-falls-off-one-groove-then-drive-pulley-bearing-housing-or-flywheel`); the older recumbents name the drive pulley, idler or brake (`spirit-recumbent-errors-drive-belt-falls-off-one-notch-then-drive-pulley-idler-or-brake`). The 4.0R (FR800) service manual has no Q&A chapter at all.
