A workload type is chosen during program setup, and there are three:

**Constant Power** - *"The program will automatically control the resistance level at the handles,
depending on user speed, to maintain a steady power workload."* Crank slower and the machine gets
harder; crank faster and it eases. The watts stay put.

**Isokinetic** - *"It provides accommodating resistance at a fixed speed level. The user controls the
resistance at the handles by pushing harder or lighter. **The desired pedaling speed is entered and
the computer increases the resistance automatically if the user tries to overcome the set speed.**"*
The speed stays put and the effort is yours to choose.

**Constant Resistance** - *"It provides a fixed resistance level independent of pedaling speed. The
user controls the power level by pushing harder or lighter."* The level stays put and both speed and
watts are yours.

Speed on this machine can be shown as **RPM or as deg/sec**, and the Pattern programs let you switch
between the two units at setup.

*The manual writes "pedaling" throughout for what the hands do on an arm crank; the Operating
Principle page confirms the machine is hand-driven - "The patient pushes the handles with their
hands."*
