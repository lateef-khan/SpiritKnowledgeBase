**`5 watts up to 1000 watts`**, word for word, under a `Workload` heading.

**Read the top of that range with the machine in mind.** This is an **arm crank**.
1000 watts is the electrical capability of the brake, not a figure a patient will
reach with their upper body - the rehabilitation steppers on the same platform
print the identical range for a leg machine. Quote it as the specification and do
not present it as an expected output.

## The bottom of the range is the useful number here

**5 watts** is the lowest workload the brake will hold, and on a rehabilitation
machine that matters more than the ceiling: a patient recovering from shoulder or
elbow surgery starts at the bottom of the scale. The manual gives no finer step
and no level-to-watts table, so the only two watt figures in the book are these
two end points.

## Watts are displayed left and right separately

The console reports the work being done, and splits it between the two arms - that
split is what the Symmetry mode is built on. See
`85ue-2025-programs-symmetry-index-between-left-and-right-arm-power`. **Those are
measured figures, not settings**, and they are a `programs` question.

## The same range is printed for four steppers, and one prints 650

The 7.0S, 7.5S, 8.5S and 8.5S-FIT recumbent steppers all print
`5 watts up to 1000 watts` - `spirit-climber-specs-workload-5-to-1000-watts` - and
the **MS300 stops at 650 watts**. Those are `product_line: climber` cards for leg
machines. **No Spirit rower states resistance in watts at all**; the CRW800's
Watts window reports work done and its range, 0 to 2000, is a display limit rather
than a capability of the brake.
