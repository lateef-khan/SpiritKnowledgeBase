> **Fuse rating**
> Replace with **only 250V, 5A glass fuse**
> Fast acting		 **5.2 x 20 mm**

**All four parts of that are the specification.**

| Part | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage | **250 V** | the machine accepts 100-240 V mains |
| Current | **5 A** | - |
| Speed | **fast acting** | a time-delay fuse of the same rating is the **wrong part** |
| Body | **5.2 x 20 mm** glass | a 5 x 20 mm fuse is 0.2 mm narrower than the manual asks for |

**The word *only* is the manual's, not a paraphrase.**

## This is the whole of what the book says about it

The manual gives no fuse holder location, no picture, no removal procedure and no
instruction on whether the owner may change it themselves. **It states the part and
stops.** Where a customer asks how to get at it, that is a service question for
Spirit Fitness - and the machine's other fuse-shaped protection, the coin cell
inside the console, is explicitly marked non-removable and professionals-only
(`85ue-2025-specs-specification-page`).

## The same fuse is printed for two steppers, and three of that family have none

The 8.5S-2025 and 8.5S-FIT-2026 recumbent steppers print this row word for word;
the 7.0S, 7.5S and MS300 print **"No user replaceable fuse"** because they run from
an external 12 V Sinpro brick instead of taking mains directly. See
`spirit-climber-specs-fuse-rating`. **The 8.5UE takes mains directly**, like the
8.5S pair, which is why it has a fuse at all - see
`85ue-2025-safety-outlet-100-to-240-volt-50-60-hz-15-amp`.

**No Spirit rower manual states a fuse rating.** None of them prints a
specification page, and three of the five run on batteries with no mains
connection at all. **Do not quote 250 V 5 A for a rower.**
