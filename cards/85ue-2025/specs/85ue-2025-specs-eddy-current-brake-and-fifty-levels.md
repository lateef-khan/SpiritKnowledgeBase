**This is the one machine in the wave whose manual states its mechanism in prose,
and it states it in full.** From the Operating Principle page:

> The patient pushes the handles with their hands. The operator can adjust the
> workload by selecting the increase/decrease indicators on the console to select
> the resistance. When the workload change is requested, **an electromagnet is
> energized at field strengths relative to the workload requested. The magnetic
> field generated by the coil is induced into a flywheel creating eddy currents to
> flow in the flywheel. The eddy currents then create an opposing magnetic field to
> the electromagnetic field creating more or less resistance at the handles**,
> determined by workload setting by the operator.

**An energised coil, not permanent magnets on a swinging bracket.** There is no
gear motor moving a magnet carrier, no brake pad, no fan and no water. The
resistance is electrical, which is why the level count is fifty rather than the
sixteen or twenty of the mechanical Spirit machines, and why the machine needs a
mains supply rather than batteries.

## The specification row and the features page do not agree on the count of modes

**The specification page prints two modes:**

> Resistance: **Constant and Isokinetic with 50 levels of effort**

**The Features page prints three:** `Constant Power`, `Isokinetic` and `Constant
Resistance`. **Both are printed and the book never reconciles them.** The
specification row's "Constant" is doing the work of the features page's two
constant modes, which are genuinely different - Constant Power varies the
resistance with the user's speed to hold a steady wattage, Constant Resistance
holds the resistance and lets the power follow. What each mode does is a
`programs` question -
`85ue-2025-programs-three-workload-types-constant-power-isokinetic-and-constant-resistance`.

**Answer "how many modes" with three, and say the specification page compresses
them to two.** Do not quote the specification row as if it were a complete list.

## Fifty levels, and no level-to-watts table

**Fifty levels of effort.** The manual never says which level produces which
wattage, and prints no level-to-watts table - only the end points of the workload
range, `5 watts up to 1000 watts`. A customer asking what level 30 is worth cannot
be answered from the book. See `85ue-2025-specs-workload-5-to-1000-watts`.

## Never carry a level count across the range

| Machine | Levels | Modes |
|---|---|---|
| **8.5UE upper body ergometer** | **50** | Constant Power, Isokinetic, Constant Resistance |
| 8.5S and 8.5S-FIT recumbent steppers | 50 | constant and isokinetic |
| MS300, 7.0S, 7.5S recumbent steppers | 20 | isokinetic only |
| CRW800 and XRW600 rowers | 16 | one, on a fan braked by a gear motor |
| CRW900 water rower | 10 | a tank adjuster, no console control |
| CRW800H2O water rower | 6 | how much water you pour in |

**The 8.5S steppers share the fifty-level scale and the eddy-current brake** -
`spirit-climber-specs-constant-and-isokinetic-fifty-levels` - because they are the
same Dyaco MED platform. They are a different product line and their cards are
separate; read across with these links and do not merge them.
