The *Service Checklist* row reads, for these books:

*Treadmill will only achieve approximately 10 kph but shows higher speed on display.* This indicates the motor should be receiving power to operate. **Low AC voltage** to the treadmill. Do not use an extension cord. If an extension cord is required it should be as short as possible and heavy duty, **16 AWG minimum**. Low voltage ("Low household voltage"). Contact an electrician or your dealer. A minimum of **220 volt AC current, 50 hz** is required.

This is the export-build wording in the service manual's 10.2 checklist; the owner's manual for the same machine prints 8 mph, 16 gauge and 120 volt (`trx1400-2023-errors-only-reaches-8-mph-16-gauge-120-volt`). Which supply a given TRX1400 is built for is a safety card, not settled here.

The three figures - the speed the treadmill reaches, the cord gauge and the minimum voltage - differ between books: 7 mph / 16 gauge / 110 V (TR300, TR6.6, TR6.4), 8 mph / 16 gauge / 120 V (TRX1400 owner's manual), 5 mph / 14 gauge / 120 V (TR65, TR75, TR85), 10 kph / 16 AWG / 220 V 50 Hz (TRX1400 service manual). Each set has its own card. A belt speed that is wrong but not limited is a calibration matter (`xterra-treadmill-errors-belt-speed-does-not-match-display-calibrate`); the outlet and circuit the machine needs are safety cards.
