**The elliptical has an adjustable stride length.** As the book prints it (under a heading spelt
STRIDE LENGTH ADJSUTMENT):

- At its **lowest (shortest) position the stride length is 18 inches** - "used when pedaling
  slowly, during quick bursts at very high resistance, and to closely simulate a walking motion".
- The **highest (longest) setting is 24 inches**, which "closely simulates the longer stride of a
  running motion".
- The adjustment can also be used simply to pick a stride that is comfortable.

**Where the control is.** "The stride length can be controlled by buttons on the left swing arm and
also can be automatically adjusted during the built-in workout programs." The console drawing labels
those swing-arm switches **Control center toggles (Thumb switch)**, and the wiring pages name a
STRIDE thumb switch and a LEVEL thumb switch on the console interface board. Which programs move the
stride by themselves is a programs question; the book prints no list here.

**The stride motor is the machine's second motor** - a 115 V (or 220 V) AC motor with a position
sensor, separate from the DC tension motor - and its calibration is a key hold on the console
(`spirit-ce850-console-stride-calibration-stride-up-and-start-held-five-seconds`). The 2016 book
sets the same range as a console value of 18 to 24 in half steps
(`spirit-ce850-2016-console-data-ranges-stride-18-to-24-and-level-1-to-20`).

**The 2016 CE800 in the same folder has no stride adjustment**; its second Function is a resistance
level only (`ce800-2016-console-data-ranges-level-0-to-20-pulse-40-to-220-and-a-pace-window`).
