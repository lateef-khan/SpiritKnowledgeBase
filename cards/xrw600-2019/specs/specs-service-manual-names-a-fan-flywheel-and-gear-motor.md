**The owner's manual never says; the service manual does.** The XRW600 owner's manual contains no
*fan*, *flywheel* or *magnet* anywhere (`spirit-xrw600-specs-resistance-mechanism-never-named`).
Its service manual, DW400-YR002, names all three parts of the mechanism:

- **Flywheel & Fan** - one label on the second outline drawing, and a replacement chapter, *9-5 Fan
  & Flywheel*, that separates "Flywheel(23) & Fan(22)" with six M5 x P0.8 x 15L screws.
- **Nylon Net** - the mesh guard over the fan on the first outline drawing.
- **Gear Motor (35)** and **Steel Cable (66)** - chapter *9-6 Gear Motor & Steel Cable*, and the
  no-resistance troubleshooting: "check the connections with Gear Motor(35), Steel Cable(66), and
  Flywheel(23)".

**So it is an air rower whose brake is set by a motor**, the same arrangement as the CRW800
(`crw800-2024-specs-resistance-system`): the console commands a gear motor (the book's *tension
motor*, working at DC 4.5-7.5 V), which winds a steel cable to move a brake against the flywheel.
There is no damper lever and no damper number; the user sets a level 1-16 on the console.

**What the service manual still does not say.** It never uses the word *air* or *magnetic*
either, prints no flywheel weight, fan diameter or drag figure, and its parts callouts carry
item numbers with no part numbers. The XRW600 motor has an eight-pin plug where the CRW800's has
five (`xrw600-2019-specs-tension-motor-connector-8-pin-and-2-pin-speed-sensor`) - the same kind
of mechanism, not the same motor.
