> Be aware that the generator is producing AC power while the StairClimber is being used. Do not
> service the StairClimber while the generator is spinning; serious electric shock could occur.

- **Unplugging the machine does not make it dead.** The staircase drives a generator; a staircase
  that is still turning is still producing power whether or not the cord is in the wall.
- **The condition is the generator spinning, not a user on the machine.** A staircase coasting to a
  stop after someone steps off is still spinning.
- **The manual gives no wait time and no discharge figure.** It does not say how long to leave the
  machine before touching it.

## This warning is printed in the 2019 manual and in none of the later ones

| Manual | Prints the generator warning? |
|---|---|
| CSC900-2019 | **yes**, printed page 5 |
| CSC900-2021 | no |
| CSC900-2024 | no |
| CSC880-2025 | no |

Checked against the PDFs, not the extraction: the word *generator* appears on the 2021, 2024 and
2025 electrical pages nowhere, and in those three books survives only as a warranty-table row
(`GENERATOR BRAKE`). The rest of the 2021 electrical page is otherwise the 2019 page - the two
safety chapters match at 98.4% on an order-free word count - so this bullet was deliberately removed
rather than rewritten.

**The hazard did not go away with the sentence.** All four books still warranty a *generator brake*,
so all four machines still have a generator. Treat a spinning staircase on any of them as live, and
say that the later manuals do not print the warning.

**This is not the elliptical and bike warning.** Those machines have **no mains plug at all** and the
generator is their only supply (`spirit-ce-safety-generator-produces-ac-power`). A CSC900 is fed from
a 120 or 230 VAC wall outlet **as well**, so it has two sources of power and unplugging it removes
only one.
