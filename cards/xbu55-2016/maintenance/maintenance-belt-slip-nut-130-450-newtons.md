**This is the XBU55 2016 upright: an 11 mm wrench on nut 130, to 450 N.** The XBR recumbents of the
same year tighten an M8 × 7T nyloc nut (88) with a 13 mm wrench to the same 450 N
(`spirit-xbr-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-450-newtons`); the CU800 2021 upright tightens nut
77 with a 13 mm wrench to 180–205 Hz
(`cu800-2021-maintenance-belt-slip-right-shroud-180-to-205-hz`).

Q: Slipping Belt — as printed:

1. **Take apart both right shrouds and the right crank arm.**
2. Use an **11 mm open end wrench** to tighten the '**M6 Nyloc Nut (130)**' until a sound wave
   tension gauge reads **450 N**. Since this model is driven by the drive belt (14), the user's
   weight or the way of use may cause the belt to slip; generally the belt will not slip under
   normal usage.
3. Since the drive belt itself has malleability and is expendable, it is normal for it to get
   loose as time passes.

## Two things the same book prints two ways

- **The nut's name.** The Q&A calls part 130 an 'M6 Nyloc Nut'; the replacement procedure 9-9
  calls the same part 130 a **1/4" × 8T Nyloc Nut**, released 'to the end' with the same 11 mm
  wrench before the M10 × P1.25 × 15L button head socket bolt (127) comes out and the bearing
  housing (10) comes apart. Order and name it by the part number, not by either thread size.
- **The figure.** 9-9 step 5 sets the belt after replacement by a **Sonic Tension Meter at
  180 ±10 Hz**; the Q&A says 450 N on a tension gauge. Two instruments, two units, printed five
  pages apart with no conversion. The Sole B94 2016 — the same Dyaco upright under the other brand
  — prints the identical 450 N Q&A and adds 540 N for a new belt
  (`b94-2016-belt-slips-or-falls-off`); this book prints no new-belt figure.

The 'Belt falling Off' answer above it — refit the belt with the chain covers still off, test
whether it comes off, adjust its position and tension, then close the covers — is a diagnosis and
belongs with the Q&A cards. The XBU55 2023 book prints **no slipping-belt Q&A**; its only belt
figure is 180 ±10 Hz inside its drive pulley replacement.
