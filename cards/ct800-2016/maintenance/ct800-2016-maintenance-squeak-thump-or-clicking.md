1. **Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout.**
2. If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops the main cause is most likely one of two
   reasons:
   - **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts that were installed
     during assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger
     wrench than the one provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. The manual adds:
     **"90% of calls to the service department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware."**
   - **The crank arm nut needs to be retightened.**
   - If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly leveled. There are
     **2 leveling pads on the bottom of the rear stabilizer**; use a **14mm wrench** (or adjustable
     wrench) to adjust the levelers.

## The crank arm nut is printed but never explained

The manual says "one of two reasons" and then prints three lettered items. **"Crank arm nut"
appears nowhere else in this manual** - not in the assembly chapter, and this revision carries no
parts list at all. A crank arm is a pedalled-machine part, and the identical sentence is printed in
the Spirit CT900 and CTSBS900 owner's manuals
(`ct900-post-workout-care-and-hardware-check`, `ctsbs900-post-workout-cleaning`). Treat it as
carried over from another product's manual, quote the loose-hardware and levelling checks, and do
not send a customer looking for a crank arm on a treadmill.

## The other CT800 manuals say something different

The **CT800 2012** and **CT800ENT 2022** manuals print **no post-workout or noise section at all**.
The **CT800 2020** manual prints a shorter three-item list with no crank arm nut, no pad count and
no wrench size - `ct800-2020-maintenance-after-each-workout-checks`. Do not read the 14mm figure or
the two rear-stabiliser pads onto those machines.

If the noise is a belt slipping or hesitating rather than a squeak, that is a tension problem:
`ct900ent-belt-tension-adjustment`.
