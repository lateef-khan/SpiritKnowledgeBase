**Neither manual says whether the chest strap is supplied.** The CU800 2012 owner's manual heads
its page simply "Wearing The Chest Strap", with no "(NOT INCLUDED)" and no "(SOLD SEPARATELY)", and
no such sentence appears anywhere else in either scan of the book - the safety page's hand-pulse
paragraph does not carry it either. The AB900 2018 **service** manual prints the same page under the
same bare heading (its owner's manual has no chest strap page at all), and a loose search of that
book for "sold separately", "not included" and "optional" finds only the console's four AA batteries. Every later commercial bike manual that has this page does say the strap is extra. **Do not
read that back onto this machine from a later manual; tell the customer the 2012 book is silent and
check the parts list or the dealer.**

1. Attach the transmitter to the elastic strap using the **locking parts** (the AB900 2018 service
   manual says **interlocking button** here; step 3 says locking parts in both).
2. Adjust the strap as tightly as possible as long as it is not too tight to remain comfortable.
3. Position the transmitter with the logo centred in the middle of your torso, facing away from your
   chest. Some people must position it slightly left of centre. Insert the round end of the elastic
   strap and secure it around your chest with the locking parts.
4. Position the transmitter **directly below the pectoral muscles**.
5. **Sweat is the best conductor**; plain water may also be used to pre-wet the electrodes - the two
   ribbed oval areas on the reverse of the belt and both sides of the transmitter. Wear the strap a
   few minutes before your workout: some users take longer to give a strong steady signal at first,
   and this lessens after "warming up". **The CU800 2012 adds a sentence no other bike manual
   prints: "wearing clothing over the transmitter/strap doesn't affect performance."** The AB900
   2018 service manual does not print it.
6. Stay within range of the console. Wearing the transmitter on bare skin assures proper operation;
   over a shirt, wet the areas of the shirt the electrodes rest on.

**The transmitter switches itself on** when it detects activity from the user's heart and off when
it does not. It is water resistant, but moisture can create false signals, so dry it after use to
prolong battery life. **Estimated transmitter battery life is 2500 hours; the replacement battery is
a Panasonic CR2032.**

Both scans of the CU800 2012 manual print the page identically, word for word. **The AB900 2018
service manual's page matches it apart from the two wordings noted above**: the same six steps, the
same self-activating transmitter note, 2500 hours and a Panasonic CR2032, and - like the CU800 2012 -
no third-party strap sentence, so neither book says whether a Bluetooth or ANT+ strap works. The
AB900's chapter is a service-manual page, not an owner's-manual one; see
`spirit-bike-programs-air-bike-2018-prints-no-heart-rate-training-page`.

**On the CU800 2012, step 6 and step 5 contradict each other slightly.** Step 5 says clothing over
the strap does not affect performance; step 6 says bare skin assures proper operation and that a
shirt must be wetted where the electrodes rest. Both are printed; the manual does not reconcile them.
The AB900 2018 service manual, which lacks the step 5 sentence, has no such contradiction.
