**No number is printed. The manual discusses Q factor for two paragraphs and
never measures it.**

**Q factor is the manual's name for the distance between the pedals.** A
stationary exercise bike is wider than a road bike because it has to make room
for the braking mechanism, pulleys, drive components and beauty covers, and the
wider frame pushes the pedals further apart.

What the CU800-2012 owner's manual actually claims:

- The pedal system is designed so that **the Q factor is the smallest in the
  industry** - a comparative claim, with no figure in millimetres or inches.
- The pedal is **custom designed and tooled to provide a two degree inward
  tilt**, to compensate for the Q factor not being perfect.
- The small Q factor together with the two degree tilt is said to put the user
  into **biomechanical neutral alignment**, so that feet, ankles, knees and hips
  line up.
- The design is credited to research with a leading sports scientist and physical
  rehabilitation expert.

**Two degrees is the only number on the page.** If a customer needs an actual
pedal spacing, it has to come from Spirit Fitness; do not derive one.

## This is the only one of the thirteen commercial bike manuals that mentions it

The word *Q factor* does not appear in the CR800-2021, CR800-2023,
CR800ENT-2023, CR900-2018, CR900-2025, CR900ENT-2021, CU800-2021, CU800ENT-2022,
CU800ENT-2024, CU900-2018, CU900-2025 or CU900ENT-2021 books, and neither does
*footpads*, *inward tilt* or *biomechanical*. The Features chapter that carries
it was dropped after this printing. **Do not assume a later commercial Spirit
bike shares the two degree tilt** - no later manual says so.

## The Sole bikes print the same paragraph, and are a different brand

Four Sole bike cards already hold this same paragraph -
`sole-b74-r72-2015-q-factor`, `sole-b94-r92-2019-pedal-design`,
`lcr-2026-foot-pedal-design` and `r92-2026-foot-pedal-design-and-q-factor` - and
none of them prints a measurement either. **They are Sole cards.** A card carries
exactly one brand, so this Spirit fact was written separately rather than added to
any of them. A Sole card must not be served for a Spirit machine or the reverse:
the claim happens to match, the machine does not.

## Both scans of this manual were compared

The CU800-2012 source is two OCR scans of one book, concatenated. This page
appears in both, as PDF page 14 of scan 1 and PDF page 16 of scan 2, and the two
readings agree on every word that matters - *smallest in the industry*, *two
degree inward tilt*, *biomechanical neutral alignment*. Nothing here rests on a
single OCR pass.
