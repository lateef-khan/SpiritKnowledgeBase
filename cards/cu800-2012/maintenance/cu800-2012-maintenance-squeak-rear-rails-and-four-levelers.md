**This page gives four levelling pads under the rear rails. Every other Spirit commercial bike
owner's manual gives two under the rear stabilizer** —
`cu900ent-general-maintenance`. The wrench is 14 mm on all of them.

1. **Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout.**
2. If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops the main cause is most likely one of two
   reasons:
   - **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts installed during
     assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger wrench
     than the one provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. As printed: *"I cannot
     stress this point enough; 90% of calls to the service department for noise issues can be
     traced to loose hardware **or the rear rails being dirty**."*
   - **Dirt build-up on the rear rails and polyurethane wheels** is also a source of noise.
     Build-up on the rails can cause a thumping sound that you would swear is coming from inside
     the main body of the machine, because noise travels and is amplified in the tubing of the
     frame. Clean the rails and wheels with a **lint free cloth and rubbing alcohol**. Stubborn
     build-up can be removed with your thumbnail or a **non-metallic scraper**, like the back edge
     of a plastic knife. After cleaning, **apply a small amount of lubricant on the rails** with
     your fingers or a lint free cloth. You only need a **thin coat**; wipe off any excess.
3. If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly levelled. There are
   **4 levelling pads on the bottom of the rear rails**; use a **14 mm wrench** (or adjustable
   wrench) to adjust the levellers.

## The rear rails are not on this machine

This is an **upright bike**. It has no seat carriage, no rails and no polyurethane wheels: its
assembly chapter bolts a **Rear Stabilizer** to the main frame with four bolts and four washers,
and the words *rail* and *polyurethane* appear nowhere else in either scan. The only wheels the
manual names are the **two transport wheels**. The paragraph is carried over from a Spirit
elliptical manual, where the rails are real —
`ce800-2013-maintenance-rear-rail-cleaning-and-lubrication` gives the identical steps for the 2013
CE800.

Read the levelling instruction against the machine in front of you: the pads are under the rear
stabilizer, and there are as many of them as the machine has. **Do not send a CU800 owner looking
for rails to scrape or grease.** The contradiction is in the source and is recorded here rather
than smoothed over.

## No crank arm nut on this page

Unlike every later Spirit bike manual, this one never names the crank arm nut as a cause of noise.
"Crank" appears only in the assembly step that screws the pedals into the crank arms — checked
with a spacing-tolerant search, because this manual is entirely OCR and `pdftotext` letter-spaces
some headings.

This is also the **only** Spirit commercial bike owner's manual that names a lubricant of any kind;
the other twelve name none — `spirit-bike-maintenance-no-lubricant-belt-or-battery-service`. The
manual gives no brand, type, part number or interval for it.
