**This is the 2012 CU800 (book titled XU878-AB04M): 170 ±10 Hz, the lowest belt figure in any
Spirit bike book.** The 2020-version CU800 sets 180–205 Hz
(`cu800-2021-maintenance-belt-slip-right-shroud-180-to-205-hz`); the 2009 CR800 and 2016 XBR95
set 180 ±10 Hz (`spirit-recumbent-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-180-hz`).

Section 8-14 Slippage says only:

1. If the belt slips, follow procedures 7-1 and 7-4 and **tighten the M8 × 7T nut on the J-bolt**.
2. If the belt falls off, follow procedure 7 to refit the belt.

Procedures 7-1 and 7-4 are the belt and drive pulley replacement:

- **7-1**: follow procedure 5 to take off the right chain cover; release the three M6 × 15 mm
  screws on the idler wheel assembly with a Phillips screwdriver and loosen the M8 × 7T nut on the
  J-bolt with a **13 mm wrench** until the belt is loose enough to take apart.
- **7-4**: use the 13 mm wrench to tighten the M8 × 7T nut on the J-bolt until the idler wheel
  applies some tension on the belt; use an **acoustic tension gauge to make sure the reading is in
  the range 170 ±10 Hz**; then tighten the three M6 × 15 mm screws on the idler wheel assembly.

So the adjustment is: right chain cover off, the three idler screws loosened, the J-bolt nut
tightened to 170 ±10 Hz on an acoustic gauge, the idler screws tightened again.

The same book's 8-11 notes that the belt wears out eventually and may break or lose tension, and
that a bad idler wheel is usually its bearing — both replacement matters. The 2012 CU800 owner's
manual gives no belt procedure at all; its general-maintenance page is carried over from an
elliptical (`cu800-2012-maintenance-squeak-rear-rails-and-four-levelers`).
