**Two complaints, two answers, and a tolerance the other bike books never state.**

| What shakes | What to do (as printed) |
|---|---|
| **The seat**, obviously shaking during use | 7.3.1: "please refer to step 3.3 to readjust the screws" - the seat-cushion adjustment made with the **13 mm and 14 mm wrenches** (`spirit-med-bike-maintenance-preventative-check-pedals-handlebar-and-seat-cushion`) |
| **The console and console mast**, with "a serious left and right swinging" | 7.3.2: "please refer to step 6.2 knob and quick release locking" - the **adjustment knob** on the mast and its **quick release**, locked. **"(Slight shaking is normal)"** |

Read the bracket literally: the mast is telescopic, held by a knob and a quick-release, and a little movement is
by design. Only a *serious* swing is a fault, and it is cured by locking the two adjusters, not by tightening the
mast bolts.

**Not the CU900's answer and not the 7.0R's.** The CU900 upright sends a shaking seat to one seat connection
screw and a shaking front armrest to its screws (`cu900ent-shaking`); the 7.0R recumbent's seat sways on its wheel
plates and is taken up with an 11 mm and a 13 mm wrench
(`70r-2025-maintenance-swivel-seat-sway-fixing-screw-11-mm-and-13-mm-wrenches`). The CR900 books check the mast
mounting screws whenever the console shakes (`spirit-cr900-maintenance-console-mast-screws`); this book does not
mention them.

The whole bike shaking is not in this chapter; the owner's manual's clicking row sends that to the leveling
feet, and the care page to the two rear pads with a 14 mm wrench (`cu900ent-general-maintenance`).
