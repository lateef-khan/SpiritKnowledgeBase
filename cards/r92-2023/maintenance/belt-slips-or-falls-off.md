**The belt slips when pedalling**

1. Take apart both left and right front shrouds (29, 30).
2. Use a **13 mm wrench** to turn the **M8 x 7T nyloc nut (88) clockwise** until the reading is **450 N**. Because the machine is belt driven, slippage is possible depending on the weight of the user or the way it is used. Slippage is generally rare.
3. The drive belt is malleable and expendable. It is normal for it to loosen over time.

**The belt falls off when pedalling**

Follow **section 9.7** of the parts replacement guide to install the drive belt. After installation and adjustment, rotate the drive pulley forward and backward as a functional test on the **drive belt (54)**. If the belt dislodges, adjust it in the **opposite direction of the displacement, one notch at a time**, and repeat the test. If it persists, inspect and potentially replace the **drive pulley (20)**, the **idler wheel assembly (10)** or the **generator/brake (55)**. If it is still unresolved, the main frame could be deformed and the machine needs replacing. That is rare, because each machine is tested before shipment.

**Two wording problems in this answer.**

- Step 2 says "sound wave frequency falls between 450N", mixing a frequency reading with a force unit. Section 9.7 step 8 gives it correctly as a belt tension gauge reading of 450 N.
- The falling-off answer names a "Generator/Brake (55)". Section 9.7 calls part 55 the **flywheel**, and this bike brakes with a gear motor and steel cable, not a generator. The LCR 2023 manual, which does use a generator brake, prints the same sentence.
