**This is the CU800 2020-version upright: the belt is reached from the right side, at nut 77, and
set to 180 to 205 Hz.** The CR800 recumbent of the same year opens both front shrouds and turns
nut 88 (`cr800-2021-maintenance-belt-slip-180-to-205-hz`); the CU800 2012 reads 170 ±10 Hz
(`cu800-2012-maintenance-belt-slip-170-hz`).

Q: Slipping Belt — as printed:

1. Take apart **both right shrouds and the right crank arm**.
2. Use a **13 mm open end wrench** to tighten the **M8 × 7T nylon nut (77)** until the **sound wave
   tension gauge reads 180~205 Hz**. Since this model is driven by the drive belt (14), the user's
   weight or the way of use may cause the belt to slip; generally the belt will not slip under
   normal usage.
3. Since the driving belt itself is malleable and expendable, it is normal for it to get loose as
   time passes.

The nut is on the J-bolt (85). The replacement procedure 10-8 tightens the same nut with the same
wrench 'until the Belt (14) is with acceptable tension', checks 180~205HZ with an audio tension
gauge, and turns the drive pulley (15) to make sure the belt sits in the centre without falling
out.

The Sole LCB 2016 and 2019 — the same Dyaco upright under the other brand — print this answer
word for word (`lcb-2016-belt-slips-or-falls-off`).

The 'Belt falling off' answer beneath it — install per assembly procedure 4, test forward and
backward, move the belt one groove toward the side it falls off, replace the drive pulley (15),
the bearing housing (10) or the flywheel (20), then the whole unit — is a diagnosis and belongs
with the Q&A cards.

The CU800ENT 2022 book, which shares this template, prints **no slipping-belt Q&A**; its only belt
figure is the same 180~205HZ inside its replacement step 8-8. The CU800 owner's manuals print no
belt work (`spirit-bike-maintenance-no-lubricant-belt-or-battery-service`).
