The CIC850's resistance comes from **permanent magnets carried on two moving brake blocks**, left
and right, that swing toward the flywheel as the resistance knob turns
(`spirit-cycle-specs-resistance-systems`). A block that touches the flywheel makes a friction
noise; the book's fix is a gap, not a pad change.

As printed under Noise Troubleshooting, cause 1 — 'Flywheel friction brake block to cause noise':

1. **Turn the hex screw clockwise with the M6 open-end wrench.**
2. If the **brake block (right)** touches the flywheel, **push the brake block (right) in the
   opposite direction to keep the gap between the brake block (right) and the flywheel at
   1.5 mm.**
3. **Check the brake block (left) the same way.**

That is the whole procedure: one hex screw per block, one figure, both sides. The manual does not
say where the hex screw is beyond its photograph, names no feeler gauge and gives no torque.

**No pad, no oil.** The XIC600 indoor cycle has a leather brake pad that is inspected weekly and
oiled (`spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-schedule-wd40-and-weekly-brake-pad`); the CIC850's
magnetic blocks are checked for wear monthly and take nothing
(`spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-schedule-water-and-monthly-brake-pad`). Do not oil a CIC850
brake block.

If the gap cannot be set, the block comes off with an M17 open-end wrench on its nylon nut and two
M4 × 8 cross-head screws — the 10-2 replacement procedure, an assembly card. The other noise cause
on the same page, worn pedal bearings, is a replacement too.
