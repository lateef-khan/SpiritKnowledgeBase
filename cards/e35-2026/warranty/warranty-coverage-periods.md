**Warranty effective February 1, 2026.**

| Component | Period |
| --- | --- |
| **Frame Weldments** | **Lifetime** |
| **Parts** | **2 Years** |
| **Labor** | **1 Year** |
| **Cosmetic Items\*** | **90 Days** |

**\* Cosmetic items including, but not limited to the following: grips, console overlays, and labels/decals.**

Every period runs from the warranty start date, which is the date of retail sale shown on the sale receipt, or 18 months from the original factory shipping date where there is no receipt.

**The table has only these four rows.** There is no separate line for electronics, the console, the incline motor or the resistance gear motor, so anything that is not frame weldment and not a cosmetic item falls under **Parts, 2 Years**.

**Labor is stated in two places and both halves apply.** The table row says **1 Year**. The Service paragraph on the same page says **"twelve (12) months from the date on the bill of sale or eighteen (18) months from the date of factory shipping as determined by the serial number"**. The 12 months is the same figure; the Service paragraph adds what happens when there is no bill of sale.

**Where these figures come from.** This table is a graphic in the PDF and `pdftotext` dropped it. The ingested `sources/sole-elliptical-e35-2026-owners-manual/text.md` carries the sentence "The warranty period applies to the following components:" followed by nothing but the cosmetic footnote. The four rows above were read from printed page 27 rendered as an image with `pdftoppm`. A reader searching the ingested text for "Lifetime" or "2 Years" will not find them, and that is why - not because the manual omits them.

**The parts term is not the same across the 2026 elliptical line.** The E95-2026 warranty table gives **Parts 3 Years** where this table gives **Parts 2 Years**. Frame Weldments, Labor and Cosmetic Items are identical on both pages. The two pages also carry different effective dates: this one is headed **"Effective February 1, 2026"**, while the E95-2026 page is headed **"Effective February 1, 2023"**. Both are reproduced as printed and neither is adjudicated here. Do not carry a parts term from one machine to the other.
