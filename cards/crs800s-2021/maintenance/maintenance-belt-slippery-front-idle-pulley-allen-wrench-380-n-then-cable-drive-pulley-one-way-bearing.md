**One figure, 380 N, and it is printed only here.** The 8.5S sets its belt to 80 N and the 7.5S its two belts to
320 N and 240 N (`85s-2025-maintenance-belt-slip-idler-screw-clockwise-to-80-n-then-slide-wheel-one-way-bearing`, `7-5s-med-maintenance-belt-slip-584-mm-belt-320-n-and-1032-mm-belt-240-n-then-cable-guide-wheel-one-way-bearing`); the CS800 and XS895 use a sonic meter at 190 Hz (`cvc800-drive-belt-slipping`).
The CRS800S owner's manuals print no belt figure.

9-5 Belt Slippery, as printed:

1. "**Use Allen Wrench to adjust front Idle Pulley** (Remark: **Belt tension is 380N**)."
2. "If the above adjustment finishes the belt slippery still exists, then **One Way Bearing in Cable Drive Pulley is
   abnormal** and it needs to be changed. (Please refer【Cable and Belt】and notice the direction of One Way Bearing)"

The figure shows the idle pulley between the two cable drive pulleys with the hex key in its tensioner from above.
No Allen-key size and no gauge are named; the target is a force.

【Cable and Belt】 is 9-1 Steel Cable and Belt replacement, whose step 7 takes off the cable drive pulley with
retaining-ring pliers and "adjust[s] Idle Pulley to loosen the Driving Belt" - the same tensioner from the other
direction - and whose step 8 changes the belt. Both are assembly cards. The one-way bearing sits inside the cable
drive pulley, the pulley the pedal cable winds onto, and lets it turn freely one way; a failed one slips however
tight the belt is.

**Two sensor gaps are set on the same pulley afterwards**, in 9-3: the optical sensor board 7-9 mm from the cable
drive pulley, and the hall sensor 1-2 mm from its magnet. Those are the speed, watt and calorie checks, an errors
question, but a pulley change disturbs them.
