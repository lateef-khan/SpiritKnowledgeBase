**This machine has 4 levelling pads — 2 at the front and 2 at the back — and its manual names no
wrench size and no crank arm nut.** Its own 2024 successor prints 2 pads on the rear stabiliser and
a 14 mm wrench instead (`ct800-2016-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking`). The figures changed
between the two printings of one machine, so check which book you are quoting.

Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout. If a squeak, thump,
clicking or rough feeling develops, the main cause is most likely one of two reasons:

1. **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts that were installed
   during assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger
   wrench than the one provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. **90% of calls to the
   service department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware.**
2. If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly levelled. **There are 2
   levelling pads on the front and 2 levelling pads on the back of the unit.**

## Three things this page does not say

- **No wrench size.** Every other Spirit stepper and climber manual that gives a pad count also
  gives a tool — 14 mm on the CS800, CVC800, XS895, 7.0S and 7.5S, ½" on the MS300. This one gives
  the count and stops. Do not import 14 mm from a sibling book.
- **No crank arm nut.** The phrase does not occur anywhere in the manual. The 2024 CRS800S manual
  puts it back, as a carry-over on a machine that has no crank arm.
- **No location for the pads beyond "front" and "back".** There is no stabiliser named and no
  diagram reference.

## If the roughness is underfoot rather than a squeak

Dirt on the pedal carriage rails and wheels feels rough rather than noisy, and the same manual
prints a cover-off cleaning procedure for it on the same page —
`crs800s-2024-maintenance-step-rail-and-carriage-cleaning`, which the 2021 and 2024 books print
word for word.
