**When the drive belt slips, check whether it is too loose.** If it is, remove the **M10 caps** with the **17 mm T-socket** (the book's "17th T-Shirt") and adjust the belt to the required tension (Figures 8.33 and 8.34).

That is the preventative-maintenance instruction. The figure it does not give is printed in the motor replacement procedure on the same chapter's later pages: **the drive belt tension can be adjusted to 80-90 lbs** (printed "80-90BLS"), set with the 17 mm wrench before the M10 caps are secured. The M10 caps are the four nuts that hold the motor down; loosening them lets the motor move to tension the belt, and the book says to push the motor forward and hook the belt back on before setting the tension.

## This is not the running belt

A slipping *running* belt is a rear-roller adjustment, which this book does not describe beyond "adjust the running belt tension" (`ct1000ent-2023-maintenance-centring-the-belt-through-the-adjustment-base`). A belt that hesitates underfoot even though the running belt is tight is the case this check is for.

## Other Spirit machines print other figures

The CT900 and CT900ENT service manuals set their drive belt by a **sonic tension meter at 120-130 Hz** with a 13 mm socket; the CT800 2020 family says **70-75 lbs** on a tension gauge with a 14 mm wrench. None of those is the CT1000ENT figure.
