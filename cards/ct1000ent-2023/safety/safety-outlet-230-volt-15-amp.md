**This is a 230-volt machine, and 15 amps - not the 120-volt, 20-amp figure of the CT850 and CT900ENT
and not the 220-volt, 10-amp figure the XT service manuals print for their 230-volt version.**

> To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the treadmill on a
> flat level surface with access to a 230-volt, 15-amp grounded outlet with only the treadmill
> plugged into the circuit.

- **230 volt, 15 amp, grounded.**
- **Only the treadmill on that circuit.** Nothing else may share it.
- **A flat, level surface.**

**The book agrees with itself on the voltage.** The grounding instructions on the same page say
`This product is for use on a nominal 230-volt circuit`, the circuit diagram is headed
`Circuit Diagram(230V)`, and the photograph of the front of the motor cover shows an `AC230V` label
under the power inlet, breaker button and rocker switch. The controller-debugging row asks for a
supply of `220~230V` and the troubleshooting matrix for `220V` with the aside
`(On 120Vac electronic power system need 110V)` - so a 120-volt build exists, but this book's safety
chapter prints one figure only, and it is 230-volt, 15-amp.

**No NEMA plug type and no frequency is printed.** The two breaker part numbers this page goes on
to name - Grainger # 1D237 and www.squared.com # QO120HM - are the US 120-volt examples every Dyaco
safety chapter carries (`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`); they are not 230-volt
parts, and the manual never says so.

**This service manual is the only CT1000ENT document on file.** There is no owner's manual to
compare it with, so this is the machine's only printed supply figure. The extension-cord rule on
the same page is 14 AWG (`ct850-2020-extension-cord-14-awg-or-better`), the GFCI prohibition is
`spirit-never-use-a-gfci-wall-outlet`, and the grounding page, adapter included, is
`spirit-product-must-be-grounded` and `spirit-temporary-adapter-for-a-two-pole-receptacle`.
