**This is the SB240 page, which prints "Class C"; the SB600 page prints "Class B" and adds UL.** The page opens
with the certification list - **"FCC part 15"** and **"Bluetooth (with FTMS)"** - then the declaration: "This device
complies with part 15 of the FCC Rules. Operation is subject to the following two conditions: (1) this device must
not cause harmful interference, and (2) this device must not accept any interference received, including
interference that may cause undesired operation."

The NOTE: "This equipment has been tested and found to comply with the limits for a **Class C digital device**,
pursuant to Part 15 of the FCC Rules", with the usual residential-installation wording and four remedies if it
interferes with radio or television reception: reorient or relocate the receiving antenna; increase the separation
between the equipment and receiver; connect the equipment to an outlet on a circuit different from the receiver's;
consult the dealer or an experienced radio/TV technician.

CAUTION: "To comply with FCC RF exposure compliance requirements, a separation distance of at least **20 cm** must be
maintained between the antenna of this device and all persons", and "This transmitter must not be co-located or
operating in conjunction with any other antenna or transmitter."

Two things on the page read as misprints, and the card keeps the printed text. Part 15 has no "Class C" for digital
devices; the sister SB600 page, the Xterra WS treadmill page and the Spirit XT page all print Class B, and the only
other Class C in this knowledge base is the Sole spin-bike console's "Class C Low Power Communication Device"
(`sole-spinner-fcc-compliance`), which is a different clause. And condition (2) is printed as "must **not** accept
any interference", where the standard sentence (and the WS treadmill page) reads "must accept". No other Xterra bike,
elliptical or stepper book prints an FCC page.
