The rate of perceived exertion (**RPE**), also known as the **Borg scale**, was developed by the
Swedish physiologist G.A.V. Borg. It rates exercise intensity from **6 to 20** by how you feel.

**Add a zero to your rating to get an approximate heart rate.** A workout that felt "comfortable" is
a **12**, so about **120** beats per minute.

| Rating | Perception of effort | Rating | Perception of effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Minimal | 14 | Somewhat hard + |
| 7 | Very, very light | 15 | Hard |
| 8 | Very, very light + | 16 | Hard + |
| 9 | Very light | 17 | Very hard |
| 10 | Very light + | 18 | Very hard + |
| 11 | Fairly light | 19 | Very, very hard |
| 12 | Comfortable | 20 | Maximal |
| 13 | Somewhat hard | | |

The page introduces the scale by saying heart rate is not the only variable: stress level, physical
health, emotional health, temperature, humidity, the time of day, and when and what you last ate all
contribute to the intensity at which you should work out.

**The same table, arranged two ways.** The XRW600 books and the 2024 CRW800 print the add-a-zero
rule **after** the table and follow it with a paragraph on training by feel - a strong, rested body
makes the pace feel easier and the RPE supports training harder, a tired one makes it feel harder
and the RPE holds you back. **The 2021 CRW800 prints the add-a-zero rule before the table instead**,
in the paragraph that opens the section, and omits that closing paragraph. The scale itself is
identical on all four.

**None of these machines has a heart rate program**, so RPE and the chest strap are the only ways the
books offer to judge intensity.

*The 2024 CRW800 prints the table as one 6-to-20 column and heads the page HEART RATE - PERCEIVED
EXERTION; the other three print two columns under the heading Rate of Perceived Exertion. The
ratings and words are the same.*
