**There is nothing to program on these two bikes.** No workout program, no profile, no preset, no
manual mode, no timer, no fitness test, no heart rate zone and no heart rate reading of any kind -
**because neither bike has a console at all.** The absence of the console is proved page by page on
`spirit-bike-console-no-console-at-all-tension-knob-only`; this card answers the programs question
that follows from it.

A loose search for `program` returns **one** hit in each manual, and both are safety boilerplate
about consulting a doctor to determine an exercise program suitable for your fitness level. Neither
manual contains `heart rate`, `pulse` or `bpm` even once.

**How you set the intensity instead - by hand, at the knob.**

- **CB900 2013** - "Pedaling resistance is controlled by the **tension knob**. Resistance can be
  changed at any time by turning tension knob: clock-wise for more resistance; counterclockwise for
  less resistance." To brake, press down on the tension knob.
- **CIC800 2021** - "Turn the knob **clockwise (+)** to increase resistance. Turn the knob
  **counterclockwise (-)** to decrease resistance." Pressing down on the same knob stops the flywheel
  and pedals.

**There are no numbered resistance levels on either machine.** The knob is continuous, neither manual
prints a scale, and there is no display to show a number. **Do not quote a level range from another
Spirit bike** - the JB950's twenty levels and the CR800/CU800 forty levels belong to machines with a
console that drives a motor.

**These are not the XIC600 or the CIC850.** Those two indoor cycles do carry a console - a cycling
console with a speed sensor, a timer and a heart rate target zone - but still have no workout
programs. See `spirit-bike-programs-cycling-console-has-no-workout-programs`.
