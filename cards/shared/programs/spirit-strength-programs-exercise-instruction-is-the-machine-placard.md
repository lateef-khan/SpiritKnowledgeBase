**Thirty-one of these manuals answer "how do I do the exercise?" with a photograph of
a sticker.** The book carries one page headed **START WORKOUT**, and that page shows
the placard fitted to the machine itself. There is no prose beside it.

What the manual therefore does **not** print, anywhere:

- the name of a single muscle;
- a starting position, a grip, a seat or pad setting written as a step;
- a range of motion, a tempo or a breathing cue;
- any rep count, set count, rest interval or training programme.

Two placard shapes appear:

- **The 28 CSS and CSD machines** use a wide black band. Left to right it gives the
  exercise name, a front and a back body figure with the worked muscles picked out,
  and a **START** and a **FINISH** drawing of a person on the machine. A dual-station
  CSD placard repeats that block once per exercise.
- **CSF-AABB and CSF-BEXT** use a portrait placard - Spirit logo, exercise name, and a
  two-frame drawing of the movement. **No body figure and no START/FINISH captions.**
  **CSF-FUNT** is a pair of tall placards listing ten exercises.

**The placard is a part of the machine, not of the book.** On 29 of these machines the
`Parts of Your Unit` drawing labels it **Workout Diagrams**, alongside the guide rods
and the weight selector pin. Twenty-three of the 31 pages also print a placard part number,
so a worn or missing placard can be ordered; the per-machine cards carry the numbers.

**Read the page, not the extraction.** `pdftotext` returns only the words *START
WORKOUT* and, where it exists, the part-number line. The exercise name and every
caption on the placard are drawn, so a text-layer search finds nothing and can be
mistaken for a machine that has no exercise page.

The three CSF machines that have no such page at all are a separate finding - see
`spirit-strength-programs-no-exercise-instruction-at-all`. The two CSI machines put
this material on the console instead - see
`spirit-strength-programs-how-to-use-lives-on-the-console`.
