*"The industry standard test for measuring **lower body** power and fatigue rates. It can also be
used for anaerobic assessment."* The 8.5UE arm ergometer prints this page for **upper** body power;
do not answer one from the other.

The Wingate typically involves **30 seconds of maximal exercise** on either an arm-crank or leg-cycle
ergometer. **Protocols can range from 10 to 300 seconds** depending on the sport or event; at the U.S.
Olympic Training Centers sprint track cyclists typically perform a 30- or 18-second test, road and
endurance track cyclists a 30-second test.

## How the console runs it

- **Set the user's weight and the force changes to 5% of the user's weight.** The force can also be
  changed manually.
- **Test time can be set to 15~300 seconds.**
- **There is a 5 minute warm up before the test starts.** A button skips it; another changes the
  warm-up resistance.
- **A three second countdown begins the test after the warm up.** Ask the user to pedal as fast as
  they can.
- The screen shows a **power-time chart**, the remaining time and the current watts; a button aborts
  the test.

## The protocol the page describes

After a five-minute warm-up **including three sprints at varying resistances**, the user may get off
the bike for a **three-minute recovery** or stay on and spin lightly, then pedals as fast as possible
with little or no resistance; **within three seconds a fixed resistance is applied to the flywheel**
and the user continues "all out" for the duration of the test. The resistance is a percentage of body
weight, applied as electromagnetic force: *"a 70kg user with a load of 5% of his/her total body
weight would have 5.25kg added to the flywheel."*

*The example is arithmetically wrong as printed - 5% of 70 kg is 3.5 kg, and 5.25 kg is 7.5% - and
the 8.5S and 8.5UE books print the same faulty example. Recorded, not repaired.*

The 8.0U and 8.5R 2025 books print this page word for word.
