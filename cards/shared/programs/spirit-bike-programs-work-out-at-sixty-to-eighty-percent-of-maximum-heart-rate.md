All three manuals print the same four-part TRAINING INFORMATION page.

- **Before beginning.** Make sure the seat is adjusted for your body size and comfort. Consult your
  physician before beginning this or any other exercise routine. Stop any exercise that causes
  discomfort and consult a medical expert.
- **Warm-up.** Begin pedalling slowly. A gradual warm-up prepares the muscles and cardiovascular
  system for a more intense workout and helps prevent injury. **Your warm-up is sufficient once your
  breathing rate begins to increase and you begin to perspire lightly. The warm-up period should
  last about five minutes.**
- **Work out.** A brisk and rhythmic workout trains the muscles and cardiovascular system to perform
  at a higher efficiency. **The key is to exercise aerobically, typically at 60% - 80% of your
  maximum heart rate.**
- **Cool-down.** Slow and relaxed activity after a workout lets the muscles and cardiovascular system
  return gradually to a resting level.

**Two wordings differ by machine.** On the air bikes the "before beginning" and warm-up paragraphs
say to hold the **movement arms**; on the JB950 they say the **handlebar**, and the JB950 adds the
handlebar and pedal straps to the list of things to adjust first. The figures are the same in all
three.

**This 60% - 80% is general advice, not a console setting.** It is not what any of these consoles
targets:

- The **air bikes' Target Heart Rate mode** aims at **65% - 85%**.
- The **JB950's HR Zone program** tracks five zones from under 59% to over 90% and estimates maximum
  heart rate as **208 - (0.7 x age)**, not 220 minus age.

**The JB950 also warns about pace.** Its instructions page says lower resistance levels let you pedal
faster and load the cardiovascular system, while higher levels give more muscle strength and
endurance at lower RPM, and that RPM rates vary dramatically with the type of ride. If you are unsure
how to use the RPM and resistance system, it tells you to seek a qualified indoor cycling instructor.
