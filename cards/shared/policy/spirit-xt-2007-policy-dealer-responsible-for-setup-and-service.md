**"It is the responsibility of the Dealer to assemble and setup any Spirit product they have sold, as well as diagnose problems and perform service, regardless of the product's location."** That sentence opens the dealer manual's customer-service page. Spirit's part is technical support: recognising the importance of the consumer's satisfaction, Spirit technical support "will always be available to assist the Dealer in diagnosing a problem" - assist, not take over.

**Using a third-party service company does not move the responsibility.** The Warranty Policy page states it as Spirit's policy that all dealers service the products they sell to end users. A dealer that chooses an independent company for service:

- is responsible for all of that company's service work;
- is still bound by Spirit's labour reimbursement policy in every case, and any charge above Spirit's one-time flat completion fee is billed back to the selling dealer;
- is responsible to the service company for every charge Spirit disallows.

Spirit will in certain circumstances work directly with an independent service company, but will not pay for any service that deviates from its dealer policy, and any deviation must be approved in advance, in writing.

What the flat fee is and how it is claimed is on `spirit-xt-2007-warranty-labor-reimbursement-80-dollar-flat-fee`; how to reach the technicians is on `spirit-xt-2007-policy-contacting-customer-service`. The 2008 dealer manual for the XBR25 and XBR55 recumbent bikes prints both passages word for word, so the rule is the same for a dealer-sold bike. The only case in which Spirit deals with the consumer itself is when there is no dealer or service organisation at all (`spirit-xt-2007-warranty-five-years-or-eighteen-months-from-shipping`).
