**The woodruff key goes in with its rounded part facing inward, toward the axle.** Both books say so.

The XS200-SS003 book (cs800-2016):

1. Remove the linked assembly (the linked assembly card).
2. Use a **12 mm open-end wrench** to remove the **hex head bolt 5/16" x 3/4"** and **flat washer Ø8.5 x 26 x 2.0T**
   securing the crank axle of the crank arm.
3. Use an **L Allen wrench and a #13 open-end combination spanner wrench** to loosen the **outer nut M8 x 6.3T**.
   Use the #13 wrench and a **hex wrench (M6)** to loosen the **inner nut M8 x 6.3T** and remove the crank arm.
4. When reassembling, place the crank arm into the centre of the crank axle. Then place the **woodruff key with
   the rounded part facing inward**. Reassemble the remaining parts in the reverse order. *(Tighten socket head
   cap bolt in range of 550 Lbs.)*

**The CS800 (2020) book (cs800-2021)** prints the same moves without the torque: a 12 mm wrench for a hex head
bolt and a flat washer locking the crank; **an M6 L-Allen wrench and a 13 mm wrench to loosen the bolt (M8 x
35L)**, then take off the crank arm assembly; reverse to refit; *the direction of the woodruff key, the round head
directs to the axle.*

**"550 Lbs" has no length unit.** It is printed exactly so in the 2016 book and in its Sole SC200 twin, and the
Sole CC81 book prints "approximately 550 pounds with a torque wrench"; none of them says in-lb or ft-lb, and
the 2020 CS800 book drops the figure. Treat it as the book's figure, not a verified torque. The 2016 book's slip
troubleshooting also names this joint: check the M8 x 35 mm socket head cap bolt and the M8 x 6.3T nut on the
crank arm assembly first.
