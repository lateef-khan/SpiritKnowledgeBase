**This is the generator-brake CU800 (2020) and CU800ENT procedure, with a belt figure of 180 to 205 Hz.** The XBU55 upright (gear-motor, 180 +/- 10 Hz) and the 2012 CU800 (170 +/- 10 Hz) have their own cards.

1. Remove the **chain covers (L) (R) (36) (37)**.
2. Use a **13 mm wrench** to release the **M8 x 7T nut (77)** and take off the **J-bolt (85)**. Use a Phillips head screwdriver to unscrew **3 pcs of M6 x 7T Phillips head screws** on the **idle arm (9)**, and the **belt (14)** can be taken off.
3. To release the idle arm (9), unscrew **3 pcs of M6 x 15 Phillips head screws (56)** with **3 pcs of Ø1/4" split washers (73)** and **3 pcs of 1/4" x 13 x 1T flat washers (69)**.
4. To refit the belt (14) and idle arm (9), use a Phillips head screwdriver to secure the **generator flywheel (20)** with 3 pcs of M6 x 15 Phillips head screws, 3 pcs of Ø1/4" split washers (73) and 3 pcs of 1/4" x 13 x 1T flat washers (69). Turn the **drive pulley (15)** to run the belt onto the pulley, hook the J-bolt on the main frame and use the 13 mm wrench to tighten the M8 x 7T nut (77) on the J-bolt until the belt (14) has acceptable tension. **Use an audio tension gauge to make sure the frequency is within 180 to 205 Hz.** Last, turn the drive pulley (15) and make sure the drive belt (14) sits in the centre without falling off the pulley.

**Two defects in the printed steps, in both books.** Step 2 calls the idle-arm screws "M6 x 7T Phillips head screws" and step 3 calls the same three screws "M6 x 15 Phillips head screws (56)" - 7T is a nut thickness, not a screw length. And step 3 ends "3 pcs of 1/4" x 13 x 1T (69)" without the part name; step 4 supplies it as flat washers.

The heading says "Drive Belt and Bearing Housing"; the steps name the part the **idle arm (9)** throughout.

The same belt figure appears in the book's slipping-belt answer (13 mm wrench on the M8 x 7T nylon nut until the sound-wave tension gauge reads 180 to 205 Hz). That adjustment, and the belt-falling-off advice, belong to the maintenance section.
