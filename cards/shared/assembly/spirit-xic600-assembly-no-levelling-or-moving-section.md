**Neither XIC600 printing has an INDOOR CYCLE SETUP section.** The assembly chapter runs
pre-assembly, five steps, then straight into seat and handlebar adjustment. The two
halves of that gap are not the same, so keep them apart:

- **Levelling is answered, but not here.** One sentence, in the maintenance chapter.
- **Moving the bike is not answered at all**, anywhere in either book.

The levelling sentence is printed as the third cause of a squeak:

> There are 2 leveling pads on the bottom of the rear stabilizer, use a 14mm wrench
> (or adjustable wrench) to adjust the levelers.

**That is the answer to "how do I level my XIC600"** - two pads under the rear
stabiliser, and the 14/15mm wrench (#85) in the carton fits. It is held by
[the levelling card](../../ct800-2016/assembly/ct800-2016-assembly-levelling-pads.md),
which covers twenty-six Spirit machines that print the same sentence in the same place.
What the XIC600 lacks is the *setup* instruction its sister machines print, not the
figure.

## The bike does have wheels, on the front stabilizer

Assembly step one says to fit "the stabilizer with the wheels onto the front of the
bike", so the front stabilizer carries wheels. **Nothing tells you how to use them** -
no tilt-and-roll instruction, no direction to lift from. The CB900, CIC800 and CIC850
manuals all print one; the XIC600 books do not. See
[the transport card](spirit-cycle-assembly-transport-wheels-engage-when-the-rear-is-lifted.md)
for what those machines say, and do not assume it transfers.

A loose-word search for `level`, `transport`, `wheel` and `caster` across both
extractions; a spacing-tolerant search for `L *E *V *E *L`, because this extractor
letter-spaces some headings; and a 300 dpi render of every page of the assembly chapter
compared against the native extraction, which matched within a word or two on all of
pp. 7-18. No page of the chapter is a flattened image hiding text.
