**The end cap and its screw are pre-installed onto the shaft. Remove them before installing
the pad, and restore them after the pad is mounted.**

The cap retains the pad, and it is fitted at the factory so the shaft does not lose its
screw in transit. There is nothing wrong with the pad - it will not pass the cap.

**Three names for the same part and three names for the pad**, one book to the next:

| Machine | The part on the shaft | The pad | Item numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abdominal | Aluminum end cap | Roll pad | 46, 88 |
| Back extension | aluminum plate | round pad (88) | 47, 79 |
| Leg extension | Aluminum end cover | Leg pad (104) | 53, 84 |
| Prone leg curl | Aluminum end cover | Round Pad (95) | 63, 80 |
| Seated leg curl | Aluminum end cover | Roll pad | 54, 91 |

**Read the item numbers off your own step table**; nothing about them carries across.
The cap itself is Φ50xΦ8.5x11 on the leg extension and prone leg curl, and its screw is
an M8x25 countersunk head hexagon socket screw on both.

## An end cap in a parts list is not this note

The CSD ab/back extension and biceps/triceps books list an **Aluminum end cap** in their
step tables and print no such note - on those two the cap is simply a part, not something
to take off first. Do not carry this instruction onto them.

The same panel usually carries the seat pad, the D-shape pad and the socket head cap
screws that hold them, and its quantities differ per machine.
