**Belt tension is given in hertz, not pounds: 120 ~ 130 Hz, set with a 13 mm T-socket on the tensioner.** The
CT1000ENT book, which shares this guide, gives 80-90 lbs for its own motor and is on a separate card.

1. Remove the motor cover (step 4.2.1 of the guide: eight umbrella head screws).
2. Use a screwdriver to remove the motor ground wire (**green yellow**) and the motor's wires from the control
   panel.
3. "Remove the motor under the cover button" - printed exactly so; the figure beside it (4.3.2) is the only
   explanation the book gives.
4. Use the tool to hold the point marked with the red arrow in figure 4.3.3, and release the belt with the
   **13 mm T-socket** ("13th T-sleeve") to take the belt off the motor.
5. Remove the **four M10 caps** with the **17 mm T-socket** ("17th T-Shirt") and take the motor out.
6. Fit the new motor by step 5 in reverse. **The motor needs to push forward, the belt hook back.**
7. As in step 4, use the 13 mm T-socket to adjust the belt tension to **120 ~ 130 Hz**.
8. Reconnect the motor ground wire and the motor wires to the control panel.

The tension figure in hertz implies a sonic (frequency) tension meter; the book does not name one. No torque
is printed for the M10 caps, and the motor wires are not colour-coded in the text.

The guide is a machine translation. Its words, as used on this card: **electronic watch** is the console,
**cross screwdriver** is a Phillips screwdriver, **umbrella head screws** are the guide's name for the
console and cover screws (no size is printed), and a **17th T-Shirt** or **13th T-sleeve** is a 17 mm or 13 mm
T-handle socket.
