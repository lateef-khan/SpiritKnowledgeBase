**Not one of these thirty-nine manuals prints a levelling instruction.** There is no
leveller, no adjustable foot, no wrench size and no levelling step in any assembly
chapter, any maintenance chapter or any parts list.

What every one of them does print, in the facility safety section, is the requirement:
**"Make sure that each machine is set up and operated on a solid level surface. Do not
install equipment on an uneven surface."** That is a placement requirement, not an
adjustment you make on the machine.

**So a Spirit strength machine that rocks is a floor problem, not a machine problem** -
except on the two ST800 units that can be bolted down, where the manual tells you to shim
rather than pull the frame down with the anchors. See
[the anchoring card](spirit-st800-assembly-anchoring-and-shimming-with-flat-washers.md).

## Do not carry the cardio range's answer across

Spirit's treadmills, ellipticals, bikes, rowers, steppers and climbers print *two levelling
pads on the bottom of the rear stabiliser, adjusted with a 14mm wrench*, and that card now
covers seventy-two machines. **None of them is a strength machine and none of these
thirty-nine prints that sentence.** See
[that card](../../ct800-2016/assembly/ct800-2016-assembly-levelling-pads.md).

## How this was checked

Searched all thirty-nine texts for *level*, *leveler*, *leveller*, *levelling*, *leveling*,
*adjustable feet*, *adjustable foot*, *foot pad*, *floor protector*, *stabilizer*,
*stabiliser* and *shim*. Four books returned a hit: three name a **foot pad** as a welded
frame component in a parts list, and one - the functional trainer - carries the ST800
shimming note. Every other occurrence of the word *level* in the corpus is either "a solid
level surface" or a resistance level on the i-Strength console.
