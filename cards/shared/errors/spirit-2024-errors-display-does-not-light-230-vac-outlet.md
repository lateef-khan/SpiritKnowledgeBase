Five checks, in the order the manual prints them.

1. Tether cord not in position.
2. Circuit breaker on front grill tripped. Push circuit breaker in until it locks.
3. Plug is disconnected. Make sure plug is firmly pushed into **230 VAC** wall outlet.
4. Breaker panel circuit breaker may be tripped.
5. Treadmill defect. Contact your Spirit Fitness dealer.

**Steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 are word for word what every other CT800 and CT850 generation prints. Step 3
is not.** These two 2024 books are the only Spirit treadmill manuals in the repository that name a
**230 VAC** outlet in this row. The figures printed by the others: **110 - 120 VAC** in the CT800
2020 and the CT850 2016 and 2020 owner's manuals
(`ct800-2020-errors-display-does-not-light`); **115 VAC** in the CT800 2012 and 2016, the
CT800ENT 2022, the CT850 2018, the CT850ENT 2022, the CT900ENT and all the XT manuals
(`ct900ent-display-does-not-light`); a plain **120 VAC** in both CT850 service manuals
(`ct850-2016-display-does-not-light`); and **120 VAC** in the CT800ENT 2024 and CT850ENT 2024
manuals of this same 2024 family (`spirit-2024-errors-display-does-not-light-120-vac-outlet`).

**The 230 VAC contradicts the same page of the same manual.** Four rows below, the row about a belt
capped near 7 mph tells the reader *A minimum of 120 volt AC current is required*
(`ct800-2020-errors-speed-caps-at-7-mph`). Nothing in either book reconciles the two, and 230 V is
not a North American domestic supply. **Do not send a customer to change their outlet on the
strength of this row** - check the machine's own rating plate first, and treat the 120 volt figure
printed elsewhere in the same table as the one to act on.
