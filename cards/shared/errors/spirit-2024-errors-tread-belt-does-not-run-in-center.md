**These two books split into two rows what every other Spirit treadmill manual prints as one**, and
they give the two halves different causes.

| Condition | Reason | Solve |
|---|---|---|
| Tread belt does not run in center. | Tread belt tension not even across tread belt. | See treadmill belt adjustment |
| Tread belt hesitates while being stepped on. | Insufficient lubricant on tread belt. Tread belt tension insufficient | See treadmill belt lubrication |

**The same two books also print the one-row version, on the facing page.** Their PROBLEM /
SOLUTION-CAUSE table carries `Running belt does not stay centered` with the usual walking-pattern
cause and the track-it-off-centre remedy, and `Treadmill belt hesitates when walked/run on` sending
the reader to General Maintenance on running belt tension. That version is on
`ct900ent-treadbelt-off-center-hesitates`, which now covers eighteen machines including the CT800
2024 and CT850 2024.

So a caller with a wandering belt on one of these two machines has **two printed answers in one
book**: a user-technique answer on one page and a belt-tension answer on the other. Neither manual
says which to try first. The tension check is the one that has a measurable outcome, so start there,
and read the walking-pattern row as the explanation for a belt that keeps drifting after the tension
is even.

**The two-row version is the service-manual version, not a 2024 invention.** Sixteen treadmill service manuals print both rows word for word in their Condition / Reason / Solve matrix - the 2015 and 2023 XT185 to XT685, the CT800 2016, CT800 2020, CT800ENT 2022, CT850ENT 2022, CT900ENT, CT1000ENT 2023 and 4.0T - and the CT850 2016 and 2020 service manuals print them as well (carded under maintenance for those two machines). The one-row walking-pattern version lives in the same books' service checklist, where they print one, so the two answers in one book described above hold for every machine here that has both tables.
