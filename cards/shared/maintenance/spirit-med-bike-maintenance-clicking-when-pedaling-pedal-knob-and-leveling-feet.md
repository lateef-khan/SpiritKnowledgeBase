**This is the owner's-manual answer, three checks in order.** The service manuals for the same machines answer a
"feeling of foot" while pedaling with two of them - lock the pedal, then check the crank
(`cu900ent-noise-and-feet-feeling`).

As printed:

- **Make sure the pedal is tightened properly.** It is common to have to **retighten the pedal after the first few
  hours use.**
- **Make sure the adjustment knob for the pedal slide is tightened as much as possible.**
- **Check that the leveling feet on the bottom of the bike are adjusted properly.**

## "Pedal slide" is a carry-over

Neither the 7.0R nor the 7.0U has a pedal slide: the phrase appears in this row and nowhere else in any of the
three books - not in the assembly steps, not in the parts list. The knob the sentence can mean on these bikes is
the **seat** adjustment knob - the 7.0U's seat-post and fore/aft knobs, the 7.0R's seat levers - which the same
troubleshooting table treats on its own row for the 7.0R
(`spirit-med-bike-maintenance-seat-will-not-slide-or-lock-thumb-nuts`). Tighten whatever adjustment knob the bike
has and say the manual's wording is borrowed.

## The other two are literal

The pedals tighten in opposite directions - right clockwise, left counter-clockwise - and the service manual's
preventative chapter is where that is printed
(`spirit-med-bike-maintenance-preventative-check-pedals-handlebar-and-seat-cushion`). The leveling feet are the
two pads under the rear stabilizer, turned with the 14 mm wrench of the care page - or the ½" wrench of the setup
page; the books print both (`cu900ent-general-maintenance`).

The "first few hours" sentence is the only interval any of these books gives for a pedal re-check; the 2018 CR900
recumbent's service manual says every week (`spirit-cr900-maintenance-weekly-pedal-check`), and answers a click
with a four-item list of its own (`spirit-cr900-maintenance-clicking-at-the-pedals`).
