The first line of the General Maintenance page in every one of these books, word for word:

**"Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout."**

That is the whole cleaning instruction. These six books name **no cleaner, no soap, no alcohol and no
product to avoid** - a damp cloth, after every session, on the places sweat lands. The recumbent and
folding-bike books of the other Xterra family add mild liquid soap and a warning to keep water off the
monitor (`xterra-maintenance-cleaning-with-a-damp-cloth-and-mild-liquid-soap-keeping-water-off-the-monitor`);
the new-layout SB240 and SB600 print the 75% isopropyl sanitizing page instead.

What the same page says to do about a thump or squeak depends on the machine: the three bikes blame
loose pedals and crank arms (`xterra-sb-maintenance-thump-clicking-or-wobble-from-loose-pedals-or-crank-arms`);
the two ellipticals and the seated stepper blame assembly hardware, dirty rails and leveling
(`xterra-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking-from-loose-hardware-dirty-rails-or-leveling`).

The SB4.5r, FS5.9e and the SB500 2014 and RSX1500 2017 printings are scans; the sentence was read from
the 300 dpi render and matches the native text of the later printings exactly.

Same sentence, other brands: Spirit's commercial bikes (`cu900ent-general-maintenance`) and Sole's
LCR (`lcr-2026-wipe-down-after-each-workout`). Those are Dyaco boilerplate twins, not evidence for an
Xterra machine.
