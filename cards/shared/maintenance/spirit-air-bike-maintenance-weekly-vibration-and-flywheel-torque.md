Weekly maintenance focuses on the **overall performance** of the bike. During these inspections
look for **vibration** and possible **loose assemblies**.

Have an **experienced rider ride each bike** to identify and help diagnose any vibration, noises
and any "unusual" feeling from the **drive belt**.

**Either faulty flywheel alignment or a loose belt can cause vibration.** Check for proper flywheel
alignment. **Torque flywheel nuts as necessary.**

Inspect each bike for loose assemblies, parts, bolts and nuts, giving particular attention to:

- Tighten all frame base hardware.
- Tighten all pop-pin handles.
- Tighten seat hardware.

The manual gives **no torque figure for the flywheel nuts** and no belt tension figure. The only
pedal torque it prints is the AB950's 33-37 lb-ft, which is a pedal-to-crank-arm figure and not a
flywheel figure — `ab950-2024-assembly-pedal-torque-33-to-37-lb-ft`.

## The JB950 sends this job to a technician

The Johnny G Spirit Bike prints the same paragraph up to the flywheel and then **changes the
instruction**: "If flywheel requires adjustment refer maintenance to a properly trained service
tech." It also adds a fourth bullet for the toe clips. See
`jb950-2022-maintenance-weekly-check-toe-clips-and-service-tech`. **Do not tell a JB950 owner to
torque the flywheel nuts**, and do not tell an air bike owner to call a technician for it.
