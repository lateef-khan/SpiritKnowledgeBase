**Four rows of these machines' schedules say *Clean*. Not one of the thirty-six manuals says what
to clean with.**

The rows are *Clean: Upholstery*, *Clean: Handgrips*, *Clean: Main Frame* and *Clean: Guide Rod*
(plus *Clean: Internal Tower* on twelve of them). Each has an interval and a tick. There is no
cloth, no soap, no vinyl cleaner, no solvent warning and no "avoid abrasives" line anywhere in the
chapter or the book.

**There is also no sanitizing or disinfection page**, and **no storage section** — nothing about
covering, moving or laying up a machine that is out of service.

## This is an absence in these books, not an absence in Spirit's guidance

Spirit's company-wide sanitizing instruction exists and reaches every machine:
**a 75% isopropyl alcohol solution and a clean, dry cloth on hard plastics, and a conditioner after
sanitizing on upholstered or soft-plastic surfaces** — `ct900-sanitizing-equipment`. Quote it as
Spirit's guidance. **Do not tell a customer their own manual says it**, because these thirty-six
books do not print it.

**The three ST800 machines in the same range do print it**, on their own page, word for word —
which is how the silence in these thirty-six was confirmed rather than assumed.

## The upholstery question in particular

*Clean: Upholstery, daily* and *Inspect: Upholstery, monthly* are the two rows most gyms act on,
and they are the two with the least behind them. The books give **no cleaner, no cleaning method,
no conditioner, and no replacement criterion** for a pad.

The **ST800FT** manual, on the other template, does give all of that — damp cloth after every
workout, a mild soap or an approved vinyl upholstery cleaner periodically, no abrasive cleaners, and
replace ripped upholstery immediately:
`spirit-strength-st800ft-maintenance-upholstery-decals-nuts-and-anti-skid-care`. **That is one
machine's page and it is not printed in the other thirty-six books.** It is a reasonable thing to
tell a customer; say where it comes from.

## Do not fill the gap from a cardio card

Spirit's treadmill, elliptical and bike maintenance cards carry wipe-down routines, levelling checks
and lubrication steps written for powered machines with belts, decks and crank arms. A selectorized
weight stack has none of those. `ct800-2016-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking` and
`cu900ent-general-maintenance` are the two most widely shared Spirit cardio maintenance cards, and
**neither applies to these machines**: their remedies are the crank arm nut, two rear-stabiliser
levelling pads and a 14 mm wrench, none of which is a part on a Spirit strength machine, and neither
sentence appears in any of these thirty-six books.
