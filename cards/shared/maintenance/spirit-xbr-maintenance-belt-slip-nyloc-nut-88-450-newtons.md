**This is the 450 N setting at nyloc nut 88.** The same nut on the 2009 CR800 and the 2016 XBR95 is
read with an acoustic gauge to 180 ±10 Hz instead
(`spirit-recumbent-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-180-hz`), and the XBU55 2016 upright turns a
different nut with an 11 mm wrench (`xbu55-2016-maintenance-belt-slip-nut-130-450-newtons`).

Q: Slippage — as printed in all five books:

1. Disassemble the front shrouds.
2. Use a **13 mm wrench** to turn the **M8 × 7T nyloc nut (88)** clockwise 'until sound wave
   frequency falls between **450N**'. Since the machine is belt driven, slippage is possible
   depending on the weight of the user or the way the user uses it; generally slippage is rare.
3. Since the drive belt (54) stretches and wears, it is normal for it to get loose as time passes.

## The sentence mixes a frequency with a force

'Sound wave frequency falls between 450N' names a frequency reading and a newton figure in one
breath, and the range the sister CR800 books print ('180~205HZ') is missing. Read it as the
belt-tension-gauge figure the same books give in their flywheel and belt replacement step: **450 N
on a belt tension gauge for a belt in service, 540 N for a new belt**, because the belt has
ductility and its tension falls after a period of use. The XBR25 2016, XBR55 2016, XBR55ENT and
XBR55 2023 all print those two numbers in the replacement procedure, for the same M8 × 7T nyloc
nut (88) on the J-bolt (87), tightened with the same 13 mm wrench until the idler wheel (10) presses
the belt tightly.

**The XBR95 2023 contradicts itself.** Its Q&A prints the 450 N sentence above, but its own
replacement step 9.5 reads the same nut with an **acoustic tension gauge to 180 ±10 Hz** and then
says a new belt needs 540 N. The XBR95 is the generator-brake recumbent whose idler wheel sits on
the induction brake (55); its 2016 book gives 180 ±10 Hz in the Q&A as well. For an XBR95 2023 the
two figures are printed nine pages apart and the book does not say which to use; this card records
both.

## What the rest of the page says

The Q&A goes on to 'Drive Belt falling off' — turning the belt back and forth to see whether it
falls off, moving it one notch against the side it falls off, and replacing the drive pulley (20),
the idler wheel assembly (10) or, on the 2016 books and the XBR55ENT, the induction brake (55) if
it keeps falling off. That is a diagnosis, not an adjustment, and is carded with the
troubleshooting Q&A.

The residential XBR owner's manuals print no belt work at all
(`spirit-bike-maintenance-no-lubricant-belt-or-battery-service`); this is the service-manual
procedure. The Sole R92 2016 and 2023 and LCR 2023 books print the identical sentence
(`r92-2016-belt-slips-or-falls-off`).
