Belt tension is **not critical for most users**; it matters for joggers and runners, to give a smooth, steady running surface.

Adjustment is made **from the rear roller with the 6 mm Allen wrench** provided in the parts package. The adjustment bolts are at the **end of the step rails**.

1. **Tighten the rear roller only enough to prevent slippage at the front roller.**
2. Turn the tread-belt tension adjusting bolts **1/4 turn each** (the TR150, TR200, TRX1000 and TR260 books say "clockwise a 1/4 turn each").
3. Inspect for proper tension by walking on the belt and making sure it is **not slipping or hesitating with each step**.
4. **Turn the bolts on both sides evenly**, or the belt could start tracking to one side instead of running in the middle of the deck.

**DO NOT OVERTIGHTEN** - over tightening will cause belt damage and premature bearing failure. **If you tighten the belt a lot and it still slips, the problem could actually be the drive belt** under the motor cover that connects the motor to the front roller; a loose drive belt feels like a loose walking belt. Tightening the motor belt should be done by a trained service person.

The Dyaco service manuals for the TRX1400, TRX2500, TRX3500 and TRX4500 add that the bolts sit **in the end caps** and "adjustment is through a small hole in the end cap"; they say to turn "both bolts in increments of 1/4 turn each", walk at a low speed, and keep tensioning until the belt stops slipping.

The TRX1400 book also carries an unprinted layer of text about an "L Allen Wrench (132)" and "only a few droplets" of lubricant; the printed page was read from the render and says what is above. The TR65, TR75, TR85 (`xterra-tr-maintenance-belt-tension-rear-roller-screws-m6-l-wrench-103`), WS200/WS300 (`xterra-ws-maintenance-belt-tension-rear-roller-screws-5-mm-allen-wrench`) and TR95H/TR75H (`xterra-tr-maintenance-belt-tension-at-2-mph-quarter-turn-both-screws`) books print different procedures.
