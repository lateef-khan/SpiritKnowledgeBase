**This is the clinical wording of the Spirit bike care page: the wipe-down is "after each use" and has a reason,
the pedals get a monthly interval, and the leveler wrench is a ½" one.** The commercial bikes print the same
three figures - 90%, 2 pads, 14 mm - with no pedal interval and no rust (`cu900ent-general-maintenance`).

As printed on the 2025 4.0R and 4.0U:

1. **Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each use to prevent rust.**
2. **Check the pedal to make sure they are tight (monthly).**
3. If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops the main cause is most likely one of two reasons:
   - **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts that were installed during assembly
     need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger wrench than the one provided if
     you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. "I cannot stress this point enough; **90% of calls to the service
     department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware.**"
   - **The crank arm nut and/or the pedals need to be retightened.**
4. If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly leveled. There are **2 leveling pads on the
   bottom of the rear stabilizer**; use a **½" (14mm) wrench** (or adjustable wrench) to adjust the levelers.

## The wrench is printed two ways, and the two are not the same size

The 2025 4.0R and 4.0U books and both Dyaco PT editions write **"½” (14mm)"** as if the two were equal. They are
not: a ½" spanner is 12.7 mm. The MED 7.0R 2021 book prints **"½” wrench"** alone, exactly as the MS300 stepper
does (`ms300-2021-maintenance-rust-pedals-and-half-inch-wrench`). Every one of these books also levels the bike in
its setup chapter with a **½" wrench** on the top nut of the leveler and then "by hand". Take a wrench of each
size; the page does not settle which fits.

**The 4.0R and 4.0U service manuals disagree with their own owner's manuals.** Their GENERAL MAINTENANCE page is
the commercial block - "after each workout", no monthly pedal check, no rust, and a plain **14mm wrench** - and
it is carded with the commercial bikes at `cu900ent-general-maintenance`. Quote the owner's manual to an owner
and the service manual to a technician, and say the two differ.

## What the page does not say

- **No interval for the wipe-down beyond "after each use"**, and no cleaner is named for it. The 2025 books print
  the standard 75% isopropyl sanitizing bullets beside this list (`ct900-sanitizing-equipment`) and the four-step
  screen-and-cupholder routine (`spirit-2026t-maintenance-post-workout-machine-care`); **the two PT editions and
  the MED 7.0R 2021 print neither** - `spirit-med-bike-maintenance-dyaco-editions-print-no-sanitizing-or-disinfection`.
- **No lubricant, no belt adjustment and no schedule table** on any of the five. The belt figure for the 4.0R and
  4.0U lives in their service manuals (`spirit-recumbent-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-180-hz`).
- **The crank arm nut is literal here.** These are bikes; the nut is the one under the crank arm end cap.

## Which books, exactly

The Spirit-branded **4.0R** and **4.0U** of October 2025 and the undated Dyaco-branded **PT 4.0 R** and **PT 4.0 U**
print this list word for word, "½” (14mm)" included. The Dyaco **MED 7.0R** rehabilitation bike of October 2021
prints the same list with "the 7.0R" for "the unit" and the wrench as **½"** only. The 2025 Spirit 7.0R and 7.0U
do **not** print this page; they carry the commercial block (`cu900ent-general-maintenance`).
