**The trigger is the condition of the part, not its age.** Printed under *Inspection* on the
SAFEGUARDS page, word for word:

> **Cables and belts pose an extreme liability if used when frayed. Always replace any cable or
> belt at first sign of wear (consult SPIRIT FITNESS if uncertain).**

The same page adds:

> DO NOT use or permit use of any equipment that is damaged and/or has worn or broken parts. For
> all SPIRIT FITNESS equipment **use only replacement parts supplied by SPIRIT FITNESS**.
>
> Replace immediately any defective parts and **do not operate unit until all repairs are
> complete**.

and, on the facility precautions page:

> Immediately replace worn or damaged components. **If unable to immediately replace worn or
> damaged components then remove from service until the repair is made.**

## There is no service life to quote

**No Spirit strength owner's manual on this card gives a cable or belt a replacement interval** —
not in months, not in years, not in operating hours, not in repetitions. Searched across all
thirty-nine manuals in the range. The books answer the question with a condition and a first-sign
rule, and nothing else.

**Do not invent one, and do not carry one across from another product line.** A treadmill's belt
figures and a rower's hundred-hour check are different machines.

## The one Spirit strength machine that does print an interval

The **ST800FT functional trainer** is on a different template and its schedule has a
*Replace: Cables, Belts and Connecting Parts* row — **Yearly** in a facility, **3 Years** at home:
`spirit-strength-st800ft-maintenance-cables-inspected-daily-and-replaced-yearly`. That figure is
that machine's, and it does not extend to the thirty-five here.

## Four books in the range do not print this sentence

- **CSF-FUNT** has no SAFEGUARDS page; its safety chapter is a different one entirely. The
  replacement rule is simply not in that book, though its facility precautions page still carries
  *immediately replace worn or damaged components*.
- The **three ST800 books** use the other template and print neither this sentence nor the
  "extreme liability" wording.

## The bullet the CSI books drop

Thirty-three of these thirty-five also print *"Check all belts, pulleys and bungee cords regularly
for signs of wear, and replace if needed."* The **CSI-CPSP and CSI-LROW do not** — they keep the
"extreme liability" sentence and drop this one. Nothing else on their SAFEGUARDS inspection block
differs.

## The whole page is carded separately, and its count is 33 rather than 35

`spirit-strength-safety-safeguards-page-usage-and-inspection` quotes the SAFEGUARDS page as a whole
and scopes it to **33 machines**, excluding the two CSI books because their SAFEGUARDS page is
rewritten - it adds a pacemaker warning and an electrical block and drops the bungee-cord bullet.
**This card is scoped to the sentence**, which the CSI books do print word for word, so it names
**35**. Both counts are right for what they cover. Send a *what does the safeguards page say*
question there, and a *when do I replace this cable* question here.

## Say who is allowed to do the work

The same page: *"Ensure that any person(s) making adjustments or performing maintenance or repair
of any kind is qualified to do so."* A frayed cable on a commercial machine is a service call, not
a floor-staff job.
