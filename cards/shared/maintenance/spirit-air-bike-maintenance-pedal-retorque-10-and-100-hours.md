**Inspect major moving parts that require constant proper torque.** Loose or mis-adjusted parts can
result in personal injury or damage to the bike.

**Pedals:** verify that the pedals are re-torqued **after the first 10 hours of use and every 100
hours of operation thereafter**. Use a **torque wrench** if components are loose.

**IMPORTANT: it is not recommended that members change out the pedals on the bike.**

This is a monthly-inspection item on both machines, so in practice the hour figures are the trigger
and the monthly walk-round is when you notice.

## Only the AB950 prints a torque figure

The AB950 2024 adds one line the JB950 does not: **the pedal to crank arm bolt torque is 33-37
lb-ft**, carded on its own as `ab950-2024-assembly-pedal-torque-33-to-37-lb-ft`, which also
compares it with the 300 in-lb the commercial Spirit bikes use. **The JB950 2022 prints no torque
figure anywhere**, checked in the extraction and in a 300 dpi render of printed p. 68. Do not quote
33-37 lb-ft to a JB950 owner; the manual tells them to use a torque wrench without saying what to
set it to. **This card is the one that answers the question for a JB950**; the figure card covers
the AB950 only.

## The AB900 prints no interval at all

The AB900 2018's monthly page (printed pp. 43-44) covers rust, corrosion and wear items but
**never mentions re-torquing the pedals, the 10-hour or 100-hour figures, or the members warning**.
Its only pedal instruction is to inspect them for play —
`spirit-air-bike-maintenance-monthly-rust-and-wear-inspection`.

## The indoor cycles say the opposite kind of thing

The CB900, CIC800, CIC850 and XIC600 ask an owner to check the pedals are tight in the crank arms
**before each use**, by hand, with no wrench and no interval, and forbid servicing the pedal's
internals — `spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-pedal-internals-and-approved-cleaners`.
