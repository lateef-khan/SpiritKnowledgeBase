Either the machine thinks a button is being pressed, or the motor controller is acting on its own.

1. Ask whether the machine was recently assembled, taken apart or moved.
   - **Yes**: replace the computer cables. **If the machine is older than 2020**, replace the computer cables, the upper incline cable and the incline adjustment switch.
   - **No**: go to step 2.
2. Ask whether the incline number on the console changes when the incline physically changes.
   - **Yes**: something in the console is commanding the change. Replace the console assembly. **If the machine is older than 2020**, first disconnect the incline adjustment switch and run the machine again. If it now behaves, replace the incline adjustment switch. If not, replace the console assembly.
   - **No**: replace the motor controller.

If you are replacing the motor controller, also check for the wear that killed it:

1. Ask how often the customer lubricates. Either way, check the running belt and running deck for wear. Slide a hand between belt and deck palm up and feel whether the texture in the middle is gone. Turn the palm down and feel the deck for damage.
   - Good: go on.
   - Worn or damaged: replace the running belt, and the running deck only if it is damaged.
2. Ask about squeaking or knocking noises. If there are any, ask whether they come from the front or the rear, and replace the front roller or the rear roller, possibly both.
