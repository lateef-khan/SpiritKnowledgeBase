1. Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout.
2. If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops the main cause is most likely one of two reasons:
   - **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts that were installed during assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger wrench than the one provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. The manual says: *"I cannot stress this point enough; 90% of calls to the service department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware or the rear rails being dirty."*
   - **Dirt build-up on the rear rails and polyurethane wheels** is also a source of noise. Build-up on the rails can cause a thumping sound that you would swear is coming from inside the main body of the machine, because noise travels and is amplified in the tubing of the frame. Clean the rails and wheels with a **lint free cloth and rubbing alcohol**. Stubborn build-up can be removed with your thumbnail or a non-metallic scraper, like the back edge of a plastic knife. After cleaning, apply a **small amount of lubricant** on the rails with your fingers or a lint free cloth. You only need a **thin coat**; wipe off any excess.
3. If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly levelled **before calling the service department**.

**These manuals give no levelling pad count and no wrench size, and they do not mention the crank arm nut.** The B94/R92, B74/R72 and 2013 LCB/LCR manuals print a different version of this same page that names the crank arm nut, 2 levelling pads on the rear stabilizer and a 14mm wrench. Do not carry those figures onto the machines listed here.

A clicking or thumping felt **specifically while pedalling** is dealt with in the assembly instructions of these manuals, not here: they say to re-tighten the pedals.
