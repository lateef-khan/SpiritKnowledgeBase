**This is the 180 ±10 Hz acoustic-gauge setting at nyloc nut 88.** The XBR 2016 and 2023 books
turn the same nut to 450 N on a belt tension gauge
(`spirit-xbr-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-450-newtons`), the CR800 2021 to 180–205 Hz
(`cr800-2021-maintenance-belt-slip-180-to-205-hz`) and the CU800 2012 to 170 ±10 Hz
(`cu800-2012-maintenance-belt-slip-170-hz`). Read the figure from the book for the machine in
front of you.

Q: Slippage — as printed:

1. Disassemble the front shrouds (29, 30).
2. Use a **13 mm open end wrench** to turn the **M8 × 7T nyloc nut (88) clockwise** to tighten
   until the **sound wave tension gauge reads 180 ±10 Hz** (figure 1).
3. Since the drive belt (54) stretches and wears, it is normal for it to get loose as time passes.

The nut sits on the J-bolt (87); tightening it pulls the idler wheel assembly (10) against the
belt. The install step says the same thing: turn the nut on the J-bolt until the idler wheel
applies sufficient tension, check 180 ±10 Hz with an acoustic tension gauge, then turn the drive
pulley (20) to make sure the belt sits in the centre and does not fall off.

**The XBR95 2016 adds a new-belt figure the 2009 CR800 does not print.** Remark 1 under its
replacement step says a new drive belt is adjusted to **540 N** because the belt has ductility and
its tension falls after a period of use. The 2009 CR800 book (titled XR898 Service Manual) gives
180 ±10 Hz and nothing else. The XBR95 2023 book keeps 180 ±10 Hz in its replacement step but
prints 450 N in its Q&A — see the XBR card.

The 'Drive Belt falling off' answer on the same page — turn the belt back and forth, move it one
notch against the side it falls off, replace the drive pulley (20), the idler wheel assembly (10)
or the induction brake (55), and finally the whole unit if the frame is deformed — is a diagnosis
and belongs with the Q&A cards.
