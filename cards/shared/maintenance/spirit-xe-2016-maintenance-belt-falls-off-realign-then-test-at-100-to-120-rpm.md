**The test speed here is 100 to 120 RPM. The XE395 and XE395ENT books print 100 to 200 RPM and the
XE795 books 80 to 100 RPM for the same step; the CE800 (2020) book prints no RPM at all.**

1. **Remove the right chain cover and the Idler Wheel Plate**, then reinstall the Poly-V belt.
2. **Rotate at low speed** and watch whether the drive pulley or the belt is offset or deformed.
   Check whether the belt pulley is offset, or whether the **belt, drive pulley and magnetic flywheel
   are not aligned in a straight line**.
3. **Rotate at high speed - **100 to 120 RPM** - and test with abrupt stops** to check for abnormalities. If
   everything is fine, reassemble fully.
4. If the three parts are not in a straight line, **adjust the magnetic flywheel** to the proper
   place. The book adds that alignment is always set before the machine leaves the factory.
5. A worn or damaged belt is replaced, not adjusted.

Slipping is the other belt symptom and has its own adjustment, the hook-screw cap with a 13 mm
wrench to 190 Hz - `spirit-xe-2016-maintenance-belt-slipping-hook-screw-cap-13-mm-190-hz`.

## The CE800 (2016) wording

The XE890B book says the same thing in its own words: release the right chain cover and loosen the
Idler Wheel Assembly, return the belt, "turn the Belt slowly and watch for the shifting between the
Belt and Drive Pulley and the alignment of the Belt, Drive Pulley and Inductive Flywheel. Check
again at speed as high as **100 to 120 RPM**." Alignment is corrected by adjusting the flywheel; a
worn belt is replaced. No figure differs; the abrupt-stop test is not mentioned in this older wording.

The Sole E25 (2019) service manual carries this procedure with a **100-200 RPM** test
(`e25-2019-poly-v-belt-falls-off`); the Spirit CVC800 climber has its own version
(`cvc800-drive-belt-drops-off`).
