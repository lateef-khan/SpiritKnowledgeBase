**Check loose hardware first.** The owners books say 90% of noise calls trace to loose assembly hardware or dirty rear rails (`sole-elliptical-maintenance-noise-two-causes-90-percent`). What follows is the service note's part-by-location tree once hardware is tight.

1. **Squeak or grind from the rail area.** Ask whether the rails have been lubricated.
   - **No** - have the customer lubricate. If the noise stops, done.
   - **Yes** - check the rails and slide wheels for damage. Damaged, replace the damaged parts. No damage, spin the slide wheels by hand: noise while spinning, replace the **Slide Wheels**; silent, lubricate the rails again.
2. **Pop or creak from the pedals.** Ask whether the customer's foot is positioned properly and the heel is not lifting. Wrong use, coach them and try again - still noisy, replace the **Pedal** and the **Foam Tape**. Right use, ask which side and replace the **Pedal** and the **Foam Tape** on that side.
3. **Loud knock.** Ask whether the foot is positioned properly, the heel is down, and how fast they are going. Wrong use, coach them and try again - still noisy, ask which side. Right use, ask which side it comes from: outside the flywheel area, replace the **rod end bearings** and **rod end sleeves**, possibly the **crossbar and pedal-arm bushings**; inside the machine, replace the **Bearing** and the **Bearing/Axle Stopper**.
4. **Rub from the front.** Have the customer send a video of them using the machine showing both sides. If the spinning **Round Disk Cover** wobbles or sits too close to the **Side Shroud Cover** on one side, replace the **Crossbar**.
