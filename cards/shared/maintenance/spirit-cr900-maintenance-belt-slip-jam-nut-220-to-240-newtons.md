**This is the spring-loaded idler with a top nut and a bottom jam nut, set to 220–240 N.** The
upright CU900 and CU900ENT tension their belt at a nut and hook screw to 180–210 N
(`cu900ent-belt-slips-or-falls-off`), and the older recumbents read a nut on the J-bolt with an
acoustic gauge (`spirit-recumbent-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-180-hz`). Do not carry any of
those figures here.

Section 7.1, Belt Slips While Pedaling Under High Load — as printed:

1. Refer to 6-5 (front shroud removal), 6-6-1 (release tension on the idler) and 6-6-2 (remove
   belt) to gain access to the belt adjustment.
2. **Loosen the bottom jam nut and back it off a few turns on the J-bolt.**
3. **Tighten the top nut to increase tension on the spring until the slipping stops.**
4. The belt tension should be set to **220 ~ 240 Newtons (50–55 lbs)**.
5. When complete, **re-tighten the jam nut.**

The same number is the install setting: section 6.6.2 says that when re-installing the Poly-V
drive belt, make sure the belt tension is set to 220–240 N (50–55 lbs). Section 6.6.1 describes
the release — loosen the adjustment nut on the J-bolt until the tension spring can be removed —
and 6.6.3 wants the idler bracket rotated fully clockwise, screws at the end of travel in their
slots, when it goes back. Those are replacement steps; the figure is the same.

**The CR1000ENT 2023 prints the pointer and the figure, not the five steps.** Its 13.1 reads only "If you find
the belt is slipping please refer to 12.15, 12.31, 12.32 to gain access to the belt adjustment" — 12.15 is the
front shroud removal and figures 12.31-12.32 are the J-bolt adjustment nut and tension spring of its 12.16 — and
its 12.16 sets the same **220 ~ 240 Newtons (50-55 lbs)** on re-installing the belt, with the idler bracket
rotated fully clockwise (12.35-12.36). Steps 2-5 above are not printed in that book; the mechanism and the
figure are.

None of the three books names the gauge. The CR800, CU800 and XBR books read their belts with a sonic
(acoustic) gauge in hertz; this pair gives a force in newtons and pounds. Both books print this
paragraph word for word; the CR900ENT differs from the CR900 only in its page folios.
