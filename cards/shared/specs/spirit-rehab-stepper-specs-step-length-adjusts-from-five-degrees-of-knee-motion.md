Three capabilities, printed the same way in all five books:

- **Self adjustable step length** - the patient can pedal *"in smaller range of knee motion, **from
  5 degrees to full range**"*. There is no gear, lever or setting for this; the linkage simply
  allows a short stroke.
- **The arms are linked to the foot pedals** for ease of coordination, and **can also be used alone
  without stepping with the feet**.
- **The Symmetry program measures the balance between left and right pedal and/or arm stroke**, and
  the graphical biofeedback display is there to make the patient even it out.

**Five degrees of knee motion is the smallest figure any Spirit manual gives for a range of
movement.** It is what makes this an early-post-operative machine rather than an exercise bike.

## Using the arms alone reverses the graph

*"If measuring arms only the graph and power readings will be reversed."* When pushing the handles,
the left side of the display shows the right arm; **it reads correctly when the patient is
pulling**. The Symmetry program page repeats this -
`spirit-rehab-stepper-programs-symmetry-measures-left-and-right-leg-power`.

## What the manual does not give

- **No stride length in inches or centimetres**, and no maximum. "Full range" is not a number.
- **No degrees for hip or ankle**, only the knee.
- **No pedal or arm force figure**, and no way to disconnect the arms from the pedals mechanically -
  using them alone means not stepping, not decoupling them.

**This is open kinetic chain exercise only** and the patient is not restrained or connected to the
machine - `spirit-rehab-stepper-specs-application-specification-medical-purpose-and-patient-population`.
