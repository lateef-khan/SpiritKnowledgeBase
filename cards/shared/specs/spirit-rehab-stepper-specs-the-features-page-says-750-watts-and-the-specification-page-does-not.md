**Every one of these five books prints two different maxima, twenty to fifty pages apart, and
neither page mentions the other.**

| Book | Features page | Specification page |
|---|---|---|
| MED 7.0S, MED 7.5S, MS300 | **"Work range up to 750 watts."** | **5 watts up to 650 watts** |
| 2025 7.0S, 2025 7.5S | **"Work range up to 750 Watts."** | **5 watts up to 1000 watts** |

**The features sentence is identical in all five books** - it was not updated when the
specification page changed from 650 to 1000. On the 2021 machines it is a hundred watts *above*
the specified ceiling; on the 2025 machines it is two hundred and fifty *below* it.

## Which to quote

**Quote the specification page**, and say the features page disagrees. The specification page is
the one that carries the units, the level count and the power supply, and it is the page that
differs between the two generations - which is what makes it the one that was maintained.

- On an MS300, a MED 7.0S or a MED 7.5S: **5 to 650 watts** (`ms300-2021-specs-workload-5-to-650-watts`).
- On a 2025 7.0S or 7.5S: **5 to 1000 watts** (`spirit-climber-specs-workload-5-to-1000-watts`).

**Do not average them and do not promise 750.** A clinic sizing a protocol to 700 watts on a 2021
machine would be over its stated ceiling.

## No level-to-watts table exists on any of them

The resistance is **isokinetic with 20 levels of effort**
(`spirit-climber-specs-isokinetic-twenty-levels`), and no book maps a level to a wattage. The watts
shown during a workout are calculated from a power table and velocity, not measured -
`spirit-rehab-stepper-programs-symmetry-measures-left-and-right-leg-power`.

**The 8.5S prints no 750-watt sentence.** Its features page has no work range line at all.
