**The fan is the resistance.** Neither book has a resistance knob, a level, a brake pad or a magnetic brake: the
harder you pedal, the harder the fan pushes back, and the only instruction is to "gradually increase the pedaling
speed and/or resistance" as you warm up. There is nothing to stop the fan with - both books say "Wait for flywheel
to coast to a stop before dismounting the bike" and never to touch a moving part.

The AB900 service manual, which the manufacturer's note assigns to the AIR650, says the same from the technician's
side: the electrical configuration is one sentence ("Console: Interface that controls all functions of the Air
Bike. Contains Key controls and LCD Display"), with no controller, no motor and no brake circuit, because there is
nothing but the fan to load.

The two drives differ. The **AIR650** turns the fan through **two chains** and a "Zhonghua Drum Group": the chainring
on the crank drives the drum through Chain Joint-68, and the drum drives the fan through Chain Joint-100, so the Spirit book's chain-slack figures and
chain oiling apply to it; the maintenance section holds those. The **AIR350** turns a "Belt pulley with Crank"
(part 18) - a belt. Neither book prints the fan diameter or a flywheel weight.
