**180 ±10 Hz** on an **acoustic tension gauge**, after the J-bolt's M8 x 7T nut has been turned
with a 13 mm wrench until the idler wheel loads the belt, and before the drive pulley is turned
to check the belt runs centred. The XR898 book and both XBR95 books print the same step.

**What each book adds:**

- **CR800-2009 (XR898)** repeats 180 ±10 Hz in its slippage Q&A. Clean.
- **XBR95-2016** repeats 180 ±10 Hz in its Q&A, and adds "Remark 1: the new drive belt need adjust
  to **540N**".
- **XBR95-2023** keeps the 540 N note and its Q&A says "until sound wave frequency falls between
  **450N**".

**The 540 N and 450 N lines are the gear-motor recumbents' figures**
(`spirit-xbr-specs-drive-belt-tension-450-newtons-540-when-new`), pasted into the induction-brake
books where the replacement step is in hertz. A sound-wave gauge does not read newtons. Set these
three machines to 180 ±10 Hz; the 2012 CU800 on the same 031101B controller is set to 170 ±10 Hz.

**The 2025 medical 4.0R and 4.0U print the same step and the same figure.** The 4.0R book: turn the
M8 x 7T nut on the J-bolt with a 13 mm wrench "until Idler Wheel applies sufficient tension on Belt.
Use Acoustic tension gauge to make sure the reading is in the range 180±10HZ"; the 4.0U book: "Use
belt tension gauge to make sure the frequency within the range of 180± 10HZ", after the idler arm is
refitted to the generator flywheel with three M6 x 15 screws. Neither prints the 540 N new-belt note
or a slippage Q&A figure (the 4.0U Q&A says only to tighten the M8 x 7T nut). The 7.0R and 7.0U
medical bikes on a different frame are set in newtons instead
(`spirit-cr900-specs-drive-belt-tension-220-240-newtons`, `70u-2025-specs-drive-belt-tension-260-to-280-newtons`).
