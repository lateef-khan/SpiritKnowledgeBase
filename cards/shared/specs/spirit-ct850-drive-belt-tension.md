**70 to 75 lbs, read in the white range of a belt tension gauge.**

This is the belt between the drive motor and the front roller, not the tread belt.

Adjust it with a **14 mm open-end wrench** on the tension screw, then lock it by tightening the
**four motor adjusting bolts** - with an **M8 L-shape Allen wrench** on the CT and 40T books, with a
**14 mm T-shaped socket wrench** on the XT books. Reconnect the motor grounding wire and the motor
cables afterwards (U / V / W on the inverter machines, red M+ and black M- on the DC machines).

**Seventeen service manuals print the same figure in the same motor-replacement step**: the CT850
2016 and 2020, the CT800 2020, both ENT CT books, the 4.0T, every XT 2015 and 2023 book and both
XT ENT books. The 2023 XT books word it "measure belt tension with a tension meter, the tension
needs to be about 70~75LBS"; the others say "white LBS area between 70-75LBS" or "white range
between 70~75 Lbs". The XT485ENT book alone mentions a second screw - "loosen a side tension
screw as well as a rear drive belt tension screw" - but sets the same 70-75 lbs.

**Two books contradict themselves.** The CT800ENT and CT850ENT print, in their Q&A chapter under
"Belt slipping", an answer copied from a bike manual: remove the "Left and Right Shrouds" and
"check the tension of drive belt with a sonic belt tension meter, the value must be 250±50HZ".
That figure belongs to a crank-and-pedal machine and is not the treadmill drive belt spec; the
motor-replacement step in the same two books says 70-75 lbs.

**Where it does not hold.** No owner's manual prints it. The CT800-2016 service manual prints no
belt-tension figure at all, and the 2007 XT dealer manual says only "about an inch of play". The
CT900 and CT900ENT use a sonic-meter frequency of 120-130 Hz, the CT1000ENT prints "80-90BLS", the
CTSBS900 sets a 260-265 mm belt sag, and no source states any belt tension for the `ct850-2018`,
which has no service manual. There is no tread-belt tension figure in any of these books.

This is a **force in pounds on a mechanical gauge**. The other Spirit commercial machines use
different units for their drive belts - the CVC800 and CE800ENT a sonic-meter frequency in hertz,
the CU900ENT newtons.
