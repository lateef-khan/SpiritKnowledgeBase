**A friction pad on the flywheel, with a continuous adjuster and no numbered levels.** The MB500 and MB550 use a
knob: "Pedaling resistance is controlled by the tension knob ... clock-wise for more resistance; counterclockwise for
less resistance. To apply the brake, press down on the tension knob." The MBX2500 uses a lever: "Push the tension
lever (63) up or down ... down for more resistance; up for less resistance. To apply the brake, press down on the
tension lever to the maximum resistance setting."

The pad is named in the maintenance schedule as the **Brake Pad** - on the MB550 and MBX2500 "a dry or squealing
leather brake pad", inspected weekly and oiled with 3-IN-ONE or 10W oil, never a silicone lubricant; the MB500 row
says only "inspect for excessive wear or squealing". The maintenance section holds that schedule.

The drive is fixed-gear: "The bike is NOT equipped with a freewheel system which means that when the flywheel is in
motion, the pedals will be in motion", so the book tells the rider to wait for the flywheel to coast down or push
the knob to stop it, and never to stop it with the pedals. The safety section holds that warning. None of the
three books prints the flywheel weight.
