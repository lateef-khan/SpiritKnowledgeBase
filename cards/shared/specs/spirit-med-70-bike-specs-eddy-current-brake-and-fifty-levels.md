**The Operating Principle page states the mechanism in full**, in the same words in all three books:

> The patient pushes the pedals with their feet. The operator can increase the workload using the
> Up and Down Key on the console. When the workload change is requested, **an electromagnet is
> energized at field strengths relative to the workload requested. The magnetic field generated by
> the coil is induced into a flywheel creating eddy currents to flow in the flywheel. The eddy
> currents then create an opposing magnetic field to the electromagnetic field creating more or less
> resistance at the pedal**, determined by workload setting by the operator.

An energised coil, not permanent magnets on a swinging bracket and not a generator - which is why
these bikes plug in (`spirit-med-70-bike-specs-mains-90-to-240-v-1-76-to-0-71-a`) and why the safety
page warns the flywheel has no freewheel and needs the mechanical brake lever to stop it. The
service manuals call the unit an *Induction Brake* with a *Brake Controller*.

**The specification row:** *Resistance - Constant and Isokinetic with 50 levels of effort.* The Quick
Start page says "There are 50 levels of resistance displayed in 10 rows of LED lights", and the Set
Up menu's Level Scale sets what one level is worth - Fine 5 W, Medium 10 W or Coarse 15 W per level
at 60 RPM - so a level is not a fixed force.

**512 is not a user level.** The maintenance-mode Brake Test steps the coil "one bit at a time ...
There are 512 levels" (the service manual adds "don't stay in high level (more than 400) for too
long"); that is the controller's drive resolution behind the fifty console levels.

The 8.0U and 8.5R print the same principle with **100** levels
(`spirit-med-8-bike-specs-eddy-current-brake-and-one-hundred-levels`); the 4.0R and 4.0U are
generator bikes with 40 (`spirit-ce-specs-forty-resistance-levels`); the medical steppers on the
same brake print fifty (`spirit-climber-specs-constant-and-isokinetic-fifty-levels`).
