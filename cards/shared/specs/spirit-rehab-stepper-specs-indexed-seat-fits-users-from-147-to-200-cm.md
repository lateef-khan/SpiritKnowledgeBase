> **Indexed seat positioning accommodates users from 147 cm to 200 cm (4' 10" to 6' 7")**

The same sentence is on the features page of all five rehabilitation stepper manuals with a dot
matrix console, unchanged between the 2021 and 2025 generations.

**Both units convert.** 147 cm is 4 ft 9.9 in; 200 cm is 6 ft 6.7 in.

## This is a height range, not a weight limit and not a seat travel

- The weight allowance is a separate figure - **440 lb / 200 kg** -
  `spirit-climber-safety-user-weight-limit-440-lbs`.
- **No manual in the family prints a seat travel in inches or centimetres**, or a number of index
  positions. The seat carries a numbered scale on the aluminium slide tube for repeatable settings,
  and the book never says how many numbers are on it.

## The console will pick the position for you

Enter the patient's height under the **Set Up key → Position**, in inches or in centimetres if the
console is set to metric, and the software calculates the seat and arm positions. The manual warns
that this is a **starting point, not a final setting**, because a patient's body symmetry may vary.
See `spirit-rehab-stepper-console-set-up-key-position-by-height-and-the-track-or-step-graph`.

## Outside the range the manual says nothing

There is no instruction for a patient shorter than 147 cm or taller than 200 cm, and no warning
about what happens. The safety list only says to *"observe all minimum and maximum seat adjustment
settings"*. **Children are allowed at the discretion of a qualified physical therapist** -
`spirit-rehab-stepper-specs-application-specification-medical-purpose-and-patient-population` - and
the height range is the only size figure the manual gives to judge that by.
