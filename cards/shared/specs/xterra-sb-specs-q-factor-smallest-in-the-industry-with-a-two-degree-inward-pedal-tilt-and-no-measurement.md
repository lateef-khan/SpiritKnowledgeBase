**No number is printed.** The Features page gives two paragraphs to the Q factor and never measures it: "Typical
stationary exercise bikes are wider than a normal road bike to allow for the braking mechanism, pulleys, drive
components and beauty covers. Since the bike is wider, so is the distance between the pedals; this width between the
pedals is called the Q factor."

"Xterra has designed our pedal system so the Q factor is the smallest in the industry, but we did not stop there. We
have also custom designed and tooled a new pedal that provides a **two degree inward tilt** to compensate for the Q
factor not being perfect. Having a small Q factor in addition to the two degree inward tilt of the pedals puts the
user into a biomechanical neutral alignment."

The same two paragraphs are Dyaco boilerplate: the Spirit CU800 book prints them word for word
(`cu800-2012-specs-q-factor-and-pedal-tilt`), and the Sole bikes print a close cousin. None of them prints a
millimetre figure, so the claim cannot be compared between brands. The other Xterra bike books do not mention the Q
factor at all.
