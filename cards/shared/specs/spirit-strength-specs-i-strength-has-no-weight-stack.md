**These two are not weight-stack machines and no plate figure applies to them.** Their product label
reads `Model / Modele: i-Strength`, and the manual describes *"an advanced, electronically adaptive
resistance"* system: *"the i-Strength machine offers benefits that far exceed those of a traditional
weight stack."* The parts-of-your-unit page labels a Counterweight and a Gas Cylinder and no stack.

Their specification table carries two rows no other machine in the range has:

| Machine | Minimum Resistance | Maximum Resistance | Maximum User Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSI-CPSP Chest Press / Shoulder Press | 33 lb / 15 kg | **284 lb / 129 kg** | 350 lb / 160 kg |
| CSI-LROW Low Row | 13 lb / 6 kg | **265 lb / 120 kg** | 350 lb / 160 kg |

**The minimum matters as much as the maximum.** The chest press will not go below 33 lb, so it is not
a rehabilitation-light machine; the low row starts at 13 lb.

**The 350 lb user weight limit is lower than the rest of the range**, which is 360 lb on every
selectorized and dual-station machine.

**These figures are the resistance the system produces, not a stack weight, and there is no pin
setting to convert.** Resistance is set on the console. If a caller asks which plates to order for
one of these, the answer is none.

**Do not read a stack figure across from a same-named selectorized machine.** CSS-SCPR is a seated
chest press with a 240 lb plate stack and CSS-LROW a low row with 270 lb; neither number applies
here, and neither machine is this one.
