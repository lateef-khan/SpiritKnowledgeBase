**Neither XRW600 owner's manual names the resistance mechanism.** (The 2019 service manual does -
see the last section of this card.) A search of both whole
documents - the native text layer and every OCR-supplemented page - returns **not
one occurrence** of *fan*, *flywheel*, *magnet*, *magnetic*, *impeller*, *air
resistance* or *damper*. The single hit for *belt* in either book is the **chest
strap**.

**This is an absence, not a gap in the reading.** It was checked three ways:

1. A **loose-word search** on each term separately, not on a phrase.
2. **Against the OCR supplement as well as the native text**, so a flattened page
   could not hide the answer.
3. **The three pages that could carry it were rendered at 300 dpi** - the parts
   callout drawing on printed p. 9, the folding page on printed p. 31 and the
   maintenance page on printed p. 37. The render adds nothing the text layer does
   not have; the p. 9 drawing labels only Console, Front Stabilizer, Main Frame,
   Levelers, Seat, Foot Pedals, Aluminum Rail and Rear Stabilizer.

## The parts list would have said, and there is none

In two earlier waves of this repository the **parts list was the only place a
manual revealed its mechanism** - neither CIC indoor-cycle book ever says
"magnetic" while its parts list lists permanent magnets, and the CRW800's prose
never says "air" while its list carries a `Fan`, a `Flywheel` and a
`Generator/Brake Controller`. **The XRW600 books print no parts list at all** -
see `spirit-rower-specs-which-manuals-print-a-parts-list` - so the one source that
would have settled it is missing.

## Do not answer from the CRW800

The CRW800 shares this console chapter almost word for word, prints the **same
1 - 16 level scale**, and **is** a fan-and-flywheel machine braked by a gear motor
- see `crw800-2024-specs-resistance-system`. **A shared console chapter is not
evidence of a shared brake.** The two books were measured against each other at
**59% of 8-word phrases**, and the whole assembly, parts and maintenance half of
the CRW800 book is absent from the XRW600's.

**Do not tell an XRW600 owner they have a magnetic brake either.** The word does
not appear in their manual, and the nearest Spirit rower that has been described
that way in this repository was described wrongly.

## What the manual does say about resistance

Only that there is some, and how to change it:

- **The level range is 1~16**, changed with the Up and Down keys during a workout
  - `crw800-2024-specs-level-range-1-to-16`.
- *"Understand that changes in resistance do not occur immediately. Set your
  desired resistance level on the computer"* - the operation-instructions page.

**What to tell a caller:** the owner's manual does not state the mechanism, so
answer the level range from the book and send the mechanism question to Spirit
Fitness rather than inferring it from a sibling model. The XRW600's warranty page
does carry a **brake** column in the 2019 printing and drops it in 2021, which
tells you a brake exists and nothing about what kind - see
`xrw600-2019-warranty-lifetime-frame-and-brake-ten-year-parts`.

## The service manual settles it: a fan, a flywheel and a gear motor

The XRW600's **service manual** (DW400-YR002, the 2019 machine) is not silent. Its outline drawing
labels a **Flywheel & Fan** and a **Nylon Net** over it, and its replacement chapters take out the
"Flywheel(23) & Fan(22)" and the "Gear Motor(35)" with its "Steel Cable(66)". So the XRW600 is an
air rower whose brake is set by a motor, like the CRW800 - see
`xrw600-2019-specs-service-manual-names-a-fan-flywheel-and-gear-motor`. Everything above about
the owner's manuals stands: a caller reading their own book will not find the word, and the
service manual still never says *air* or prints a flywheel weight or drag figure.
