**The chapter names three parts and says one sentence about each:**

- **Console** - "Interface that controls all functions of the Bike."
- **Main controller** - "The circuit board consists of the DC power supply for console."
- **Generator brake** - "It can change to increase or decrease resistance level of brake."

Its *General information* adds that the console "Contains Key controls and **LED Display**" and that
"Main controllers include power supply, driver control circuit."

**No working voltage, current or resistance figure is printed for any of the three**, and no board
number appears in the text - the CS24004 display board and the CS52005 controller are named only on
the photographs and the circuit diagram.

**The bike is self-powered.** The operation chapter of both books says the 4.0 R "has a built-in
generator for power and does not need to be plugged into an AC outlet. To power up the bike simply
start to pedal" - the 4.0U book prints the sentence with *4.0 R* in it, a leftover from the
recumbent's text. The generator is the same unit that brakes the flywheel, which is why the parts
list calls the controller a *Generator/Brake Controller* and the flywheel unit an *Induction Brake*.

This is the same three-part text the 2023 XBR95 service manual prints, with *Brake Controller* where
this book says *Main controller* (`xbr95-2023-specs-electrical-configuration-generator-brake`); the
7.0R and 7.0U medical bikes are a different design that plugs into the mains
(`spirit-med-70-bike-specs-mains-power-through-an-ac-input-module-and-switching-power-supply`).
