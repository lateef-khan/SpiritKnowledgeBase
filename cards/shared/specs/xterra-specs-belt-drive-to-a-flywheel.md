**Belt, not chain, on every machine whose book names the drive.** The AIR650 (two chains) and the MB indoor cycles
(drive not named) are the exceptions. What each book prints:

| Machine | Drive parts named | Belt designation |
|---|---|---|
| SB600 | Plastic Belt Wheel Ø260 on the crank (A8), Belt (A12), Flywheel (A31), Idler Wheel Assembly | **900 J6** |
| SB120 | Crank w/ pulley (35), Belt (59), Flywheel assembly (66), flywheel adjustors | **310 J5** |
| UB120 | Crank w/ Pulley (8), Belt (42), Flywheel Assembly (36), Flywheel Adjustor Assembly Ø18x58L | **310J J5** |
| FB160, FB360 | Pulley (36), Pulley (60), Idler wheel (41), Flywheel (39), Belt (65) - **two belts**, quantity 2 | not printed |
| SB4500 | Drive Pully (10), Idler Wheel Assembly (13), Flywheel (62), Drive Belt (63) | not printed |
| SB150, SB250, FS150 | the troubleshooting table: "Squealing - V-belt slipping - Adjust v-belt"; "Grinding Noise - Crank bearing / Idler pulley / Flywheel defective" | not printed |
| AIR350 | "Belt pulley with Crank (18)" turning the fan | not printed |

"310 J5" and "900 J6" are poly-V (J-section) belt sizes - 310 mm with five ribs, 900 mm with six. The SB600 service
manual says a belt that slips is fixed at the idler wheel assembly's adjustment screw (A13), and a belt that comes off
"similar to a bicycle chain coming off" is refitted and moved one notch against the slip; a belt that keeps coming
off means a bent Small Iron Tube (A84), idler assembly or frame. The maintenance and assembly sections hold those
procedures. No book prints the flywheel weight.
