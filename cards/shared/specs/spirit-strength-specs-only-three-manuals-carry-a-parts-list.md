**Thirty-six of the thirty-nine have no parts list. Their "Exploded View Diagram" is a drawing with
balloon numbers and no key** - one or two pages of line art, and nothing that tells you what a
balloon number is or how to order it. On the CSI pair the pages carry no text at all beyond the
heading; a render of both returns `EXPLODED VIEW DIAGRAM` and the page number and nothing else.

**Where a component name does exist, it is in the assembly hardware tables**, step by step, as
`No. | Description | Specifications | Qty`. Those are numbered locally to the assembly drawing - the
manual says so: *"The numbers in the instructions that are in parenthesis (#) are the item number
from the assembly drawing for reference."* **They are not part numbers.** Item 210 is
`Pulley Set with Magnets` in one book and something else in the next.

## The three that do have a list

| Manual | Page | Items | Also has |
|---|---|---|---|
| ST800DR3 Dumbbell Rack | printed 9 | 15 | Exploded view |
| ST800FI Flat Incline Bench | printed 10 | 44 | Exploded view |
| ST800FT Functional Trainer | printed 12-13 | 77 | Exploded view |

These are proper `Item No. / Description / QTY` tables covering the whole machine, and they carry
fastener sizes - `Hex Bolts M12 x 160`, `Bushing φ38 x φ25.4 x 18`, `Washer φ13 x φ24 x 1.5`. **Still
no orderable part number.** The description and the size are all there is.

## What this means at a parts desk

- **Do not promise a customer a part number from an owner's manual in this range.** There is none to
  give, on any of the thirty-nine.
- **For the thirty-six, the assembly step is the only description of a part.** To identify a bolt or
  a pad, find the assembly step that installs it and read the Description and Specifications columns.
- **Quote the machine and the balloon number together or the number means nothing**, because the
  numbering restarts per book.

The one place a real dimension is printed is the pulley and the two rubber bumpers -
`spirit-strength-specs-only-component-dimensions-are-the-pulley-and-two-bumpers`.
