The Digital Resistance Module is the boxed motor unit that the CSI owner's manuals list as part
72 of each machine. Its maintenance manual names its outside and inside on three pages, **names
only - no item numbers, part numbers or dimensions**.

**Front View, eight labels:** Screen, Screen holder, Upper cover, Light strip, Left cover, Front
panel, **Steel rope** (the cable that comes out of the front panel to the machine's pulleys), Foot
pad.

**Back View, five labels:** Screen back cover, Right cover, Rear cover, **AC Switch**, **Power
inlet**.

**Anatomical Diagram, seven labels** (the module opened): **PFC Module**, **Controller module**,
**Cement resistor**, **AC Switch**, **Fan**, **Motor module**, **Power inlet**.

**How they map to the electronics.** The PFC module is the power panel that makes the 320 V DC bus;
the controller module is the motor-control motherboard; the cement resistor is the 300 W 40-ohm
brake resistor pack; the motor module is the servo motor with its reel and rope
(`spirit-strength-specs-i-strength-module-electronic-system-block-diagram`). The disassembly
chapter takes them out in that order - rear cover, bottom plate, motor module (after pulling its
communication, power, ground and temperature-sensing wires and separating the red, black and
blue motor terminals), reel and rope, controller and resistor, PFC module, the two cooling fans,
then the screen, front panel and light strip, and the covers.

The cables between them are numbered on the internal-cable page
(`spirit-strength-specs-parts-digital-resistance-module-internal-cable-list`). Nothing on these
three pages names a weight stack, because the machine has none
(`spirit-strength-specs-i-strength-has-no-weight-stack`).
