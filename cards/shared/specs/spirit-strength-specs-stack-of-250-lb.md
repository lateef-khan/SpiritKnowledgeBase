**The manual prints no weight-stack rating.** This total is the sum of the plate quantities its
assembly hardware table lists, and it is not a figure the manufacturer states anywhere in the book -
say so when you quote it. Why that is, and how it was checked, is at
`spirit-strength-specs-no-weight-stack-rating-printed`.

**The most common stack in the range - eight of the nineteen selectorized single stations.** Every
one comes to 250 lb from sixteen or seventeen pieces, but the mix differs:

| Machine | 20 lb plates | 10 lb plates | Top plate | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSS-LATR | 10 | 4 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-LEXT | 10 | 4 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-PRLC | 10 | 4 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-SROW | 10 | 4 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-SLGC | 10 | 1 x `10lb weight stack-A` + 3 x `10lb weight stack-B` | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-GLUT | 8 | 8 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-LATP | 8 | 8 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |
| CSS-SCEX | 8 | 8 | 1 x 10 lb | 250 lb |

**The three eight-and-eight machines step in 10 lb over half the stack**; the five ten-and-four
machines step in 10 lb only over the bottom four plates. If a customer is choosing between a lat
pulldown and a lateral raise on step size, that is the difference.

CSS-SLGC splits its four ten-pound plates across two part numbers, `-A` and `-B`. Its page 11
specification table is also font-corrupted in the PDF, so read that machine from a render.

The optional 5 lb increment weight rides on top of the stack on the machines that have one -
`spirit-strength-specs-five-pound-increment-weight`.
