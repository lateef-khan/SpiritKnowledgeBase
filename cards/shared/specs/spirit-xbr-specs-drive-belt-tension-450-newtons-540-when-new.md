**450 N**, read with a **belt tension gauge** once the J-bolt's M8 x 7T nyloc nut has been
tightened with a 13 mm wrench until the idler wheel presses the belt. Then the note every one of
these four books adds:

> If the drive belt were new, it has to adjust the tension of value to be **540N**, because the
> belt has ductility and the tension of value will be decreased while using a period of time.

So a **new belt is set high, at 540 N, and settles toward 450 N**; a belt that has run is set to
450 N. The books give the figure as a force - no frequency and no gauge part number.

**Every one of the four Q&A pages garbles the unit**: "turn the nut clockwise until **sound wave
frequency falls between 450N**". A sonic gauge reads hertz, not newtons. The replacement step is
the figure to trust; the Q&A only tells you which nut to turn.

**The uprights and the generator recumbents use hertz instead** - 180 ±10 Hz on the XBU55
(`spirit-xbu55-specs-drive-belt-tension-180-hz-sonic-meter`) and on the XBR95 and XR898
(`spirit-xbr95-specs-drive-belt-tension-180-hz-acoustic-gauge`).
