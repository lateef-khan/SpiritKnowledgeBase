**The Operating Principle page** prints the eddy-current paragraph the whole medical range uses - an
electromagnet "energized at field strengths relative to the workload requested", its field induced
into the flywheel, the eddy currents "creating more or less resistance at the pedal" (the 8.5R page
prints "and electromagnet" for *an*). An energised coil, not permanent magnets and not a generator;
the service manuals photograph it as the *Brake and Angle Sensor* on two coil sockets of the lower
control board (`spirit-med-8-bike-specs-lower-control-board-cs51009-01-sockets-and-leds`).

**The specification row:** *Resistance - Constant and Isokinetic with 100 levels of effort.* Twice
the fifty the 7.0R and 7.0U print for the same brake
(`spirit-med-70-bike-specs-eddy-current-brake-and-fifty-levels`); the level scale is a console
setting, and no level-to-watts table is printed.

**Bidirectional.** The *Unique uses* page adds: "Bidirectional resistance allows both forward and
reverse pedaling for instantaneous retro-cycling. It provides a lower start-up, smooth, and
comfortable resistance." The same page names the exclusive 2% slope on the pedal surface and the
optional adjustable crank arms, which shorten the pedal circle to a range of motion as small as 15
degrees.

The work load range the brake spans is `spirit-med-8-bike-specs-workload-5-to-1200-watts`. The 8.5UE
ergometer on the same platform prints fifty levels for its arm crank
(`85ue-2025-specs-eddy-current-brake-and-fifty-levels`).
