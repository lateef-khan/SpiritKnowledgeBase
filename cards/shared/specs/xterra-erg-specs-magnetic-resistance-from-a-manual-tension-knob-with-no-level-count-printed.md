**A knob, a cable, a bracket of magnets at the flywheel - and no level count anywhere.** The ERG180 cover calls the
machine a MAGNETIC ROWER and its parts drawing labels a **Resistance Knob** (C) on the front of the frame; the
service manual calls the same part the **Tension Control Knob** (7 on the outline, part 30) and lists a magnet
bracket (8) carrying six magnets (87). The ERG160 book lists the same set - Tension knob (30), Magnet bracket (8),
five 25 x 10 x 5 mm magnets (87) and a Ø150 flywheel (89) - and its service manual describes the link: the knob
pulls a **tension cable** to the magnet bracket, and after reassembly "the minimum space between magnets and
flywheel is generally set at about 2.5-3mm" (that replacement procedure is the assembly section's). The ERG220
book names the mechanism only in its troubleshooting table: "No tension - Tension needs adjustment - Turn tension
adjustment knob accordingly", and "Magnetic wheel not working properly - Replace magnetic wheel".

**None of the three owner's manuals prints how many positions the knob has, or describes turning it.** The
adjustment pages cover the pedal straps, the levelers, moving and storing; the consoles run on two AA cells and
have no level window. Treat the resistance as continuous between the knob's stops.

The ERG160 book also lists a separate **Adjust knob (47)** - that is the console's mounting knob, which the
service manual turns counterclockwise to remove the console, not a resistance control. The console-driven Xterra
rowers have sixteen levels (`xterra-specs-resistance-has-16-levels`); the ERG500 uses an air damper
(`erg500-2018-specs-air-resistance-with-a-damper-set-by-a-tension-lever-and-no-level-count`); the Xterra EU100,
SB120 and UB120 bikes have a marked eight-level knob (`xterra-specs-manual-tension-knob-with-8-levels`).
