**Twenty levels of resistance, and the same four bands of five in all ten
printings:**

- **The first 5 levels** are very easy workloads; the changes between levels are
  set to a good progression for de-conditioned users.
- **Levels 6-10** are more challenging, but the increases in resistance from one
  level to the next remain small.
- **Levels 11-15** start getting tough, as the levels jump more dramatically.
- **Levels 16-20** are extremely hard, and are good for short interval peaks and
  elite athletic training.

This paragraph is word for word the same across all ten books - three XBR25 and
XBR55 recumbents, three XBU55 uprights, both 2023 printings and both ENT
touchscreen printings. The 2023 books restate it as a numbered list rather than a
paragraph; the figures do not change. **Level 20 is the maximum on all ten.**

The 2023 consoles confirm the scale on their faceplate: the ten Direct Resistance
Level buttons on the XBR25 2023, XBR55 2023 and XBU55 2023 are marked **2, 4, 6,
8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 and 20**, evenly spaced. The markings were read from the
console drawing rendered at 400 dpi, since that page is a picture with no text
behind it. The XBR95 2023 strip, by contrast, runs to 40.

## One residential bike in this range has forty, not twenty

The XBR95, in all four of its printings, prints the same paragraph with **40**
levels and bands of ten - see
`spirit-residential-bike-specs-forty-resistance-levels`. A level number means a
different workload on the two scales, so **never quote a level from one on the
other**.

## No physical rating goes with the number

Level 1 to 20 is a console scale, not a measurement. **No Spirit residential bike
manual prints a level-to-watts table**, and the Watts readout reports the work the
rider is doing rather than the setting. There is no resistance figure in watts,
newtons or kilograms anywhere in the range - see
`spirit-residential-bike-specs-no-specification-table`.
