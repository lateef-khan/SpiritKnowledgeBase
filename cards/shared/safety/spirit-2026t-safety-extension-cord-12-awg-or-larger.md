Both manuals print the rule inside the same safety instruction as the outlet requirement:

> Do not use an extension cord unless it is 12 awg(2.5mm) or larger, with only one outlet on the
> end. The 7.0T should be the only appliance in the electrical circuit. Do not attempt to disable
> the grounded plug by using improper adapters, or in any way modify the cord set; a serious
> shock or fire hazard may result along with computer malfunctions.

The 8.0T manual prints the identical sentence with `8.0T` in place of `7.0T`.

- **12 awg minimum.** This is the heaviest extension-cord figure in any Spirit manual.
- **One outlet on the end.** Not a multi-way lead and not a power strip.
- **Nothing else on that circuit.**
- **Never defeat the grounding pin** with an adapter, and never modify the cord set.

**The metric figure in the brackets does not match the AWG figure.** 12 AWG is about 3.3 mm2,
while 2.5 mm2 is nearer 13 AWG. Both manuals print `12 awg(2.5mm)` exactly that way, and neither
reconciles the two. **12 awg is the safe reading** - it is the larger conductor, and it is the
number the sentence leads with.

**The 4.0T in the same family asks for a far thinner cord** - 1 mm2, roughly 17 AWG:
`40t-2026-safety-extension-cord-1-mm2-or-better`. The two figures are not interchangeable, and
neither is the 16 AWG of the CT850 2016 or the 14 AWG of the CT850 2020.
