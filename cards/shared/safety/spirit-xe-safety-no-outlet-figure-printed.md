**None of these eight manuals prints a voltage, an amp figure, a breaker rating or a NEMA type.**
That is an absence, not a zero, and not a licence to quote a figure from another XE manual - the
other six residential machines are given 110 volt or 115 volt at 15 amp
(`spirit-xe-safety-outlet-110-volt-15-amp`, `spirit-xe-safety-outlet-115-volt-15-amp`), and the
commercial CE850 is given 110 volt at **5 amp** (`spirit-ce850-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`).

The eight split into three groups for three different reasons.

## The 2005 and 2007 machines run from an external power supply

**XE100, XE200, XE300, XE150, XE350, XE550.** Both manuals say only:

> The XE Series Elliptical is supplied with an external power supply. When power is connected to the
> Elliptical, the console will light up.

There is **no `flat level surface` WARNING, no outlet sentence, no extension-cord rule and no
grounding page** in either book (`spirit-xe-2005-2007-safety-instructions-list`). Their electrical
page carries only the cover-removal rule, the ten-percent supply-voltage paragraph and the
rain-and-moisture rule. **Neither manual states the adapter's own rating**, so no voltage or current
figure may be given for these six machines from the owner's manual.

## The XE395-2023 plugs in, and its manual still names no figure

Its assembly page has the reader `Plug the power cord into the machine and a grounded wall outlet`,
and `Power Cord` is item 101 of its parts list, so the machine is mains-powered. Its ELECTRICAL
SAFETY page nevertheless gives no voltage and no amp figure. What it says about the circuit is one
sentence:

> Optimally the unit should be the only device plugged into the circuit.

together with the AFCI/GFCI paragraph naming the breaker brands that passed testing
(`xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`). **`Optimally` is the manual's own word; it is not the
`with only the elliptical plugged into the circuit` requirement the 2010 and 2021 manuals print.**

## The XE795-2023 is generator-powered and has no electrical page at all

Its console runs off a built-in generator driven by the pedals and **no power cord appears in its
parts list** (`spirit-ce-safety-generator-produces-ac-power`). Its table of contents runs from
Product Labels to Warnings/Compliance/Notes to Safety Instructions and straight on to Parts
Included - **there is no electrical page**, so it prints no outlet figure, no extension-cord gauge,
no grounding instructions, no circuit-breaker paragraph, no supply-voltage rule, no
electrical-storm rule and no temperature or humidity limit. Exactly the CES880-2025 pattern
(`ces880-2025-safety-instructions-list`).

**The XE395-2023 manual, the same 2023 book for the sibling machine, does have that page.** The two
2023 manuals are otherwise a matched pair; this is the one chapter that only one of them carries.

## What to tell a customer

Say the manual does not state a supply requirement, and get the figure from the machine's own rating
label or from Spirit. **Do not read the 15 amp figure across from the 2010 or 2021 XE books**, and
never read the treadmill figures across - a Spirit treadmill is 15 or 20 amp for a motor these
machines do not have.

## The CR900ENT-2021 and CU900ENT bikes plug in, and their manuals name no figure either

Both books say only:

> Plug in the power cord located at the front base of the unit. When the power is turned on, the
> screen will show the initial image and then enter the Home screen.

- **They are mains-powered.** There is a power cord and a power switch, and the touchscreen console
  will not come up without them.
- **No voltage, no amperage, no breaker rating, no NEMA type, no extension-cord rule and no
  grounding page** appears in either manual. The words *volt*, *amp*, *outlet*, *grounded* and
  *circuit breaker* are absent from both.
- **Their electrical page is missing something too.** Where the non-ENT CR900 and CU900 manuals open
  `IMPORTANT ELECTRICAL INSTRUCTIONS` with the spinning-generator shock warning, these two print
  only the rain, moisture, temperature and humidity paragraph. That is the tell that they are not
  self-powered machines.

Do not fill the gap from a sibling. The CU800ENT-2022 and CR800ENT-2023 are given **110 volt at 5
amp** (`spirit-ce850-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`) and the CU800ENT-2024 **120 volt at 15
amp** (`spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-120-volt-15-amp`); nothing in the CR900ENT or CU900ENT manual
says either applies. Get the rating from the machine's own data plate or from Spirit.

## The CR900ENT service manual does print a voltage - on the adapter, not as a requirement

Its circuit diagram draws the supply: a wall plug into an `AC ADAPTER FSP100-RTAAN2, INPUT: AC
100-240V, OUTPUT: DC 24V / 4.17A`, a DC jack, and a `DC 24V INPUT` on the driver board; its
software-update page says `turn off the Elliptical / Bike power to wait adapter to discharge for 3
seconds then resume the power`. So the CR900ENT runs from an external universal adapter that accepts
100 to 240 volts AC. **That is the adapter's own rating, read off a drawing; the book still prints no
outlet requirement, no amp figure, no breaker, no extension-cord gauge and no grounding page** -
the statement above stands for the CU900ENT and for the CR900ENT's owner's manual. The CU1000ENT is
the same arrangement with a 100 W, 24 V / 5 A adapter
(`cu1000ent-2023-safety-mains-powered-through-a-100-w-adapter`).

## The 2007 dealer manual does state the adapter's rating - and gives the XE400 and XE500 a power cord

**The 2007 Spirit Manufacturing dealer service manual for the XE100, XE200, XE300, XE400 and XE500
prints no outlet voltage, no amperage, no breaker, no extension-cord rule, no grounding page and no
safety chapter of any kind.** Its contents are a two-page troubleshooting guide, nine repair
procedures, the dealer warranty pages and five parts lists; the words *GFCI*, *AWG* and *weight
limit* appear nowhere in it, and *safety* only as the engineering-mode item `Safety On/Off`. So the
statement above stands for all five machines - **but the book does say what powers them**:

> No power to the console - 1. Unit must be plugged into an outlet with power. 2. Test adapter for
> voltage with a volt meter. (Procedure 1)

- **The XE100, XE200 and XE300 run from a plug-in DC adapter, and the book labels it.** Procedure 1
  says `The adapter will be labeled 9VDC or 12VDC` and expects a reading a few volts above the label;
  the XE100 and XE200 parts lists carry `Adaptor 1.5 Amp` (000137) and the XE300 list `9V,DV,2.0A
  Adaptor - 2.0 Amp` (000138). That is the adapter's own output rating, read off the part, not an
  outlet requirement - the same position the CR900ENT service manual leaves the CR900ENT in (below).
- **The XE400 and XE500 plug in with a power cord.** Their parts lists carry a `Power Cord` (022812),
  an `On/Off Switch` with `Ground Wire` (006093), and an `Incline Adaptor (Transformer)` (006096)
  feeding the incline controller; Procedure 6 wires the on/off switch to the transformer, and the
  XE400/XE500 wiring schematic draws a `PLUG`, the transformer and the controller. **Neither list
  carries a 9 or 12 V adapter**, and the book still prints no voltage for the cord, no amperage and no
  grounding rule beyond the part name. Get the rating from the machine's own label.

**The XE400 and XE500 have no owner's manual on file**; this dealer book is their only source, and it
prints no safety instructions for them at all (`spirit-xe-2005-2007-safety-instructions-list` is the
XE100-XE300 owner's list). The 2007 XBR25 and XBR55 bikes are the same arrangement as the XE100-XE300
(`spirit-xbr-2007-safety-mains-power-through-an-ac-adapter`).

## The CE900ENT service manual prints the same adapter drawing as the CR900ENT book

**The CE900ENT elliptical service manual prints no safety chapter, no outlet figure, no breaker, no
cord gauge and no grounding page.** Its circuit diagram, headed `(CE900-ENT) ELLIPTICAL CIRCUIT
DIAGRAM`, draws a wall plug into an `AC ADAPTER FSP100-RTAAN2, INPUT: AC 100-240V, OUTPUT: DC 24V /
4.17A`, a DC jack and the `DYA-W10A-IMX6-R10-LCD BOARD`; the driver-board page labels the feed `DC
24V INPUT`; and its software-update page says `turn off the Elliptical / Bike power to wait adapter
to discharge for 3 seconds then resume the power`. So the CE900ENT runs from the same external
universal adapter as the CR900ENT, accepting 100 to 240 volts AC - **the adapter's own rating, read
off a drawing, and no requirement anywhere in the book**. The word *ground* appears once, as the
`GROUND` connector on the I/O board. The CE1000ENT-2023 is the same arrangement with a 100 W, 24 V /
5 A adapter (`ce1000ent-2023-safety-mains-powered-through-a-100-w-adapter`); the LED-console CE800
and CE900 need no outlet at all (`spirit-ce-safety-no-mains-outlet-needed`).

**The XE795-2023 service manual does print a figure**, unlike the owner's manual on this card: a
220-volt, 15-amp outlet and a nominal 120-volt circuit, on a machine with no cord
(`xe795-2023-safety-outlet-220-volt-15-amp`). The XE395-2023 has no service manual on file.
