> The flywheel in the 7.0R does not have a freewheel, but is directly connected to the pedals. The
> 7.0R is equipped with auto-braking software that will stop the flywheel when it detects the user
> is trying to stop pedaling. In the unlikely event that the electronics fails, or the Auto-brake
> function is disabled in the software, a spinning flywheel can make the 7.0R difficult to stop when
> pedaling at higher RPM(Revolutions per minute). There is an emergency brake lever provided that
> will stop the flywheel when pressed.

- **No freewheel.** The pedals are coupled to the flywheel, so a spinning flywheel keeps the cranks
  turning - a patient cannot simply stop pushing and let it coast.
- **Auto-braking software is the normal stop.** It brakes the flywheel when it detects the user
  trying to stop pedaling.
- **The lever is the back-up.** It is for the case the electronics fail or the auto-brake is
  disabled in software, and it stops the flywheel when pressed. The manual does not say where on the
  frame it is in this sentence; the parts drawing labels it `Manual mechanical brake` on the 7.0R
  and `Manual mechanical brake lever` on the 8.0U.
- **The risk is at higher RPM**, which is when a coupled flywheel carries the most energy.

**The 8.0U and 8.5R print the same paragraph with two changes** - `and it is a speed independent
system` after `directly connected to the pedals`, and `a manual mechanical brake lever` where the
7.0R and 7.0U write `an emergency brake lever`. Same control, two names.

**The item above it is about the cranks catching clothing:**

> Warning: The adjustable crank arms may become entangled in pant legs if the pant legs are loose
> fitting. To avoid injury roll up the pant legs or secure the pant legs in some other fashion.

The 8.0U and 8.5R call the crank arms `optional` and end `in some other safety method`. The
adjustable crank is what lets a patient pedal in a range of knee motion as small as 15 degrees.

**The Dyaco MED 7.0R of 2021 prints both bullets** word for word, with `emergency brake lever`.

**The Spirit indoor cycles have no freewheel either, and no auto-brake** - their rule is to slow
the pedals with the brake knob (`spirit-indoor-cycle-safety-fixed-gear-dismount`). The 8.5UE
ergometer prints the no-freewheel sentence for its handles (`85ue-2025-safety-instructions-list`).
