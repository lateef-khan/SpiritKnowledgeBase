**None of the five folding-bike books tells the owner how to fold the bike, when to, or to check
the frame lock before riding.** The word *fold* appears only in the product name and the *How To
Move The Folding Bike* paragraph, which is about rolling it on its wheels:

> To move the folding bike, grasp the rear stabilizer and carefully lift it until the exercise
> folding bike can be rolled on the front wheels. Carefully move the folding bike to the desired
> location and then lower it.

The lock pin is mentioned once, in assembly step 1, as the way to open the frame:

> Pull out the Lock Pin (No. 11) from the Front Support Tube (No. 01) and adjust the Front Support
> Tube (No. 01) to the open position then re-insert the Lock Pin (No. 11) to secure the frame
> position. (FB160, FB360, FB180; the FB150 and FB350 say `adjust main frame (01) to the correct
> position then insert lock pin (11) to tighten the main frame`)

Reversing that step is the only folding procedure the books imply, and no page says so. The
maintenance checks - seat nuts, pedals, stabilizer bolts, handlebar - do not include the pin, and the
safety list has no *ensure the frame is locked* item.

How it was checked: every page read; the text searched for `fold`, `lock`, `pin` and `latch`; the
hits are the product name, assembly step 1, the parts list (`Lock pin`) and the moving paragraph.

**What a customer can be told**: the pin that opens the frame is the pin that holds it open; it must
be fully re-inserted before the stabilizers go on and before every ride, because nothing else locks
the front support tube. That is the assembly step read as a rule, not a sentence the manual prints.
