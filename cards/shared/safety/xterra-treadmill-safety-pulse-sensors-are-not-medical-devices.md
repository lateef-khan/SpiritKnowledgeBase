**Fifteen of the nineteen manuals print this sentence on the safety page**, as a Fitness Equipment
bullet (TR300, TR6.6, TR6.4, TRX2500, TRX3500, TRX4500, TRX5500) or as item 18 of the numbered list
(TRX1400, TR65, TR75, TR85, TR95H, TR75H, WS200, WS300):

> The pulse sensors are not medical devices. Various factors, including the user's movement, may
> affect the accuracy of heart rate readings. The pulse sensors are intended only as exercise aids
> in determining heart rate trends in general.

The eight new-layout books say it a second time on the safety warning sticker: `Heart Rate monitoring
systems can be inaccurate; use them for reference only`
(`xterra-treadmill-safety-warning-sticker-on-the-upright`).

**The TR150, TR200, TRX1000 and TR260 print no such sentence.** Their safety page has no pulse bullet;
the TR150 and TRX1000 have no hand pulse at all, the TR200 and TR260 do (a handgrip pulse feature) and
still say nothing about accuracy. The only disclaimer those four give is the calorie note, `This is
only a rough guide ... which cannot be used for medical purposes`, which is about calories, not pulse.

How to read a pulse on the grips or a chest strap, and the erratic-reading checklist, are console
cards; this is the safety rule that the number is a trend, not a diagnosis.

The Spirit 2026 treadmill and Sole E35 print the same sentence for their own machines
(`spirit-2026t-safety-heart-rate-readings-are-not-medical`, `e35-2026-pulse-sensor-accuracy-disclaimer`).

**Bikes, ellipticals and the seated stepper.** Twenty-three of the thirty non-treadmill books print
the same sentence word for word - every book with the appliance-style list: as a Fitness Equipment
bullet on the FB150, FB350, FB160, FB360, EU100, EU150, SB150, SB250, SB2.5r, SB4500, SB500, SB4.5r,
RSX1500, FS1.5, FS2.5, FS3.0, FS3.5, FS150, FS5.8e and FS5.9e; as item 13 of the FB180 list; as item 18
of the SB240 and SB600 list (`xterra-safety-instructions-list-8-item-appliance-list`,
`xterra-safety-instructions-list-13-item-plug-in-list`, `fb180-2025-safety-instructions-list-16-items`,
`xterra-sb-safety-instructions-list-21-items`). The SB240 and SB600 sticker adds `Heart Rate monitoring
systems can be inaccurate; use them for reference only` (`xterra-sb-safety-warning-sticker-on-the-upright`).

**The seven books with the indoor-cycle list print no such sentence** - AIR350, AIR650, MB500, MB550,
MBX2500, SB120 and UB120. The SB120 and UB120, which do have hand pulse grips, say on the console page
only that `The measurement value shouldn't be regarded as the basis of medical treatment`; the FB150,
FB350 and FB180 console pages say the same thing a second time. Those are console cards.

**Rowers.** Four of the eleven rower books print the sentence word for word - as a Fitness Equipment
bullet on the ERG220 and ERG400 (`xterra-erg-safety-instructions-list-13-item-plug-in-list`), as item 13
of the ERG750W list (`erg750w-2025-safety-instructions-list-16-items`) and item 18 of the ERG800W list
(`erg800w-2023-safety-instructions-list-21-items`). Two more say it only on the frame sticker - `Heart
rate monitoring systems can be inaccurate; use them for reference only` on the ERG160 label
(`xterra-erg-safety-warning-sticker-with-the-yellow-belt-mark`) and the ERG180 sticker
(`xterra-rower-safety-warning-sticker-bilingual-children-under-13`); the ERG220 and ERG800W have it in
both places.

**The ERG500, ERG550W, ERG600W, ERG650W and ERG700 print no such sentence** - every one of them reads
a pulse from a wireless chest strap and none of their safety pages says the reading is a trend, not a
diagnosis; the ERG500 console page's calorie note (`should not be used as a medical standard`) is about
calories. The four strength books have no pulse reading and no sentence.
