**All three manuals print the rule, with one word of difference.**

The 4.0T:

> 4. Keep children away from the treadmill. There are obvious pinch points and other caution areas
>    that can cause harm.

The 7.0T and 8.0T name the moving parts as well:

> 5. Keep children away from the 7.0T. There are moving parts, obvious pinch points and other
>    caution areas that can cause harm.

- **The rule is keep away, not supervise.** It applies whether or not the machine is running - the
  hazard named is the pinch points, which are there when it is still.
- **No age is attached to this item** in any of the three manuals.

**Each family qualifies it elsewhere, differently.** The 4.0T permits a child of 8 or above to use
the machine under supervision (`40t-2026-safety-children-aged-8-and-above`); the 7.0T and 8.0T
permit a child only at a qualified physical therapist's discretion or with parental consent
(`spirit-2026t-safety-intended-patient-population`). Neither permission cancels this rule for a
child who is not being supervised.

The related rule about stopping a child starting the machine is
`spirit-2026t-safety-secure-the-tether-cord-after-use`.

**Both Spirit MT200 owner's manuals print the 7.0T and 8.0T form of the rule**, with `treadmill`
in place of the machine's name:

> Keep children away from the treadmill. There are moving parts, obvious pinch points and other
> caution areas that can cause harm.

Keep away rather than supervise, and **no age is attached to it** in either MT200 manual.

**The MT200 manuals qualify it differently from the 2026 machines.** The 2010 manual's intended
patient population says only that there is no particular restriction on age, gender, height and
nationality; the 2022 manual adds `Children at the discretion of a Qualified Physical Therapist
and or parental consent`, the 7.0T and 8.0T wording. Neither manual prints a rule about storing
the tether cord away from children.
