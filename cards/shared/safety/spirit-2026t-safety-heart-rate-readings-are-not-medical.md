**This is a safety disclaimer about what the reading is worth, not an explanation of how the
console reads a pulse.**

The **4.0T** states it in full, as two items of its safety instructions:

> 16. The hand pulse sensors are not medical devices. Various factors, including the user's
>     movement, may affect the accuracy of heart rate readings. The pulse sensors are intended
>     only as an exercise aid in determining heart rate trends in general.

> 17. Heart rate monitoring systems may be inaccurate. Over exercising may result in serious
>     injury or death. If you feel faint stop exercising immediately.

The **8.0T** states the short form as item 13, `Heart Rate Monitoring systems may be inaccurate`,
and says on its Application Specification page what the readings may be used for:

> the heart rate readings are not for medical purpose but for information purposes only used for
> physical evaluation and physical planning purposes

It also notes there that **a heart rate monitor is not standard equipment on the 8.0T** - it is an
optional purchase.

- **Not a medical device**, on either machine. The reading is an exercise aid for trends in
  general.
- **User movement is the cause the 4.0T names** for a bad reading from the hand grips.
- **Do not use it for a clinical decision.** The 8.0T manual says so in those words.

**The 7.0T manual prints no heart rate disclaimer.** Its equivalent safety item, item 13, is the
over-exercise warning alone, with the heart rate clause absent
(`spirit-2026t-safety-over-exercise-can-cause-injury-or-death`).

The Sole manuals carry a disclaimer of the same shape
(`sole-bike-hand-pulse-not-a-medical-device`); it is a different brand's manual and not an answer
for a Spirit machine.
