**All seven older-layout manuals print the same two paragraphs, on the safety page**, and they are the
only place any Xterra owner's manual names an amperage or a cord gauge.

> WARNING - To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the
> treadmill on a flat level surface with access to a 120-volt, 15-amp grounded outlet.
>
> DO NOT USE AN EXTENSION CORD UNLESS IT IS 14AWG OR BETTER, WITH ONLY ONE OUTLET ON THE END. THE
> TREADMILL SHOULD BE THE ONLY APPLIANCE IN THE CIRCUIT IN WHICH IT IS CONNECTED. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO
> DISABLE THE GROUNDED PLUG BY USING IMPROPER ADAPTERS, OR IN ANY WAY MODIFY THE CORD SET. A SERIOUS
> SHOCK OR FIRE HAZARD MAY RESULT ALONG WITH COMPUTER MALFUNCTIONS.

| Requirement | Figure |
|---|---|
| Outlet | 120-volt, 15-amp, grounded |
| Surface | flat and level |
| Extension cord | only if 14 AWG or better, with a single outlet on its end |
| Circuit | the treadmill is the only appliance on it |
| Plug | never disabled with an adapter, cord set never modified |

The grounding page of the same books agrees on the voltage - `for use on a nominal 120-volt circuit`
(`xterra-treadmill-safety-grounding-nominal-120-volt-circuit`).

**The service manuals disagree.** The Dyaco service books for the TR150 and TR260 ask for a
`16 AWG or better` cord and, on the TR150, a `230-volt, 10-amp (110-volt, 15A)` outlet
(`xterra-treadmill-safety-service-manual-outlet-and-extension-cord-figures`). Quote the owner's manual
to an owner and say the service book differs.

**The troubleshooting table of three of these books says 16 gauge and 110 volts.** The Service
Checklist row *Treadmill will only achieve approximately 7 mph but shows higher speed on display* in
the TR300, TR6.6 and TR6.4 reads `Do not use an extension cord. If an extension cord is required it
should be as short as possible and heavy duty 16 gauge minimum ... A minimum of 110 volt AC current is
required.` That row is an errors card and a slow-belt remedy; the 14 AWG on this page is the heavier
cord and the installation rule, and 14 AWG satisfies both.

**The 2023 and later books drop both paragraphs.** The TRX 2024 batch and the new-layout books print no
amperage and no cord gauge on their safety pages
(`xterra-treadmill-safety-no-outlet-amperage-or-extension-cord-rule-printed`).

The Spirit CT850-2020 prints the same 14 AWG rule for its own machine
(`ct850-2020-extension-cord-14-awg-or-better`); the Spirit XT-2015 books ask for 110 volts
(`xt-2015-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`). Those are Spirit cards.
