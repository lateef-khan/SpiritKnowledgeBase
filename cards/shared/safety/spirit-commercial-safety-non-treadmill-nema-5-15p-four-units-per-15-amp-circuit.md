**Three rules for every non-treadmill machine that plugs in, and the middle one is the opposite of what the manuals say.**

> For those non-treadmill cardio units that require power, they use a NEMA 5-15P plug.

> Those that require a 120V/15AMP circuit, they do not require a dedicated circuit. Up to four (4) units may be daisy-chained together on each 15AMP circuit.

> The non-treadmill cardio receptacles must use a NEMA 5-15R or NEMA 5-20R T-slot receptacle.

| Rule | Figure |
|---|---|
| Plug on the cord | NEMA 5-15P |
| Receptacle | NEMA 5-15R, or NEMA 5-20R T-slot |
| Dedicated circuit | **not required** |
| Machines per 15 amp circuit | **up to four** |

- **Which machines**: the 120V/15AMP column of the sheet's table - every ENT console, the CE850, the medical bikes and steppers, every stepper and the CRW800 rower (`spirit-commercial-safety-non-treadmill-120-volt-15-amp-or-self-generating`). The self-generating machines have no plug and are not on this card.
- **"Daisy-chained"** is the sheet's word for several units on one circuit; it does not describe plugging one machine into another, and the sheet shows no such lead.
- **The photographs** show an ordinary duplex 5-15R, the T-slot 5-20R, and a straight-blade 5-15P. A 5-15P fits either receptacle.
- **Treadmills are the other way round**: a dedicated circuit each, an isolated ground and a 5-20P (`spirit-commercial-safety-dedicated-circuit-and-isolated-ground-defined`, `spirit-commercial-safety-treadmill-plug-nema-5-20p-into-a-5-20r-t-slot`, `spirit-commercial-safety-treadmill-circuit-120-volt-20-amp-or-15-amp`).

## This contradicts every manual that prints an outlet requirement

**The owner's and service manuals of these machines all say the machine must be alone on its circuit.** The CE850 and CE800ENT books ask for a 110-volt, 5-amp outlet `with only the elliptical plugged into the circuit` and a *dedicated* 5 amp breaker (`spirit-ce850-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`); the XS895 books say `with only the Incline stepper plugged into the circuit` (`spirit-xs895-safety-outlet-120-volt-15-amp`); the CS800-2016, CRS800S-2021 and CRW800-2016 service manuals say `with only the climber` or `elliptical` `plugged into the circuit` (`spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-120-volt-15-amp`). The sheet, written by Spirit Fitness for U.S. markets, says up to four such machines may share one 15 amp circuit. Neither document mentions the other. Quote both and say which is which; the manual is the one packed with the machine.

**The CE900ENT and CU900ENT have only this sheet.** Their manuals print no figure of any kind (`spirit-xe-safety-no-outlet-figure-printed`), so for those two the 5-15P, the 15 amp circuit and the four-unit rule are the whole of what Spirit has written.

**A shared circuit changes the breaker question.** Four machines on one 15 amp circuit that trips is not the high-inrush single-machine case the manuals describe (`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`); the sheet gives no guidance on a tripping shared circuit.

**Grounding is unchanged**: a 5-15P is a grounded plug, and the grounding pages still apply (`spirit-product-must-be-grounded`).
