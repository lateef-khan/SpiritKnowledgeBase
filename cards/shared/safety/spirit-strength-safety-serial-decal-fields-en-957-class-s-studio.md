The **Serial Number Decal** on the thirty-four CSS, CSD and CSF machines carries seven lines, in this
order:

```
MODEL NUMBER:   <the SP- drawing number, e.g. SP-4301>
PRODUCT     :   <the machine's name, e.g. SEATED CHEST PRESS>
COMPLIES WITH EN 957-1/2                                   CE
CLASS: S STUDIO
MAX USER WEIGHT : <the figure>
SERIAL NUMBER:  <blank on the drawing, filled on the machine>
                                                  MADE IN CHINA
```

Beside it sits a separate black barcode label headed `SERIAL NUMBER`, showing the serial as digits
under the barcode and the Spirit service number **(870) 935-1107**.

Two of these lines get asked about:

- **`COMPLIES WITH EN 957-1/2`** — the European standard for stationary training equipment, parts 1
  (general requirements) and 2 (strength training equipment). The CSS books also print the CE mark
  and a read-the-manual pictogram beside it.
- **`CLASS: S STUDIO`** — EN 957 class S, studio/commercial use, as against class H for home use.
  This is what makes these machines commercially rated, and it is the answer to "is this a light
  commercial machine".

The **MODEL NUMBER** on the decal is Spirit's internal SP- drawing number, not the CSS/CSD/CSF
catalogue code the manual's cover uses. CSS-SCPR's decal says `SP-4301`; CSF-HRAC's says
`SP-4202-P` with the product spelled out as `Half Power Rack with Platform`; CSF-LEGP's says
`SP-4216`, CSF-AABB's `SP-4218`, CSF-BEXT's `SP-4220`. Expect the sample serial on the drawing to
read like `SP-42181906001` or `1234 5678 9876` — those are placeholders.

**On eight machines the decal's metric weight figure contradicts the manual.** See the card on that.

The two CSI machines use a different label entirely and carry no EN 957 or Class S Studio line in
this form.
