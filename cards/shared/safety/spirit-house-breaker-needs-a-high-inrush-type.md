> Circuit Breakers: Some circuit breakers used in homes are not rated for high inrush currents that
> can occur when a treadmill is first turned on or even during use. If your treadmill is tripping
> the house circuit breaker (even though it is the proper current rating) but the circuit breaker
> on the treadmill itself does not trip, you will need to replace the home breaker with a high
> inrush type. This is not a warranty defect. This is a condition we as a manufacture have no
> ability to control. This part is available through most electrical supply stores.
> Examples:Grainger part # 1D237, or available online at www.squared.com part # QO120HM.

- The test is **which breaker trips**: the house one trips, the machine's own does not.
- The fix is a **high inrush type** house breaker, even when the existing one is the right rating.
- **Not a warranty defect.** The manual says so in those words.
- Parts named: **Grainger # 1D237**, or www.squared.com **# QO120HM**.

All three manuals print this paragraph, the CVC800 with the word `climber` in place of
`treadmill`.

The same answer is printed a second time as a row of each treadmill's diagnosis guide:
`ct850-2016-house-breaker-trips-not-treadmill-breaker`. If the breaker **on the machine** is the one
that trips, that is a different fault with a different cause:
`ct850-2016-trips-onboard-10-amp-circuit` and `ct850-2020-trips-onboard-20-amp-circuit`.

**The 2026 Spirit 4.0T owner's manual prints the same paragraph, with two changes.**

> Circuit Breakers: Some circuit breakers are not rated for high inrush currents that can occur
> when a treadmill is first turned on or even during use. If your treadmill is tripping the
> circuit breaker (even though it is the proper current rating) but the circuit breaker on the
> treadmill itself does not trip, you will need to replace the facility breaker with a high
> inrush type. This is not a warranty defect. This is a condition we as a manufacturer have no
> ability to control. This part is available through most electrical supply stores.

- It says **facility breaker**, not `home breaker`, and drops `used in homes` - this is a
  commercial-capable machine.
- **It names no part numbers.** Grainger # 1D237 and QO120HM are the CT850 and CVC800 manuals'
  examples. Do not quote them as the 4.0T manual's, though the requirement they satisfy - a high
  inrush breaker of the proper current rating - is the same.

Everything that decides the answer is unchanged: the test is which breaker trips, the fix is a
high inrush type, and it is not a warranty defect.

**The 2026 7.0T and 8.0T owner's manuals print no circuit-breaker paragraph.** Their manuals are
silent on a tripping supply breaker.
