**120 volt, 15 amp, in both manuals - but each states it in a different place.**

The **CT800 2020** manual states it on its electrical and grounding pages:

> The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 15 amp circuit breaker.

> This product is for use on a nominal 120-volt/15 amp dedicated circuit and has a grounding plug.

The **CT800ENT-2022** manual states it as the installer's requirement, in the WARNING header of its
safety instructions:

> To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the treadmill on
> a flat level surface with access to a 120-volt AC, 15-Amp. grounded outlet. With only the
> treadmill plugged into the circuit.

and repeats the figure on its grounding page as `a nominal 120-volt/15 amp dedicated circuit`.

- **120 volt, 15 amp, dedicated, grounded**, in both.
- **Only the treadmill on that circuit** - the ENT manual says so in words; the 2020 manual says
  only `dedicated`.
- **A flat, level surface** - the ENT manual only.
- Neither manual prints a NEMA type.

**The ENT manual drops the breaker sentence.** Its circuit-breaker paragraph ends at
`available through most electrical supply stores` and prints neither
`The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 15 amp circuit breaker` nor the Grainger and
QO120HM examples the other three CT800 manuals give
(`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`). Its 15-amp figure comes from the safety and
grounding pages quoted above.

**The 2012 and 2016 manuals print 110 volt in the same sentence.** Same 15 amps, different nominal
figure: `spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-110-volt-15-amp`. Nothing in any of the four manuals reconciles
the two; quote the figure from the machine's own manual.

**Other Spirit machines need a different circuit.** The commercial CT850 asks for 20 amps
(`spirit-ct850-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`); the CVC800 climber asks for the same 120 volt, 15
amp but is a different product line (`cvc800-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`).

The ENT manual's extension-cord rule is `ct850-2020-extension-cord-14-awg-or-better`; the 2020
manual prints no extension-cord requirement at all.

## The ENT manual contradicts itself, and the 2020 manual does not

**The CT800ENT-2022 manual's troubleshooting section says 110 volt.** Its row for a treadmill that
only reaches about 12 mph closes `A minimum of 110-volt AC current is required`, while its safety
and grounding pages both say 120. The manual never reconciles the two. The safety chapter's figure
is the 120-volt one quoted above; treat the troubleshooting row's 110 as the minimum that row
states and quote 120 as the requirement.

**The CT800-2020 manual is consistent.** The same troubleshooting row in it reads
`A minimum of 120 volt AC current is required`, matching its safety pages. The 2012 and 2016
manuals print 110 in both places, matching theirs.
