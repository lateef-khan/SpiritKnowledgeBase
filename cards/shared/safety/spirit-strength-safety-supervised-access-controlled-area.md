Every one of the thirty-nine Spirit strength owner's manuals prints this paragraph word for word,
under **SAFETY GUIDELINES AND PRACTICE** (**SAFETY GUIDELINES AND PRACTICES** in the three ST800
books). It is the closest thing the range has to a policy on who may use a machine.

> Spirit Fitness recommends that all fitness equipment be used in a supervised area. It is
> recommended that the equipment be located in an access controlled area. Control is the
> responsibility of the owner. The extent of control is at the discretion of the owner.
>
> It is the responsibility of the purchaser/user of Spirit Fitness products to read and understand
> the owner's manual, and warning labels; as well as instruct all individuals, whether end users or
> supervising personnel, on proper usage of the equipment.

Three things follow from it, and customers ask about all three:

- **Supervision is recommended, not required.** Spirit does not set a staffing rule and does not
  say what "supervised" means. It hands both to the owner.
- **The owner instructs the users.** Training end users *and* supervising personnel on proper use
  is the purchaser's job, not Spirit's and not the dealer's.
- **The owner's duty covers the warning labels too**, which is why a missing or unreadable decal
  has to be replaced rather than ignored.

The same paragraph continues into five placement and handling bullets: use the machine only as the
manual describes; do not lean on or pull the frame whether the machine is at rest or in use;
improper use can injure bystanders as well as the user; do not use a machine that is not on a solid
level surface or is improperly installed; and provide an adequate safety perimeter between the
machine, the walls and other equipment. The thirty-six CSS, CSD, CSF and CSI books then put a
figure on that perimeter; the three ST800 books do not.

The CSI name plate decal carries a shorter form of the same rule as printed text on the machine:
*WARNING - Use the stationary training equipment in a supervised environment.*
