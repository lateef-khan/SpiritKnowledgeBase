**Both air bike manuals print the same warning in capitals:**

> **WARNING: DO NOT DISMOUNT THE BIKE OR REMOVE YOUR FEET FROM THE PEDALS UNTIL BOTH THE PEDALS AND
> THE MOVEMENT ARMS HAVE STOPPED COMPLETELY.** Failure to comply may lead to loss of control and
> serious personal injury.

The AB900 writes `MOVEMENT ARMS` here and `the moving arms` in its SAFEGUARDS list
(`spirit-air-bike-safety-safeguards-list`). Same part, same rule.

## The part people get caught by

**The pedals keep turning when the machine is driven by the arms alone.** The AB950 spells this out
where the AB900 does not:

> NOTE: that the pedals will continue to rotate when the bike is used with the movement arms only.
> Be certain to maintain feet on the front foot platforms when exercising with the movement arms
> only so that feet stay clear of the rotating pedals.

So a rider doing an arms-only interval must have both feet planted on the **front foot platforms**,
not resting near the cranks. The AB900's SAFEGUARDS page states the same requirement as a placement
rule - feet go on the pedals or on the stationary foot platforms at the sides of the flywheel cage,
and nowhere else, and legs must not be extended sideways into the path of the pedals and movable
arms while the bike is in motion.

**Neither manual gives a way to brake the machine.** There is no knob, no handle and no emergency
brake on either air bike; the fan simply slows. The only instruction is to wait.

**This is not the indoor cycle answer.** The CB900, CIC800, CIC850, XIC600 and JB950 are fixed-gear
flywheel machines with a brake control, and their warning is about the flywheel rather than the
movement arms - `spirit-indoor-cycle-safety-fixed-gear-dismount`.
