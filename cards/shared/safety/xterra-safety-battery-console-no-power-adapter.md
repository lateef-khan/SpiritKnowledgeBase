**These thirteen machines have no power adapter, no power cord and no outlet requirement.** Their
books name no voltage, no plug and no jack; the only electrical part is the console, and every book
tells the owner to fit or replace its batteries. A customer asking where to plug one in should be
told there is nothing to plug in.

| Machine | What the book says about power |
|---|---|
| FB150, FB350, FB160, FB360, FB180 | `If the display is faint or shows no figures, please replace the batteries.`; the console table on the FB160/FB360/FB180 gives the battery type |
| AIR350, AIR650 | `Place 2 AAA batteries into the Console` (AIR350 assembly step 1); `The console operates on 4*AA batteries (not included)` (AIR650) |
| MB500, MB550 | `Load the transmitter with batteries`; `User Changeable Batteries (Sensor and Receiver)` |
| MBX2500 | `Install one CR2032 3V battery` |
| SB120, UB120 | console table `BATTERY TYPE 2 AAA or UM 4`; troubleshooting `The batteries in the computer may be dead` |
| EU100 | `Computer requires two "AA" batteries` |

Which battery, how many and how to change them are console facts and live on the console cards;
this card records only that the machine is unpowered.

**The safety page still reads like an appliance's.** The folding bikes and the EU100 print `Never
operate the appliance with the air openings blocked` and `To reduce the risk of burns, fire electric
shock`, and the FB180 warns about *power requirements*; those lines are the Dyaco template with the
cord items cut out (`xterra-safety-instructions-list-8-item-appliance-list`). The AIR, MB, SB120 and
UB120 books use the indoor-cycle list, which has no electrical item at all.

How it was checked: every page of each book read; the text searched for `adapter`, `adaptor`,
`plug`, `cord`, `outlet`, `volt` and `battery`; the only hits are the battery sentences above, the
warranty's boilerplate `Proper connection to a power supply of sufficient voltage` (printed in every
Xterra warranty, powered or not) and the folding bikes' `Insert the computer wire`. The AIR650's
service manual is the Spirit AB900's by the manufacturer's note; that book is about the AB900 and is
not evidence about the AIR650's batteries - the AIR650 owner's manual says four AA.

The seventeen plug-in machines of the same range are listed on
`xterra-safety-external-power-supply-and-power-jack`. The Spirit CIC indoor cycles print the same
finding for their own machines (`spirit-indoor-cycle-safety-no-mains-outlet-or-voltage`).
