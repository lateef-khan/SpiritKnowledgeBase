**Yes - and the manuals say it should always be used.**

> A safety tether cord is provided with this unit. It is a simple switch design that should always be
> used. It is for your safety should you fall from the StairClimber when the Steps Per Minute (SPM)
> exceeds your capability. Pulling this safety tether cord and activating the switch will stop the
> stairs movement.

**To use it:**

> Fasten the plastic clip onto your clothing securely to assure good holding power. NOTE: the switch
> has a strong enough power to minimize accidental, unexpected stopping. The clip should be attached
> securely to make certain it does not come off. Be familiar with its function and limitations. The
> StairClimber will stop, depending on the level within **one to two steps** anytime the switch is
> activated.

> If for any reason you feel unstable on the rotating staircase, immediately press the START/STOP
> button or pull on the safety switch to stop the staircase and step off onto the side support steps.

- **One to two steps**, and the manuals say the distance depends on the level. This is not an
  instant stop; the staircase is still moving after the cord is pulled.
- **Two ways to stop**, and the manuals give them as alternatives - the START/STOP button or the
  cord.
- **Step off onto the side support steps**, not off the moving staircase.

**The CSC880 adds a second stop the CSC900 does not have.** Its parts diagram names a `Safety Key`
and a `Safety-Stop Sensor`, and its quick-start page says:

> There are Safety-Stop Sensors on each panel of the stair climber. If the beam is broken, the
> machine will stop.

That is a light beam across the staircase, independent of the tether cord. The CSC900 manuals name no
such sensor.

**Do not tell a CSC900-2024 owner any of this.** The 2024 printing dropped the tether-cord paragraph
entirely and names the STOP button as the only stop
(`csc900-2024-safety-moving-staircase-and-stop-button`). Ask which year the machine is.
