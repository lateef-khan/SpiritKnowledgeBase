**This is the service-manual rule, and it is stricter than the owner's manual for the same
machines.** Five Dyaco service books, covering six treadmills, print it word for word:

> Never use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any
> appliance with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often. Route the power cord away from any moving
> part of the treadmill including the elevation mechanism and transport wheels.

**The owner's manual of every one of these machines says something else**: `Avoid AFCI/GFCI circuit
breakers if possible`, then names three breaker brands that did not trip in testing
(`xterra-treadmill-safety-avoid-afci-gfci-breakers-tested-brands`).

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Owner's manual (all 19 Xterra treadmills) | avoid if possible; Eaton, Leviton and Schneider breakers tested and passed with nothing else on the circuit |
| Service manual (TR150, TR260, TRX1400, TRX2500, TRX3500, TRX4500) | never |

The service books are Dyaco documents dated 2018 to 2023 and reuse a page older than the owner's
manuals; the owner's manual is what the customer holds. Give the owner's manual rule and say the
service book is absolute.

**The TRX5500 service manual has no safety chapter at all**, so for that machine only the owner's
manual speaks (`trx5500-2024-safety-service-manual-has-no-safety-chapter`).

The same absolute sentence is the Spirit CT800 rule (`spirit-never-use-a-gfci-wall-outlet`); that card
covers Spirit machines only.
