**Both manuals state it twice, on the same chapter's two pages, and the two agree.**

The Important Electrical Instructions page, closing the circuit-breaker paragraph:

> The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 15 amp circuit breaker.

The Grounding Instructions page, describing the circuit the product is built for:

> This product is for use on a nominal 110-volt/15 amp dedicated circuit, and has a grounding plug
> that looks like the plug illustrated below.

- **110 volt, 15 amp, dedicated, grounded.**
- **A dedicated circuit** - the manual asks for the breaker to serve this outlet.
- Neither manual prints a NEMA type for the plug or the receptacle.

**Neither manual prints a `flat level surface` installation sentence** and neither says in words
that nothing else may share the circuit; `dedicated` is the only word they use for it. The CT850,
CVC800 and XT-2015 manuals say `with only the treadmill plugged into the circuit` outright - these
two do not, so do not quote that phrase for a CT800-2012 or CT800-2016.

**The 2020 and ENT-2022 manuals raise the voltage to 120.** Same 15 amps, different nominal figure:
`spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-120-volt-15-amp`. Nothing in any of the four manuals reconciles 110
with 120; quote the figure from the machine's own manual.

**Other Spirit machines need a different circuit.** The commercial CT850 asks for 120 volt, 20 amp
(`spirit-ct850-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`) and the CVC800 climber for 120 volt, 15 amp
(`cvc800-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`). Do not carry those onto a CT800.

The rest of that chapter is `spirit-product-must-be-grounded`,
`spirit-temporary-adapter-for-a-two-pole-receptacle`, `spirit-never-use-a-gfci-wall-outlet` and
`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type` - which is where the Grainger and QO120HM breaker
examples are.
