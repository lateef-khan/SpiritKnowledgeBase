Both CT850 service manuals print the requirement in the same words:

> To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the treadmill on
> a flat level surface with access to a 120-volt, 20-amp grounded outlet with only the treadmill
> plugged into the circuit.

- **120 volt, 20 amp, grounded.**
- **Only the treadmill on that circuit.** Nothing else may share it.
- **A flat, level surface.**

This is the figure as a requirement the installer must meet. The same voltage also appears as a
number in the machine's own electrical description (CT850 2016 p. 11, printed 10:
`Work voltage:AC 120V~`), which is a specification, not this requirement.

**The 2016 manual does not print one voltage.** Its cover says `(110V)`, and the body prints
`~230VAC`, `200-240V`, `110-120V`, `110 volt AC current, 60 hz` and `AC 120V system` in different
places. The safety chapter's own figure is the 120-volt one quoted above. The full list of what
the manual says where is in `ct850-2016-mains-voltage-figures-disagree`. The CT850 2020 manual
prints 120 V consistently.

**The 20-amp figure disagrees with the diagnosis guide in the same 2016 manual**, which has the row
`Treadmill trips on board 10 amp circuit` (p. 56, printed 55). That row is about the breaker on the
treadmill, not the supply circuit, and the manual never reconciles the two numbers; see
`ct850-2016-trips-onboard-10-amp-circuit`. The 2020 manual prints the same row as a 20 amp circuit:
`ct850-2020-trips-onboard-20-amp-circuit`.

**The CVC800 climber asks for 15 amps, not 20**: `cvc800-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`.

## The owner's manuals state the same 20 amps, in two more places each

**The CT850 2016 and 2020 owner's manuals print the figure twice**, and both times it is 20 amps.
Closing the circuit-breaker paragraph of their Important Electrical Instructions page:

> The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 20 amp circuit breaker.

and on their Grounding Instructions page:

> This product is for use on a nominal 120-volt/20 amp dedicated circuit, and has a grounding plug
> that looks like the plug illustrated below.

Neither owner's manual prints the service manuals' `flat level surface` sentence or the words
`with only the treadmill plugged into the circuit`; `dedicated` is the only word they use for it.

**The CT850ENT-2022 owner's manual states it as the installer's requirement**, in the WARNING
header of its safety instructions, and is the only manual of the family that names a plug type:

> WARNING - To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the
> treadmill on a flat level surface with access to a 120 volt AC (NEMA 5-20P electrical cord) 20
> amp grounded outlet. With only the treadmill plugged into the circuit.

Its grounding page repeats `a nominal 120-volt/20 amp dedicated circuit`. **NEMA 5-20P is printed
only by the ENT-2022 manual**; the 2016 and 2020 manuals print no NEMA type for either the plug or
the receptacle, so do not quote it for them. The ENT manual drops the dedicated-breaker sentence
and the Grainger and QO120HM examples the other three print
(`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`).

## The CT850 2018 owner's manual asks for a different circuit

**It prints 110 volt and 15 amp, in both places**, where the 2016, 2020 and ENT-2022 manuals print
120 volt and 20 amp for the same commercial treadmill. That figure has its own card, because it is
the CT800 family's figure and not this one:
`spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-110-volt-15-amp`. **Nothing in any CT850 manual reconciles the two.**
Quote the figure from the machine's own manual, and do not fit a CT850-2018 to a 20-amp circuit or
a CT850-2016, CT850-2020 or CT850ENT-2022 to a 15-amp one on the strength of the other book.

## The CT850-2013 owner's manual prints the same 20 amps, in the same two places

**`The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 20 amp circuit breaker`** closes its
circuit-breaker paragraph, and its grounding page reads **`This product is for use on a 120-volt/20
amp dedicated circuit`** - the 2016 and 2020 sentence without the word `nominal`. Its drawing shows
a 20-amp T-slot receptacle. Like the 2016 and 2020 owner's manuals it prints no `flat level surface`
sentence, no `only the treadmill plugged into the circuit`, no NEMA type and **no extension-cord
gauge anywhere** - the appliance list it prints has no cord line
(`spirit-ct800-safety-instructions-list`). The 2018 book's 110-volt/15 amp figure is not in it.
