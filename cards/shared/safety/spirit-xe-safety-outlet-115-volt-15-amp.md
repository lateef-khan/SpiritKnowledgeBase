**115 volt, 15 amp, grounded, nothing else on the circuit, and a dedicated 15 amp breaker.** All
four manuals print the same WARNING at the head of their safety instructions:

> WARNING - To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the
> elliptical on a flat level surface with access to a 115-volt, 15-amp grounded outlet with only the
> elliptical plugged into the circuit.

and all four close their circuit-breaker paragraph with the breaker rating:

> The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 15 amp circuit breaker.

| Requirement | Figure |
|---|---|
| Nominal supply | 115 volt (see the contradiction below) |
| Current | 15 amp |
| Outlet | grounded |
| Circuit | only the machine plugged into it |
| Breaker | dedicated 15 amp |
| Surface | flat and level |

**The XG400 manual prints `E-Glide` where the other three print `elliptical`.** Its cover reads
`XG400 E-Glide Trainer`; the requirement is the same.

## The same manuals say 110 volt two pages later

**The XE395ENT and XG400 grounding pages name a different nominal voltage for the same circuit:**

> This product is for use on a nominal 110-volt/15 amp dedicated circuit, and has a grounding plug
> that looks like the plug illustrated below.

**115 on the safety page, 110 on the grounding page, in one book, and nothing in either manual
reconciles them.** The amp figure agrees at 15 in both places; only the nominal voltage differs.
Quote both sentences to a customer rather than picking one - a nominal 110-volt and a nominal
115-volt circuit are the same domestic supply described two ways, and neither manual says which
figure it means.

**The XE195 and XE295 manuals have no grounding page at all**, so 115 volt is their only statement.
Neither prints the `nominal 110-volt/15 amp` sentence, and neither prints the `This product must be
grounded` paragraph or the temporary-adapter rule (`spirit-product-must-be-grounded`,
`spirit-temporary-adapter-for-a-two-pole-receptacle`). Their only grounding sentence is the safety
list bullet `Connect this appliance to a properly grounded outlet only`.

**No manual prints a NEMA type and none prints a frequency.**

## This is not the commercial CE figure and it is not a treadmill figure

**The Spirit CE850 and CE800ENT commercial ellipticals ask for a nominal 110-volt, 5-amp circuit on
a dedicated 5 amp breaker** (`spirit-ce850-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`). These four
residential machines ask for **15 amp**, three times that. Never answer an XE owner from the CE850
card or a CE owner from this one.

**No Spirit treadmill card answers for these machines either**, and this one answers for no
treadmill. The CT800 family is 110 or 120 volt at 15 amp, the CT850 is 120 volt at 20 amp, the MT200
is 115 volt at 20 amp and the 2026 machines need a NEMA 5-20P on a 120-volt, 20-amp circuit. **115
volt at 15 amp is printed by no Spirit treadmill manual in this repository.**

## The rest of the residential range

| Machine | Outlet requirement |
|---|---|
| **XE195, XE295, XG400, XE395ENT (2021)** | **115 volt, 15 amp, dedicated 15 amp breaker** |
| XE395-2010, XE795-2021 | 110 volt, 15 amp, no breaker rating (`spirit-xe-safety-outlet-110-volt-15-amp`) |
| XE100, XE200, XE300, XE150, XE350, XE550, XE395-2023, XE795-2023 | none printed (`spirit-xe-safety-no-outlet-figure-printed`) |

A house breaker that trips while the machine's own does not is a different problem with a different
answer, and the Grainger and QO120HM examples in these four manuals are carded there:
`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`. The extension cord these four allow is 18AWG or
better with one outlet on the end (`xt385-2015-safety-extension-cord-18-awg-or-better`).

## A Sole elliptical prints the same two figures, and is a different machine

**The Sole E95-2026 owner's manual asks for a 115 volt, 15 amp grounded outlet too**
(`e95-2026-power-outlet-requirements`), and the Sole E25-2023 asks for 120 volt at 15 amp
(`e25-2023-electrical-power-requirements`). **Those are Sole cards and this is a Spirit card.**
Matching figures do not make them one fact: check the brand on the machine in front of you before
quoting either, because the rest of each manual's electrical chapter - extension-cord gauge,
breaker advice, grounding page - does not match.
