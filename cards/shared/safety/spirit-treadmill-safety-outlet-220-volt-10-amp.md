**This is the 10-amp figure the service manuals print for a 230-volt supply. The same sentence gives
the 120-volt-system figure beside it, and that one is 110 volt at 15 amp on every XT book but the
XT485ENT, whose 120-volt figure is 120 volt at 15 amp.** The 120-volt figures have their own cards:
`xt-2015-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement` and `spirit-ct800-safety-outlet-120-volt-15-amp`.

The XT385-2015, XT485-2015, the five 2023 XT and the XT685ENT service manuals print, in their
Important Safety Instructions:

> To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the treadmill on a
> flat level surface with access to a 220-volt, 10-amp grounded outlet with only the treadmill
> plugged into the circuit. 【120VAC electronic power system is 110-volt, 15-amp】

- **220 volt, 10 amp, grounded**, on the 230-volt build.
- **Only the treadmill on that circuit**, and **a flat, level surface**.

**The books do not all spell the figure the same way.**

| Service manual | Sentence as printed |
|---|---|
| CT900 | `a 220-volt, 10-amp grounded outlet` - the only figure, with the `220` printed in red |
| XT185-2015, XT285-2015 | `a 230-volt, 10-amp (110-volt, 15A) grounded outlet` |
| XT385-2015, XT485-2015 | `a 220-volt, 10-amp grounded outlet ... 【120Vac electronic power system is 110-volt, 15-amp】` |
| XT185, XT285, XT385, XT485, XT685 2023; XT685ENT | `a 220-volt, 10-amp grounded outlet ... 【120VAC electronic power system is 110-volt, 15-amp】` |
| XT485ENT | reversed: `a 120-volt, 15-amp grounded outlet ... 【220Vac electronic power system is 220-volt, 10-amp】` |

230 volt and 220 volt name the same build: the XT185 and XT285 books of both years open with a
`Special Note on ... CEGS version` saying `the power input is 110AC for normal version versus 230VAC
for CEGS version and an additional Filter Choke circuit is added for CEGS version`. The 10 amp does
not change between the two spellings.

**The CT900 service manual prints this figure as the machine's only one, and then contradicts it.**
Its grounding paragraph on the same page reads `This product is for use on a nominal 120-volt
circuit`, its owner's manual says `a nominal 110-volt circuit` (`ct900-grounding-instructions`),
and its two troubleshooting checklists ask for a `220 VAC wall outlet` with `a minimum of 210 volt
AC current` on one page and a `110 VAC wall outlet` with `a minimum of 110 volt AC current, 60 hz`
on another. Nothing in either CT900 book reconciles these. The `220` on the safety page is set in
red where every other figure on the page is black - it reads as a hand-edited number - so quote it
with the page it is on and say what the other pages print.

**The XT owner's manuals never print a 220-volt or 230-volt figure.** The 2015 owner's manuals ask
for 110 volt at 15 amp and the 2023 owner's manuals print no outlet requirement at all. So a customer
with a CE/CEGS machine has this figure only from the service manual.

**The grounding page of these same service manuals names a nominal 220-volt circuit** `(on 120Vac
electronic power system need 110Vac)` - except the XT185-2015 and XT285-2015 books, which say
`nominal 110-volt` only, and the XT485ENT, which says `nominal 120-volt (on 220Vac electronic power
system need 220Vac)`: `spirit-product-must-be-grounded`. The extension cord these pages ask for is
16 AWG (`spirit-extension-cord-16-awg-or-better`).

**Sole prints the same figure for the same Dyaco chassis.** The Sole TT8-2019 service manual asks for
a 220-volt, 10-amp outlet, the F60-2020 for 230-volt, 10-amp, and the F63-2026 for `230-volt, 10-amp
(110-volt, 15A)` - the XT185-2015 spelling exactly (`tt8-2019-electrical-power-requirements`,
`f60-2020-power-outlet-requirements`, `f63-2026-power-outlet-requirements`). Those are Sole
machines with their own cards; this card is the Spirit one.

## One XT owner's manual does print the figure: the Australian XT485-2013

**The statement above that the XT owner's manuals never print a 220-volt or 230-volt figure is true
of the North American books.** The 2013 XT485 owner's manual is an Australian edition (its dealer
letter is signed BOYLES FITNESS Equipment Pty Ltd.) and its safety page asks, as the installer's
requirement:

> install the treadmill on a flat level surface with access to a 230-volt, 10-amp grounded outlet
> with only the treadmill plugged into the circuit.

Its grounding page says `This product is for use on a nominal 230-volt circuit`
(`spirit-product-must-be-grounded`), and its cord rule is metric - `1m㎡ OR BETTER, WITH ONLY ONE
OUTLET ON THE END` (`40t-2026-safety-extension-cord-1-mm2-or-better`). No 110-volt or 120-volt figure
appears anywhere in the book. 230 and 220 name the same build, as the table above shows; 10 amp is
the figure in both spellings (`xt485-2013-safety-instructions-list`).
