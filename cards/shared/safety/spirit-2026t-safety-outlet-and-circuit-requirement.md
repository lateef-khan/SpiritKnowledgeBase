All three 2026 manuals - 4.0T, 7.0T and 8.0T - print the same bullet on their Electrical Safety
page, word for word:

> Power: 120 volts, 60 Hz, dedicated 20-amp circuit (NEMA 5-20P standard power plug/ receptacle
> type)

| What | Figure |
|---|---|
| Voltage | 120 volts |
| Frequency | 60 Hz |
| Circuit | dedicated, 20 amp |
| Plug and receptacle | NEMA 5-20P |

**NEMA 5-20P is the plug on the cord.** It is the 20-amp grounded plug whose neutral blade is
turned sideways, so it will not enter an ordinary 15-amp 5-15R socket. The manual prints the
label `NEMA 5-20P` beside a picture of the plug on the same page. This is the first Spirit
treadmill family whose manual names a NEMA type at all - the CT850, CVC800 and XT manuals print
none, so there is no earlier Spirit figure to compare it against.

**Dedicated means nothing else on that circuit.** The safety instructions page states the same
requirement as the job the installer must do, and says so:

> To reduce the risk of burns, fire, electric shock, or injury to persons, install the 7.0T on a
> flat level surface with access to a 120-volt AC, 60 Hz, 20-amp grounded outlet. [...] The 7.0T
> should be the only appliance in the electrical circuit.

The **4.0T** prints that same sentence with the frequency left out - `access to a 120-volt,
20-amp grounded outlet` - and `The treadmill should be the only appliance in the circuit in which
it is connected`. The 60 Hz figure is on its Electrical Safety page above, so nothing is missing;
the two pages agree.

**These figures are not the other Spirit families' figures.** The CT850 asks for 120 volt, 20 amp
with no plug type (`spirit-ct850-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`), the CVC800 climber for 120
volt, **15 amp** (`cvc800-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`), and the 2015 XT treadmills for **110
volt, 15 amp** (`xt-2015-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`). Do not carry a NEMA 5-20P
answer onto any of those, and do not answer a 2026 machine with their amp figures.

**The motor is not the same on all three**, even though the supply is. The 4.0T names a 4.0HP AC
motor on this page and the 7.0T and 8.0T both name a 3.0HP AC motor; that is a specification, not
this requirement.

**The 2025 printing of the 4.0T manual prints this page word for word**, so it holds for the 40t-2025 as well. The February 2025 book (revision 02.04.25) and its May 2025 reprint (revision 05.19.25) differ only in the version and revision line; both were compared with the 2026 book page by page and no rule or figure on the safety, electrical, grounding or operation pages changed.

**The 2025 printing of the 7.0T manual is not the same.** Its safety item 2 asks for a 115-volt
outlet and its electrical page names NEMA 5-15P beside a NEMA 5-20P drawing
(`70t-2025-safety-outlet-115-volt-20-amp-and-a-nema-5-15p-line`); do not add the 70t-2025 to this
card.

**The 4.0T service manuals give the same circuit without the plug.** Both the ST8700-ST017 book and
its ST8700A-ST026-01 revision print the CT850 chapter's sentence - `install the treadmill on a flat
level surface with access to a 120-volt, 20-amp grounded outlet with only the treadmill plugged into
the circuit` - and no NEMA type; the 5-20P is the owner's manual's statement alone.
