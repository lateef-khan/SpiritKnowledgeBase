Three of the numbered safety instructions are about the weight stack, and they are the hazard a
strength machine has that no treadmill or bike has. Thirty-six CSS, CSD, CSF and CSI manuals print
them as items 12, 14 and 16; the ST800FT functional trainer prints the same three as bullets.

> 12. Be sure that the weight pin is completely inserted. Use only the pin provided by the
>     manufacturer.
>
> 14. Never pin the weights in an elevated position. DO NOT use this unit if found in this
>     condition.
>
> 16. DO NOT use dumbbells or other incremental weights, except those provided by the manufacturer.

What each one is guarding against:

- **A half-inserted pin** can slip out under load and drop the stack. The pin must be *completely*
  inserted, and it must be the pin Spirit supplied — not a bolt, not a longer pin from another
  machine.
- **A stack pinned in an elevated position** leaves plates hanging above the guide rods with nothing
  under them. The manual does not say to fix this yourself: it says do not use the machine. Items 10
  and 15 send you to floor staff.
- **Loose plates or dumbbells added to the stack** exceed what the frame, cable and stops were built
  for. Only Spirit's own incremental weights are allowed.

The eight-item warning decal on the machine repeats the pin rule in its own words —
*weight selector pin must be fully inserted into the weight plate* — but the decal is reproduced in
these PDFs at a resolution too low to read reliably, so quote the manual, not the picture of the
decal.

The ST800FI flat/incline bench and the ST800DR3 dumbbell rack have no weight stack and print none of
these three rules.

**The CSD-CPSP May 2025 update (version 5.0, revision 01/23/2025, file dated 2025 0521) and the CSS-DELT 2026 revision (file dated 20260821) print this page in the same words**, so the two newer books add nothing and change nothing here; they are cited so the page can be checked in either printing.
