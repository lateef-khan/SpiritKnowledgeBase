> Never use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any
> appliance with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often.

All three manuals print this sentence, the CVC800 with the word `climber` in place of `treadmill`.

The instruction is absolute: the manual gives no exception and no alternative GFCI rating. The
reason it gives is nuisance tripping from a large motor, not a fault in the machine.

A breaker that trips while the machine's own breaker does not is a different problem with a
different answer: `spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`.

**The 2026 Spirit 4.0T owner's manual prints the same prohibition**, and names the device by its
international name first:

> Never use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S ver. = GFCI, a ground fault circuit interrupter)
> wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will
> trip often.

Same rule, same reason, and still no exception and no alternative rating. **RCD and GFCI are the
same device** - the 4.0T manual says so itself, so a customer who asks about an RCD is asking
about this card.

**The 2026 7.0T and 8.0T owner's manuals do not print this sentence anywhere.** Their Electrical
Safety pages begin with the cover-removal warning instead. Do not tell a 7.0T or 8.0T owner that
their manual forbids a GFCI outlet; it is silent.

**The 2023 XT manuals take a different line again** - they describe arc-fault and ground-fault
breakers as prone to nuisance tripping and point at built-in surge suppressors, rather than
forbidding the outlet: `xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`.

**All four Spirit CT800 owner's manuals print this prohibition**, the 2012, 2016 and 2020 manuals
word for word in the CT850's wording:

> NEVER use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any
> appliance with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often.

**The CT800ENT-2022 manual names the device by its international name first**, as the 2026 4.0T
manual does:

> NEVER use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S. ver.= GFCI) - wall outlet with this treadmill. As
> with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will trip often.

Same rule and same reason in all four, with no exception and no alternative GFCI rating. **RCD and
GFCI are the same device** - the ENT manual says so itself.
