> Never use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any
> appliance with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often.

All three manuals print this sentence, the CVC800 with the word `climber` in place of `treadmill`.

The instruction is absolute: the manual gives no exception and no alternative GFCI rating. The
reason it gives is nuisance tripping from a large motor, not a fault in the machine.

A breaker that trips while the machine's own breaker does not is a different problem with a
different answer: `spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`.

**The 2026 Spirit 4.0T owner's manual prints the same prohibition**, and names the device by its
international name first:

> Never use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S ver. = GFCI, a ground fault circuit interrupter)
> wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will
> trip often.

Same rule, same reason, and still no exception and no alternative rating. **RCD and GFCI are the
same device** - the 4.0T manual says so itself, so a customer who asks about an RCD is asking
about this card.

**The 2026 7.0T and 8.0T owner's manuals do not print this sentence anywhere.** Their Electrical
Safety pages begin with the cover-removal warning instead. Do not tell a 7.0T or 8.0T owner that
their manual forbids a GFCI outlet; it is silent.

**The 2023 XT manuals take a different line again** - they describe arc-fault and ground-fault
breakers as prone to nuisance tripping and point at built-in surge suppressors, rather than
forbidding the outlet: `xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`.

**All four Spirit CT800 owner's manuals print this prohibition**, the 2012, 2016 and 2020 manuals
word for word in the CT850's wording:

> NEVER use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill. As with any
> appliance with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often.

**The CT800ENT-2022 manual names the device by its international name first**, as the 2026 4.0T
manual does:

> NEVER use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S. ver.= GFCI) - wall outlet with this treadmill. As
> with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will trip often.

Same rule and same reason in all four, with no exception and no alternative GFCI rating. **RCD and
GFCI are the same device** - the ENT manual says so itself.

**All four Spirit CT850 owner's manuals print the prohibition as well.** The 2016, 2018 and 2020
manuals print the CT850 service manual's sentence word for word, opening `NEVER use a ground fault
circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill`. The **CT850ENT-2022** manual prints the
CT800ENT wording, naming the device by its international name first:

> NEVER use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S. ver.= GFCI) - wall outlet with this treadmill. As
> with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will trip often.

Same rule, same reason, no exception and no alternative GFCI rating in any of the four.

**The Spirit XT685-2010 owner's manual prints the prohibition word for word**, in the CT800 2012
manual's wording, opening `NEVER use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this
treadmill`. Same rule, same reason, no exception and no alternative GFCI rating.

**It is the only XT owner's manual that forbids the outlet.** Its 2015 and 2023 successors describe
arc-fault and ground-fault breakers as prone to nuisance tripping and point at built-in surge
suppressors instead (`xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`), so do not tell an XT685-2015 or
XT685-2023 owner that their manual forbids a GFCI outlet, or an XT685-2010 owner that theirs merely
warns about one.

**The Spirit CU800ENT-2024 upright bike prints the same warning**, at the head of its ELECTRICAL
SAFETY page:

> NEVER use a RCD - Residual Current Device (U.S. ver.= GFCI) - wall outlet with this Upright Bike.
> As with any appliance with a large motor, the RCD/GFCI will trip often.

**The bike has no large motor.** The reason the sentence gives is boilerplate from the treadmill
manual; the instruction itself is printed for this machine.

**It is the only Spirit commercial bike that prints it.** The CU800ENT-2022 and CR800ENT-2023 print
the high-inrush house-breaker paragraph instead (`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`),
and the other ten bikes print nothing about breakers or outlets at all.

**Four of the fourteen 2024 New Black Units print it** - the CT800ENT-2024, CT850ENT-2024,
CE800ENT-2024 and CR800ENT-2024, at the head of their ELECTRICAL SAFETY page, printed page 6, in the
same words. **The CT800-2024 and CT850-2024 dropped it**: their electrical page opens at
`Route the power cord away from any moving part` and never mentions an RCD or a GFCI wall outlet,
although both still print the AFCI/GFCI nuisance-tripping paragraph lower down
(`xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`). The other eight 2024 manuals print neither.

## Seven Spirit residential bike service manuals print the prohibition

**The XBR25-2016, XBR55-2016, XBU55-2016, XBR95-2016, XBR55-2023, XBU55-2023 and XBR95-2023 service
manuals print it word for word**:

> Never use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this Bike. As with any appliance
> with a large motor, the GFCI will trip often.

The 2016 books capitalise `Bike`, the 2023 books write `bike`; nothing else differs. **None of these
bikes has a large motor** - the XBR25, XBR55 and XBU55 set their resistance with a small tension gear
motor and the XBR95 with a generator brake - so the reason is treadmill boilerplate, as it is for the
CU800ENT-2024 above; the instruction itself is printed for these machines. The two XBR95 books print
it on a self-powered machine that never sees a wall outlet
(`spirit-bike-safety-no-mains-outlet-needed`).

**The 2023 owner's manuals give the opposite advice for the same machines.** XBR55-2023 and
XBU55-2023 owners hold a book that says avoid AFCI/GFCI breakers if possible and names breakers
that passed testing (`xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`), and a service manual that says
never. Say both, and which book each comes from.
