> **WARNING!** Be aware that the generator is producing A.C. power while the bike is being used. Do
> not service the bike while the generator is spinning; serious electric shock could occur.

> Never expose this product to rain or moisture. This product is not designed for use outdoors,
> near a pool or spa, or in any other high humidity environment. The operating temperature
> specification is 5 to 48 degrees Celsius (40 to 120 degrees Fahrenheit), and humidity is 95%
> non-condensing (no water drops forming on surfaces).

| Limit | Figure |
|---|---|
| Temperature | **5 to 48 degrees C (40 to 120 degrees F)** |
| Humidity | **95 %**, non-condensing |

- **Celsius first.** The commercial CR900 and CU900 books print `40 to 120 degrees Fahrenheit` alone
  (`ct900ent-operating-environment-voltage`); these four print the Celsius range and bracket the
  Fahrenheit, as the 4.0T treadmill does (`40t-2026-safety-operating-environment-limits`). The two
  scales do not convert exactly - 5 C is 41 F and 48 C is 118 F - and the manual does not say which
  pair governs.
- **No air pressure, altitude or storage figure**, and no voltage paragraph: there is no mains
  supply to vary (`spirit-bike-safety-no-mains-outlet-needed`).
- **The generator warning is the only electrical rule**, and it is the reason there is no
  unplug-before-service sentence: the way to make the bike safe is to stop the pedals
  (`spirit-ce-safety-generator-produces-ac-power`).

**The Dyaco PT editions print both paragraphs word for word**, headed `Important electrical
information`, in five languages.

**The plug-in medical bikes use a different, metric, medical-device block** - 10 to 36 degrees C,
30 to 90 %, 700 to 1060 mbar - and this card is not their answer
(`spirit-med-bike-safety-operating-environment-iec-60601`).
