**Quick Start is the quickest way to start a workout: after the console powers up, press Start.**

- The **Time counts up from zero**.
- The **workload is adjusted by hand** with the **Level Up and Down keys** (the XE395-2023 prints
  them as **Level +/-**).
- The **dot matrix shows only the bottom row lit at first**. As you increase the workload more rows
  light and the machine gets harder to pedal.

**There are 20 levels of resistance**, and the manuals band them:

| Levels | What the manual says |
|---|---|
| **1-5** | Very easy workloads, stepped for de-conditioned users |
| **6-10** | More challenging, but the increases from one level to the next stay small |
| **11-15** | Start getting tough as the levels jump more dramatically |
| **16-20** | Extremely hard; good for short interval peaks and elite athletic training |

The XE395-2010, XE195, XE295 and XG400 print "extremely hard"; the XE395-2023 prints "extremely
difficult". Nothing else differs.

**Twenty is not every XE.** The **XE795 of both generations has 40 levels** on a differently banded
scale: `spirit-xe795-console-quick-start-with-forty-resistance-levels`. The 2005 and 2007 machines
print no level count in Quick Start at all -
`spirit-xe-console-quick-start-lights-the-bottom-row-of-the-dot-matrix` - and the XE150's own
console counts levels **1 to 16**.

**The XE395ENT touchscreen has the same 20 levels but a different start.** You press **Start
Workout** on the screen or the START key; see `xe395ent-2021-console-power-up-and-quick-start`.
