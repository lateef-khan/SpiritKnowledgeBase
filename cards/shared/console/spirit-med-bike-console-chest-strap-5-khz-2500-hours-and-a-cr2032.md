**"The chest strap transmitter is not a standard part, but is a separate purchase. Most transmitters
that operate at 5kHz frequency will also work."** All seven books print the sentence word for word.

**Battery:** "estimated transmitter battery life is **2500 hours**. If your chest strap has a
replaceable battery the replacement battery is **CR2032**." The strap "is automatically activated
when it detects activity from the user's heart" and deactivates when it receives none; dry it after use,
because moisture can create false signals and shortens battery life.

**5 kHz is the analogue Polar-style band.** The same sentence is printed for the 7.0T and 8.0T
treadmills (`spirit-2026t-console-chest-strap-5khz`) and the rehabilitation steppers
(`spirit-rehab-stepper-console-chest-strap-five-kilohertz-and-a-cr2032`). **The 4.0T treadmill's
2026 book says Bluetooth or ANT+ instead** (`40t-2026-console-chest-strap-bluetooth-ant`), and so do
the Dyaco PT editions of the 4.0 bikes
(`spirit-med-40-pt-bike-console-chest-strap-bluetooth-or-ant-plus-with-no-frequency`) - check which
book the caller holds before naming a strap.

**On the 8.0U and 8.5R this page contradicts the console chapter.** Those two books print the same
5 kHz sentence, but their POWER ON & CONSOLE OPERATION page says the console reads a heart rate sensor
over **BLE and ANT+** and "has support for wireless connection through ANT+ and Bluetooth"
(`spirit-med-80-bike-console-wifi-ble-ftms-and-ant-plus`). Nothing reconciles the two; the strap page
is a reused block. A caller with an 8-series bike and a Bluetooth strap should try it.

**The 8-series and 7.0 books name no receiver part**; the 4.0 parts lists carry a Bluetooth board and
the 7.0 parts lists a "W/Receiver, HR". Neither is described in a console chapter.
