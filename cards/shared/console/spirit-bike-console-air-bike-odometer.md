1. Enter engineering mode - **Start and Enter for 2 seconds** - and step to the **ODO** setting with
   the arrows. **The ODO sign blinks.**
2. Press **Enter** to get into it.
3. Press **up or down** to switch to another engineering mode setting; **pressing the Reset button
   twice** exits engineering mode. With no activity for 30 seconds the console switches to STANDBY
   Mode.

**Two display rules the manual attaches to the odometer:**

- **When exercise time goes over 1:59:59, the LCD displays an `H` sign.** The field runs out of digits
  and switches to whole hours.
- **When exercise distance goes over 99.99, the LCD displays `KM` or `ML` and the number loses its
  decimals.** Past that point the odometer reads in whole units.

**So an odometer with no decimal point is not broken** - it has simply passed 99.99.

**The odometer survives a keypad reset**: holding Stop for 2 seconds does not erase the ODO value.
**Removing the batteries zeroes it**, along with every other setting, and there is no way to zero it
deliberately from the menu.

**There is no hours counter.** These consoles record distance only; the JB950's odometer, by
contrast, tracks hours of use.
