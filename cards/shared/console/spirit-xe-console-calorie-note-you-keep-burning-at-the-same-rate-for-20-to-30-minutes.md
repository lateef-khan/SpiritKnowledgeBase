Both manuals print the same warning:

> **Calorie readings on every piece of exercise equipment, whether it is in a gym or at home, are not
> accurate and tend to vary widely.** They are meant **only as a guide to monitor your progress from
> workout to workout**. The only way to measure your calorie burn accurately is **in a clinical
> setting connected to a host of machines**, because every person is different and burns calories at
> a different rate.

**And then the claim this card is titled for:**

> Some good news is that you will continue to burn calories **at about the same rate as during
> exercise for 20-30 minutes** after you have finished exercising!

**The XE100 and XE200 claim something different in the same book.** Their console section, on p. 24
of the same manual as the XE300's p. 36, says you keep burning **at an accelerated rate for at least
an hour**. The two sentences cannot both be right and neither section acknowledges the other. See
`spirit-xe-console-calorie-note-you-keep-burning-at-an-accelerated-rate-for-an-hour`, which is also
what every XE from 2010 onwards prints.

**Treat both as marketing copy, not as a figure to quote to a customer.** What the manuals agree on
is the part above: the reading is a comparison guide between your own workouts and nothing more.

**Entering your weight is what makes the number closer to right**, and the same paragraph says so.
