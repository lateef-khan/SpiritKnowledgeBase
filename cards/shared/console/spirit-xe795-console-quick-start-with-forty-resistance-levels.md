**Quick Start is the quickest way to start a workout: after the console powers up, press Start.**

- The **Time counts up from zero**.
- The **workload is adjusted by hand** with the **Level Up/Down keys** (2023: **Level +/-**).
- The **dot matrix shows only the bottom row lit at first**; more rows light as the workload rises.

**There are 40 levels of resistance**, twice as many as any other residential XE elliptical - the
XE895-2018, which shares this machine's console face, has 20.

**The banding as printed contradicts itself, and every printing of both generations repeats the same
error.** Word for word, all five documents - the August 2018 printing (p. 19), both 2021 printings
and the 2023 manual - say:

> The first 10 levels are very easy workloads and the changes between levels are set to a good
> progression for de-conditioned users. Levels 6-10 are more challenging, but the increases in
> resistance from one level to the next remain small. Levels 11-15 start getting tough as the levels
> jump more dramatically. Levels 30-40 are extremely difficult and are good for short interval peaks
> and elite athletic training.

**Levels 6-10 cannot be both "very easy" and "more challenging", and levels 16-29 are never
described.** This is the 20-level paragraph (`spirit-xe-console-quick-start-with-twenty-resistance-levels`)
with only its first and last figures changed. **Only "40 levels of resistance" should be quoted as
fact; the banding in between is not reliable.**

**The 2018 and 2021 manuals print "extremely difficult"; so does the 2023.** The 20-level machines
mostly print "extremely hard". **The error is not a 2021 typo** - it was already in the August 2018
book.

**All four Spirit XBR95 recumbent bike printings print a forty-level Quick Start too**, so this card
is no longer elliptical-only - and **the bikes band the forty levels correctly where the XE795 does
not**.

| Levels | What the XBR95 manual says |
|---|---|
| **1-10** | Very easy workloads, stepped for de-conditioned users |
| **11-20** | More challenging, but the increases from one level to the next stay small |
| **21-30** | Start getting tough as the levels jump more dramatically |
| **31-40** | Extremely hard; good for short interval peaks and elite athletic training |

**That is the same paragraph the XE795 prints, with the bands repaired.** The XE795 books leave
"Levels 6-10" and "Levels 11-15" standing from the twenty-level text and jump to 30-40; the XBR95
books scale every band to the forty levels and leave no gap. **If a customer needs a usable answer
about what the forty levels feel like, the XBR95 wording is the one to quote.**

The 2016, 2018 and 2021 books print Level Up/Down keys; the 2023 book prints **Level + / -**. All four
count the Time up from zero and light only the bottom row of the dot matrix at first.

**The XBR95 is the only forty-level Spirit residential bike.** The XBR25, XBR55, XBU55 and both ENT
bikes have 20: `spirit-xe-console-quick-start-with-twenty-resistance-levels`.

**The XE795-2023 service manual prints the Quick Start paragraph with the forty levels banded 1-10, 11-20, 21-30 and 31-40** - "the first 10 levels", "Levels 11-20", "Levels 21-30", "Levels 31-40" - a clean banding that does not repeat the owner's manuals' error. Start Workout on the screen or the START key begins it, and the Level Up/Down keys set the workload.
