**Three shapes of the same list.** Which one a customer is reading changes what is asked of them.

## Three air rowers: six numbered items, opened by a use limit

Both XRW600 printings and the CRW800 open with a sentence the water rowers do not print:

> This warranty applies only to products in **ordinary household or Light Commercial use** (see restrictions above), and the consumer/facility is responsible for the items listed below:

then: **1.** return the registration card within 10 days; **2.** proper use per this manual; **3.** proper installation per the instructions and all local electric codes; **4.** expenses for making the rower accessible for servicing, including anything not part of it when it shipped; **5.** damages to the finish during shipping, installation or after; **6.** routine maintenance as specified in the manual.

## Two water rowers: six bullets, no registration, no use limit

The CRW900 and CRW800H2O open flatly - *The consumer is responsible for the items listed below* - and the list is not numbered. **The registration bullet is gone**, and a new one appears in its place: *proper connection to a grounded power supply of sufficient voltage, replacement of blown fuses, repair of loose connections or defects in house or facility wiring*. The other five bullets match the air rowers' items 2 to 6, with *fitness equipment* substituted for *Rower* throughout.

## The 8.5UE: eight items headed RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE FACILITY

The ergometer's list runs to eight and is addressed to a facility rather than a consumer. It keeps the registration item and the grounded-supply item, so it is the only one of the six that has both. **Its numbering is broken**: item 1 is not a duty at all but the lead-in sentence, *The facility is responsible for the items listed below*, which has been swept into the numbered list. So there are seven duties printed as eight items, and "responsibility 5" in that book is the grounded-supply duty, not the fifth thing asked of the facility.

## What is common to all six

- **Damage to the finish during shipping and installation is the owner's**, not Spirit's, in every one of the six.
- **Routine maintenance is a condition**, and each manual points at its own schedule. A machine that has not been maintained as the book specifies is not being maintained as the warranty requires.
- **Getting the machine to the engineer is the owner's expense**, including anything added since delivery - a mat, a screen, a platform.
- **No manual gives a maintenance log, a form or a proof requirement.** None says the owner must be able to show maintenance was done. Do not tell a customer they must produce records.
