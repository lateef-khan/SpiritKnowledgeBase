Every SB700 manual and every SB900 manual before 2023 prints the same schedule. Only the effective date changes.

| Component | Residential warranty (all service performed in-home) | Light commercial warranty (all service performed in-facility) |
|---|---|---|
| Parts/Electronics | 3 Years | 3 Years |
| Frame | Lifetime | Lifetime |
| Labor | 1 Year | 1 Year |

| Machines | Effective date |
|---|---|
| sb700-2011, sb700-2016 | January 1, 2011 |
| sb700-2019 | January 1, 2019 |
| sb700-2020 | July 1, 2020 |
| sb700-2021 | November 1, 2020 |
| sb900-2014, sb900-2016 | May 1, 2014 |
| sb900-2019 | May 1, 2019 |
| sb900-2020 | July 1, 2020 |
| sb900-2021, sb900-2022 | November 1, 2020 |

Both columns carry the same numbers, so a light commercial installation gets the same periods as a household one. That is unlike the LCB and LCR bikes, whose two columns differ.

SOLE's responsibilities include providing new or remanufactured parts, at SOLE's option, and technical support to independent dealers and servicing organizations. Where there is no dealer or service organization, SOLE administers the warranty directly to the consumer.

Two things on the same page pull against the light commercial column: the consumer-responsibilities paragraph says the warranty applies only to products in **ordinary household use**, and exclusion 8 excludes "product use in any environment other than a residential setting". The manual does not reconcile them.

The console is warranted separately. Where the machine carries the Universal Group Cycling Monitor, that monitor is warranted for one year by Direction Technology, not by SOLE.
