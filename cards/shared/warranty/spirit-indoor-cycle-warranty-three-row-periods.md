| Warranty | Frame | Parts | Labor | Wear Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial - a dues-paying gym (Health Clubs, YMCA, Community Centers) | **10 Years** | 3 Years | 1 Year | 1 Year |
| Commercial - a non-dues-paying facility | **15 Years** | 3 Years | 1 Year | 1 Year |
| Residential | **Lifetime** | 3 Years | 1 Year | 1 Year |

**The gym row is the shortest frame, not the longest.** A dues-paying health club gets ten years of frame where a hotel or office gets fifteen and a home gets lifetime. Parts, labor and wear items do not move between the three rows on any of the three manuals.

**All three manuals print the same twelve figures.** What moves is the row labels and the last column's heading:

| Manual | Row 1 label | Row 2 label | Last column heading | Effective date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CB900 2013 | Commercial (Health Clubs, YMCAs, Community Centers) | **Commercial**, unqualified | Wear Items* | January 1, 2012 |
| CIC800 2021 | Commercial (Health Clubs, YMCA, Community Centers, dues paying facilities) | Commercial (non-dues paying facilities) | Wear Items* | September 24, 2021 |
| CIC850 2022 | Commercial (Health Clubs, YMCA, Community Centers, dues paying facilities) | Commercial (non-dues paying facilities) | **Console & Wear Items*** | November 8, 2021 |

**The CB900 row labels never use the phrase *dues paying*.** Its second row is the bare word *Commercial*, and nothing in that manual says what separates it from the first row, which names health clubs, YMCAs and community centres. The CIC800 and CIC850 spell the distinction out. Read a CB900 owner the row labels as printed rather than importing the CIC wording.

**Only the CIC850 covers the console under this column, and it covers it for 1 year.** Its heading reads *Console & Wear Items*; the CB900 and CIC800 head the same column *Wear Items* and warrant no console period anywhere. Do not tell a CB900 or CIC800 owner their console is covered for a year - their manuals do not say it. What counts as a wear item is `spirit-indoor-cycle-warranty-wear-items`.

**The labor figure here is 1 Year, and the SERVICE paragraph two pages later dates it from a twelve-month or eighteen-month clock.** The two agree on these three manuals, unlike the 900-series bikes where they do not (`xt-2023-warranty-labor-period`).

## These are not the other Spirit bike figures

**The XIC600 indoor cycles print one Residential row and no commercial row at all** - Frame Lifetime, Parts 3 Years, Labor 1 Year, no wear column (`spirit-indoor-cycle-warranty-residential-only-periods`). The parts and labor figures agree with this table, so it is easy to answer an XIC600 from here and give a gym owner a fifteen-year frame their manual never promises.

**The AB900 and AB950 air bikes cut the frame to 5 Years** in both rows and add a Console column at 2 Years (`spirit-air-bike-warranty-periods`). **The JB950 prints one Commercial row at 10 Years frame** and no residential terms (`spirit-jb950-warranty-commercial-only-periods`).

**The CR800, CU800 and 800ENT commercial bikes are a different table again** - Frame Lifetime in both rows, an Electronics or Brake column at 5 or 10 years, Parts 5 or 10 Years and Labor 2 Years (`spirit-bike-warranty-commercial-periods`, `spirit-bike-warranty-residential-periods`). Their parts period is not 3 years and their labor period is not 1 year.

**Do not answer a Sole indoor cycle from this card.** The Sole SB700, SB900 and SB1200 tables are a different brand's terms entirely (`sole-bike-indoor-cycle-warranty-periods`).

When the clock starts is in the paragraph above the table (`xt-2023-warranty-start-date`). Which facility each row means, and where the manuals contradict themselves, is `spirit-indoor-cycle-air-bike-warranty-use-environment`.
