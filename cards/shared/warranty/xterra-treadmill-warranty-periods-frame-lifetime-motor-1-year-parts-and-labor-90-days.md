**Home Use Limited Warranty**, as printed on the warranty page of these 4 books, under the heading *Effective March 13, 2021 ... LIMITED WARRANTY*. The periods run **from the date of retail sale as shown on a sales receipt, or eighteen (18) months from the original factory shipping date when there is no receipt** (`xterra-treadmill-warranty-start-date-sales-receipt-or-18-months-from-factory-shipping`).

**The four figures are the same in all four books; only the wording around them moves.** The TR150 and TR200 print the motor column as *1 Year* and name the warrantor *XTERRA Inc.*; the TRX1000 and TR260 print *1 Years* and name *XTERRA Fitness Inc.* No deck column is printed, so no separate deck period exists in these books - the deck falls under Parts at 90 days.

**Ninety days is the shortest parts and labor cover in the Xterra range.** The same 90-day figures appear on the newer TRX1400, WS200 and WS300 pages, which drop the motor column altogether (`xterra-treadmill-warranty-periods-frame-lifetime-parts-and-labor-90-days-no-motor-column`). Every other Xterra treadmill book prints one year or more.

**The SERVICE paragraph on the same page contradicts the table**: it says *Twelve (12) months from the date on the bill of sale or eighteen (18) months from the date of factory shipping ... establishes the warranty period should service be required*. The table says 90 days for parts and labor. The book does not reconcile the two; the 12-month sentence is Dyaco boilerplate that survives on every Xterra warranty page whatever the table says (`xterra-treadmill-warranty-start-date-sales-receipt-or-18-months-from-factory-shipping`).
