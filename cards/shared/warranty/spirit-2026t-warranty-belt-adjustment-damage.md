**No.** The Attention box beside the treadbelt tracking procedure states:

> Damage to the running belt resulting from improper tracking /tension adjustments is not covered under the warranty.

The same page makes tracking the owner's job: "Proper belt tracking is an owner responsibility common with all treadmills" (the 7.0T and 8.0T print "common with all rehabilitation treadmills"). The manual also warns that over-tightening "will cause belt damage and premature bearing failure", and that a small adjustment makes a dramatic difference - turn the bolt a quarter turn at a time.

**The 4.0T states this only in the text layer of its PDF.** The sentence is in the file, in capitals - "ATTENTION: DAMAGE TO THE RUNNING BELT RESULTING FROM IMPROPER TRACKING / TENSION ADJUSTMENTS IS NOT COVERED UNDER THE SPIRIT WARRANTY" - positioned over the right-hand column of printed page 44, but it does not render. A 4.0T owner reading the printed page has not been shown it. Treat the exclusion as stated for the 7.0T and 8.0T, and flag the 4.0T as an unresolved documentation defect before quoting it to a 4.0T customer.

**The four Spirit CT800 manuals and the four CT850 ones print the same Attention box**, in the same place beside the treadbelt tracking procedure, and name the warrantor in it:

> Damage to the running belt resulting from improper tracking / tension adjustments is not covered under the **Spirit Fitness** warranty.

It renders on the printed page in all eight - the CT800 2012 manual sets it under an ATTENTION heading of its own, the other seven print it as an inline "ATTENTION:" line - so a CT800 or CT850 owner has been shown it. The same page makes tracking the owner's job in the same wording: "Proper belt tracking is an owner responsibility common with all treadmills." Their shared exclusion list is `spirit-ct800-warranty-exclusions`, whose item 3 carries the general "improper maintenance" case.

This sits alongside, not inside, the numbered exclusion list. Exclusion 3 in that list already refuses damage from "improper maintenance"; the Attention box is the specific case. The full list is `ct900-warranty-exclusions`.

**The CT900 prints the box too, in the CT800 and CT850 position and with a third
wording.** Beside its treadbelt tracking procedure, in capitals:

> ATTENTION: DAMAGE TO THE RUNNING BELT RESULTING FROM IMPROPER TRACKING / TENSION
> ADJUSTMENTS IS NOT COVERED UNDER **THE SPIRIT WARRANTY**.

Three wordings, one rule: the 2026 manuals name "the warranty", the CT800 and CT850 name
"the Spirit Fitness warranty", the CT900 names "the Spirit warranty". The CT900 page
also carries the same owner-responsibility sentence - "Proper belt tracking is an owner
responsibility common with all treadmills." Its exclusion list is
`ct900-warranty-exclusions`, whose item 3 carries the general "improper maintenance"
case.

**Two machines in the CT900 family do not print it, and the difference matters.** The
**CT900ENT** prints the TREADBELT TRACKING ADJUSTMENT procedure and the owner-
responsibility sentence but **no Attention box and no warranty sentence at all**; the
**CTSBS900** is a slat belt sled and prints no tracking adjustment procedure, only a
"Belt Tension / Tracking - Inspect" row in its maintenance schedule. Neither manual
states this exclusion, so do not quote it to an owner of either - their only relevant
exclusion is the general "improper maintenance" of item 3
(`ct900ent-exclusions`, `ctsbs900-warranty-exclusions`).

**A Sole manual states the same rule about a Sole machine.** That is a different machine's fact, not this one - see `f50-2026-belt-damage-from-adjustment-not-covered`.
