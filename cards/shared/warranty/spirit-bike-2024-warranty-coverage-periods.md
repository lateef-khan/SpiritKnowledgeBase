| Warranty | Frame\* | Generator Brake | Parts | Labor | Wear Items\*\* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial (Dues Paying Facilities) | 10 Years | 5 Years | 3 Years | 3 Years | 6 Months |
| Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | Lifetime | 5 Years | 5 Years | 2 Years | 6 Months |
| Residential | Lifetime | 5 Years | 10 Years | 2 Years | 6 Months |

\*Frame is defined as the welded metal base of the unit and does not include removable parts, wear items, or coatings.

\*\*Wear items include: plastic covers, overlay, grips, hardware, pull pins/knobs, decals/stickers, headphone jack, USB port, paint/coatings/finish, straps, and pedals.

> Prisons and correctional facilities are excluded from warranty coverage.

**Yes, a dues-paying gym is covered** - and these are the only two Spirit bike manuals in this knowledge base that give one its own row. Every earlier bike manual either excludes a dues-paying facility outright or never mentions one (`spirit-bike-warranty-use-environment`).

**Only the dues-paying row loses the lifetime frame.** It reads 10 Years where the non-dues-paying and residential rows both read Lifetime. A dues-paying gym also gets 3 years of parts against 5, and 3 years of labor against 2 - parts and labor move in opposite directions, exactly as they do on the 2025 ellipticals. Brake and Wear Items are 5 Years and 6 Months in all three rows.

**This is where the bikes and the ellipticals part company.** The CE900 2025 and CES880 print the same three row labels, the same brake figure and the same two footnotes, but their **non-dues-paying** row reads Frame **10 Years** where these two read **Lifetime** (`spirit-ce-2025-warranty-coverage-periods`). A non-dues-paying facility that owns both machines has a lifetime frame on the bike and a ten-year frame on the elliptical. Do not answer one from the other's card.

**Wear items are 6 months in every row**, and the list under the table is closed and specific. Grips, pedals, straps, decals, the headphone jack, the USB port and the paint are wear items - so a USB port fault at seven months is outside the parts period whichever row applies.

**The opening paragraph does not match the table.** Both manuals warrant "all its **home use** Recumbent Bike parts" or "all its **home use** Upright Bike parts" and say the warranty is administered "directly to a **consumer**", above a table whose first two rows are commercial and a responsibilities list addressed to a **facility**. Neither manual reconciles the two. Quote both rather than choosing.

**The recumbent and the upright manuals are the same document twice over here.** The CR900 2025 and CU900 2025 warranty sections match at **99.5%** word for word, and every difference is the machine's name. Both are effective **October 01, 2024**, and both product-registration pages carry the same *Version: 3.0, Revision: 06.12.25*. Nothing in the warranty section distinguishes a recumbent from an upright but the machine's name.

**These are not the earlier CR900 or CU900 figures.** The 2018 and ENT manuals print **one** unqualified Commercial row - Frame Lifetime, Brake 5 Years, Parts 3 Years, Labor 3 Years - and no residential row at all (`spirit-bike-900-warranty-commercial-periods`). A CR900 or CU900 question has two possible answers; ask which manual came with the machine.

**Nor are they the CU800ENT 2024 figures**, although that manual's terms are dated a week later. It has no brake column and no general Labor column, and its frame is 10 Years in both of its rows (`cu800ent-2024-warranty-coverage-periods`).
