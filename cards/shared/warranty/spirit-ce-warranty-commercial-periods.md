| Warranty | Frame | Brake / Electronics | Parts | Labor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Lifetime | 5 Years | 5 Years | 2 Years |

**All four figures are the same in all seven manuals.** What moves is the name of the second column, the row label, and the printed effective date:

| Manual | Second column heading | Row label | Effective date |
|---|---|---|---|
| CE800 2013 | Electronics | Commercial | March 1, 2012 |
| CE800 2021 | ELECTRONICS | Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | November 08, 2021 |
| CE800 2023 | ELECTRONICS | Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | June 08, 2023 |
| CE800ENT | EMS BRAKE | Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | July 3, 2024 |
| CE850 2016 | Brake | Light Commercial (5 Hours use or less in a non-dues paying facility) | December 23, 2015 |
| CE850 2020 | Brake | Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | November 08, 2021 |
| CE850 2022 | Brake | Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities) | July 06, 2022 |

**The column heading changes but the figure does not.** A CE800 manual calls it *Electronics*, a CE850 manual calls it *Brake*, and the CE800ENT calls it *EMS Brake*. No manual prints both columns, and none says whether *Electronics* and *Brake* cover the same components, so answer "the second column is 5 years" rather than naming a part the manual in hand does not name.

**The CE800ENT is described by two names in two documents.** Its owner's manual warranty table heads the column **EMS BRAKE**, while its service manual calls the same component an **induction brake** throughout (`ce800ent-flywheel-and-induction-brake`, `ce800ent-no-resistance`). Neither document defines EMS or says the two are the same part. Quote whichever name the customer's document uses, and do not tell a customer that a fault in a part their service manual calls an induction brake is or is not the EMS brake the table covers.

**Only the CE850 2016 caps the hours.** Its row reads *Light Commercial (5 Hours use or less in a non-dues paying facility)*, and its exclusion 8 repeats the cap. The CE850 2020 and 2022 manuals relabel the same row *Commercial (Non-Dues Paying Facilities)* and drop the hours from the label - but keep the five-hour cap in exclusion 8 and keep "Light Commercial use (see restrictions above)" in the responsibilities lead sentence, pointing at a restriction their table no longer prints. Which environment each row covers is `spirit-ce-warranty-use-environment`.

**The CE800ENT prints two columns this row does not have** - Console Parts 3 Years and Console Labor 1 Year. Nothing above is wrong for a CE800ENT; it is incomplete. Read the console periods from `ct800ent-2022-warranty-console-periods` and never fold them into Parts 5 Years.

**These are not the CE900 or CES880 figures.** The CE900 2021 prints one Commercial row reading Frame Lifetime, Generator Brake 5 Years, Parts 3 Year, Labor 3 Years, with no residential row at all (`ce900-2021-warranty-coverage-periods`). The CE900 2025 and CES880 2025 print three rows with a 10-year frame and a Wear Items column (`spirit-ce-2025-warranty-coverage-periods`). Neither table is this one.

**Do not answer this from a Spirit treadmill card either.** The CT800 and CT850 tables read the same four figures but their second column is *Motor/Deck*, not a brake or electronics column (`spirit-ct800-warranty-commercial-periods`).

The home row of the same table is `spirit-ce-warranty-residential-periods`. When the clock starts is in the paragraph above the table (`xt-2023-warranty-start-date`); the two-year labor figure is dated separately in the SERVICE paragraph (`spirit-ct800-warranty-labor-period`).
