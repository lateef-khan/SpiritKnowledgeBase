**The manuals do not draw the line, and they contradict themselves about which side of it these machines sit on.** Answer this question from the source, not by inference.

What the source does say:

- The section is headed **WARRANTY - COMMERCIAL**, the single table row is labelled **Commercial**, and everything around it addresses a **facility**: RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE FACILITY, facility wiring, a facility's routine maintenance.
- The opening paragraph warrants "all its **Treadmill** parts". The equivalent XT and CT900 paragraphs warrant "all its **home use** treadmill parts"; the words *home use* are dropped here.
- Exclusion 2 nevertheless still refuses service call reimbursement for a "Treadmill **not in ordinary household or light commercial use**" - residential wording, left standing inside a commercial warranty.
- **Prisons and correctional facilities** are the only environment named as excluded (`spirit-2026t-warranty-prisons-excluded`).

What the source does **not** say: it never distinguishes a dues-paying from a non-dues-paying facility, never caps hours of use per day, never defines light commercial use, and prints no residential table to fall back on.

**Do not import the XT limit.** The residential XT manuals exclude "any environment other than a residential setting or non-dues paying facility with 5 hours use or less per day" - see `xt-2023-warranty-use-environment`. That sentence is **not** in the 4.0T, 7.0T or 8.0T. Neither is the CT900's split of Commercial (Dues Paying) from Commercial (Non-Dues Paying), which carries two different sets of periods.

Treat a dues-paying placement as something these manuals leave open rather than as approved or refused, and refer the question to the dealer.

**The 2025 printings of the 4.0T and 7.0T are the same text**, one Commercial row and no named setting. The 2025 medical bikes are the same shape again (`spirit-med-bike-warranty-use-environment`).
