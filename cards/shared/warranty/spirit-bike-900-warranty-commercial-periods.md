| Warranty | Frame | Generator Brake / EMS Brake | Parts | Labor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Lifetime | 5 Years | 3 Years | 3 Years |

**There is no second row.** These four manuals warrant one environment and print no Residential row, no light-commercial row and no dues-paying row. **They state no home terms at all**, so nothing in them answers "how long is my CR900 covered at home". Do not borrow the CR800 or CU800 Residential row to answer it - that is a different table on a different machine (`spirit-bike-warranty-residential-periods`).

**All four figures are the same in all four manuals.** What moves is the brake column's name, the row label, and the printed effective date:

| Manual | Brake column | Row label | Effective date |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR900 2018 | GENERATOR BRAKE | Commercial | January 1, 2017 |
| CU900 2018 | GENERATOR BRAKE | Commercial | November 19, 2021 |
| CR900ENT 2021 | EMS BRAKE | Commercial (All Facilities) | November 8, 2021 |
| CU900ENT | EMS BRAKE | Commercial (All Facilities) | November 19, 2021 |

**The brake column is named for two different parts and reads 5 Years on both.** The two 2018 manuals head it *Generator Brake* and the two touchscreen manuals *EMS Brake*. Neither pair defines the term or says whether the two are the same component, so quote whichever name the customer's manual uses rather than naming a part their document does not name.

**The parts figure is printed two ways and means one thing.** The CR900 2018 and CU900 2018 print **3 Year**; the CR900ENT and CU900ENT print **3 Years**. Three years on all four.

**"Commercial" here is not narrowed to a non-dues-paying facility.** The two ENT manuals say *All Facilities* in the row label itself, the two 2018 manuals leave the word bare, and **none of the four excludes a dues-paying gym anywhere in the warranty section** - their exclusion lists run to eight items with no such item on them (`ce900-2021-warranty-exclusions`). That is the opposite of the CR800 and CU800 manuals, whose exclusion 8 bars a dues-paying facility outright: `spirit-bike-warranty-use-environment`.

**Three years of labor sits against a twelve-month service clock.** The SERVICE paragraph on the same page dates the labor warranty period from twelve months on the bill of sale or eighteen months from factory shipping, and none of the four manuals reconciles that with the 3 Years in this row. Quote both (`xt-2023-warranty-labor-period`).

**The printed effective date is no guide to the manual's year, on these four more than on any others.** The CR900 2018 book carries terms effective **January 1, 2017**, although its PDF was re-exported in November 2021. The CU900 2018 book is a 2018 document - it prints *Revision: 08.08.2018* - whose **warranty page has been replaced**: the terms on it are effective **November 19, 2021**, the same day as the CU900ENT's. So a CU900 owner's body text and their warranty page come from three years apart. Read the effective date off the warranty page in hand and never infer it from the model year.

**A recumbent and an upright of the same generation share this table.** The CR900 2018 and CU900 2018 print the same four figures; so do the CR900ENT and CU900ENT. The row is not a CR or a CU fact - only the effective dates separate them.

**The CE900 2021 elliptical prints these four figures too, and it is a different machine.** Frame Lifetime, Generator Brake 5 Years, Parts 3 Year, Labor 3 Years, one Commercial row and no residential row - the same shape and the same numbers (`ce900-2021-warranty-coverage-periods`). Answer a bike from this card and an elliptical from that one.

**These are not the 2024-terms figures.** The CR900 2025 and CU900 2025 replace this single row with three, cut the frame to 10 Years in a dues-paying gym and add a Wear Items column (`spirit-bike-2024-warranty-coverage-periods`).
