Under the heading **NORMAL RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE CONSUMER**:

> This warranty applies only to products in ordinary household use, and the consumer is responsible for the items listed below:

1. The warranty registration card must be completed and returned to the address listed on the card **within 10 days** of the original purchase to validate the manufacturer's limited warranty.
2. Proper use of the fitness bike in accordance with the instructions provided in this manual
3. Proper installation in accordance with instructions provided with the fitness bike and with all local electric codes.
4. **Proper connection to a grounded power supply of sufficient voltage, replacement of blown fuses, repair of loose connections or defects in house wiring.**
5. Expenses for making the fitness bike accessible for servicing, including any item that was not part of the fitness bike at the time it was shipped from the factory.
6. Damages to the fitness bike finish during shipping, installation or following installation.
7. Routine maintenance of this unit as specified in this manual.

**The seven items are word for word identical in all ten manuals**, over five and a half years and six machines. Every one of them calls the machine a *fitness bike*, whatever the warranty table above calls it.

## The lead sentence is not the same on all ten

| Manuals | Lead sentence |
|---|---|
| XBR25 2019; XBR55 2019, 2021; XBU55 2019, 2021; XBR55ENT and XBU55ENT 2021 | "products in ordinary **household** use" |
| XBR95 2016, 2018, 2021 | "products in ordinary **household or light commercial** use" |

**The XBU55 2019 and 2021 are the pair to watch.** They print a Light Commercial row of periods in the table two inches above (`spirit-xb-warranty-light-commercial-periods`) and then restrict this list to household use, which the XBR95 manuals - printing the same row - do not. Nothing in either book reconciles it (`spirit-xb-warranty-use-environment`).

**The three XBR95 manuals print an extra space before the comma** - "light commercial use ," - in all three printings.

## Item 4 is the one that costs money

**Blown fuses, loose connections and defects in house wiring are the owner's, not a warranty repair.** So is a power supply that is not grounded or not of sufficient voltage. **All ten XB manuals carry this duty**, which is not true of the Spirit home elliptical range - only the XE395 2010 and the two XE195 printings impose it there, and the rest drop it (`spirit-xe-warranty-consumer-responsibilities-electrical-duty`).

**All four 2023 XB manuals drop it too**, along with item 1, and print five items instead of seven: `spirit-xb-2023-warranty-consumer-responsibilities`.

## Item 1 is a duty the manual also enforces as an exclusion

The 10-day card deadline is repeated as **exclusion 5**, which excludes a bike with no registration card on file outright. The two halves are `spirit-xb-warranty-registration` and `spirit-xb-warranty-exclusions`. **None of the ten offers an online route inside item 1**, even though every one of them prints a registration page a few dozen pages earlier that does.

## Item 5 is a cost, not a task

It makes the owner pay to make the bike reachable for a service call, and to deal with anything that was not in the box. It is one of five charges the owner carries during the warranty period; the other four are in the SERVICE steps (`xt-2023-warranty-owner-costs`).

**Do not answer this from a Spirit commercial machine's list.** The CT800, CT850, CE800 and CR800 seven-item list says the card may be "**completed online**" in item 1, calls the machine *fitness equipment*, and extends item 4 to "house **or facility** wiring" (`spirit-ct800-warranty-consumer-responsibilities`). Those three differences matter to a customer.
