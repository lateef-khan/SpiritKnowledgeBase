**The motor goes in at the 11 o'clock position, and the cable nut stays loose until the bracket is set.**

**6.10.2 Remove the gear motor cable and gear motor.** As shown in figure 6.10.3, remove the gear motor cable by
taking one end of the cable off the gear motor and releasing the nut with a wrench. As shown in figure 6.10.4,
loosen the **three fixing screws** of the gear motor to remove it.

**6.10.3 Refit the gear motor.** As shown in figures 6.10.5 and 6.10.6, make sure the **starting position is at
11 o'clock** when installing the gear motor. If that is not possible, use a screwdriver to loosen the screw in
the middle, rotate to the correct point, then pull the screw and pull the motor back to the holder.

**6.10.4 Refit the gear motor cable.** As shown in figure 6.10.7, **do not tighten the nut** after fitting the
cable to the motor. As shown in figure 6.10.8, once the motor bracket is on the main frame, the **aluminium plate
must be equidistant from the magnet on the left and right**; if it is not, adjust the two bottom screws on one
side as in figure 6.10.9 (do not lock them, so they can still be adjusted back and forth). Connect the gear
motor cable and fit the spring, then set the resistance to **level 20** on the console. Once the cable has pulled
to position, turn the aluminium plate and see whether it touches the magnet bracket; if it does, move the
bracket back, then lock the screw.

The motor's electrical checks - about 2 to 3 ohm across the armature, about 5 k-ohm across the position sensor,
measured at the 14-pin console cable - are the "Motor Error" troubleshooting in the same book, not this card.

**The 7.0S (RS9500-SS021-02) and 7.5S (RS9600-SS021-03) service manuals print this procedure word for word**, figure numbers included. The 11 o'clock start, the loose nut and the level-20 check are in all three.
