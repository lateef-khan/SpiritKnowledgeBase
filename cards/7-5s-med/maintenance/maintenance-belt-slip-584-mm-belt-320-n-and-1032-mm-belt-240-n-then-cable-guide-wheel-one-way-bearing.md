**Two belts, two figures: the 584 mm belt at 320 N and the 1032 mm belt at 240 N.** The 8.5S sets a single drive
belt to 80 N (`85s-2025-maintenance-belt-slip-idler-screw-clockwise-to-80-n-then-slide-wheel-one-way-bearing`) and the CRS800S its belt to 380 N (`crs800s-2021-maintenance-belt-slippery-front-idle-pulley-allen-wrench-380-n-then-cable-drive-pulley-one-way-bearing`); the XS895 and CS800 read theirs in hertz
(`cvc800-drive-belt-slipping`). None of those numbers belongs on this machine.

7.1 Slip of Belt, as printed:

1. **7.1.1** - If the belt is found slipping, "as shown in Figure 7.1.1, Figure 7.1.2 or reference on step 6.9.4,
   **adjust the tension by tightening the screw and nut**."
2. **7.1.2** - "If you adjust the belt tension, there is still slippery phenomenon. The reason may be that **the
   one-way bearing in the cable guide wheel is broken**, you need to **replace the cable guide wheel** (refer to
   steps 6.8)."

The screw and nut are the ones 6.9.1 loosens to free the idler wheel - the socket head cap bolt and the **J bolt**
(part 163, M8 × P1.25 × 80L). 6.9.4 gives the targets: "adjust the tightness of those two bolts to adjust the belt
tension. **The belt tension for belt (584L) should be 320N. The belt tension for belt (1032L) should be 240N.**"
The two belts are parts 37 and 38, both 8PJ poly-V. The book names no gauge; the figures are forces.

The cable guide wheel of 7.1.2 carries a **one-way bearing** (part 54, two on the machine) that lets the pedal
cable pay out and return; when it fails the drive slips however tight the belt is. Its replacement (6.8) is an
assembly card, and two things in it matter afterwards: the one-way bearing goes in the direction of figure 6.8.4,
and the optical sensor is reset to **7-9 mm** from the wheel, or the step count goes wrong.

Nothing else in the book tensions a belt. The front connecting cable is set to 40-50 lb when it is replaced
(6.5.2), which is a cable, not a belt.
