**One sentence, and the only rail instruction in either medical stepper's service manual.** 3.3 Check for Aluminum
Track, as printed: "regular cleaning of the surface of the aluminum sliding rail can avoid PU wheel loss" (figure
3.3.1, the seat carriage rail).

Read *loss* as wear: the seat carriage runs on polyurethane wheels - parts-list item 101, twenty-seven PU wheels on
the machine - and grit on the rail eats them. Keep the rail surface clean and the wheels last.

**What is not printed:** no interval beyond "regular", no cleaner, no cloth, and no lubricant. The 7.5S owner's
manual names no lubricant anywhere in its 75 pages either
(`7-5s-med-maintenance-monthly-wear-check-on-grips-cushions-and-pedals`). Say the book is silent rather than
importing a rule from another machine.

**The 8.5S service manual prints no rail item.** Its preventative chapter is the pedal and handlebar checks only
(`spirit-med-stepper-maintenance-preventative-check-pedals-and-swing-arm-handlebars-for-looseness`), and its owner's-manual schedule has no rail row (`spirit-85s-maintenance-machine-care-schedule`).

Other Spirit rails are cleaned with something named: the CR900 recumbent bike with a damp cloth, mild cleaner and
rubbing alcohol (`spirit-cr900-maintenance-periodic-cleaning-and-seat-track-rails`), the CRS800S step rails with a
rag and alcohol behind the mast covers (`crs800s-2024-maintenance-step-rail-and-carriage-cleaning`). Those are
their books' instructions, not this one's.
