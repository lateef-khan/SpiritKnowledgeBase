1. **Wipe down all areas in the sweat path with a damp cloth after each workout.**
2. If a **squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling** develops, the main cause is most likely one
   of two things:
   1. **The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.** All bolts installed during
      assembly need to be tightened as much as possible. It may be necessary to use a larger
      wrench than the one provided if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. The manuals are
      emphatic here: **90% of calls to the service department for noise issues can be traced to
      loose hardware.**
   2. **The crank arm nut needs to be retightened** — on the CU900ENT the pedals too, see
      `cu900ent-check-pedals`.
3. If squeaks or other noises persist, **check that the unit is properly levelled**. There are
   **2 levelling pads on the bottom of the rear stabilizer**; use a **14 mm wrench** (or an
   adjustable wrench) to adjust the levellers.

## Sixteen machines print this, one does not

Every Spirit commercial bike owner's manual in the repository prints the list above except the
**2012 CU800**, and so do four Spirit **indoor cycles** — the CIC800 2021, CIC850 2022 and both
XIC600 printings. The 2012 CU800 prints a different page: rear-rail and polyurethane-wheel cleaning, a
lubricant, **4** levelling pads under the rear rails and **no crank arm nut**. Do not quote two
pads or a rear stabilizer for it —
`cu800-2012-maintenance-squeak-rear-rails-and-four-levelers`.

The wording varies without changing a figure. The CR800 2021/2023 and CU800 2021 print it as a
numbered list with sub-items a/b/c and the lead-in "I cannot stress this point enough"; the CR900,
CU900, CR900ENT, CU900ENT, CU800ENT 2022 and CR800ENT 2023 print it as prose with "It is important
to note that". The 2024 CU800ENT and the 2025 CR900 and CU900 move the same paragraph under a
**MACHINE CARE - CONTINUED** heading beside a schedule table
(`spirit-bike-maintenance-machine-care-schedule`). The CIC800 and CIC850 print it as a/b/c; both
XIC600 printings say "one of **three** reasons" and number the same three I/II/III, having promoted
the two pedal-and-cleaner notes to items 1 and 2 of the list
(`spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-pedal-internals-and-approved-cleaners`). **No figure changes** —
90%, 2 pads and 14 mm on all sixteen.

## Four indoor cycles carry a schedule table this card does not

The CIC800 and CIC850 print a four-row Part / Action / Frequency / Cleaner table alongside this
list (`spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-schedule-water-and-monthly-brake-pad`); the two XIC600
printings print a five-column version of it whose figures differ
(`spirit-indoor-cycle-maintenance-schedule-wd40-and-weekly-brake-pad`). Neither is the eleven-row
commercial Machine Care table.

**The air bikes and the JB950 print none of this.** They break service into Daily, Weekly and
Monthly regimens instead — `spirit-air-bike-maintenance-daily-dry-off-and-six-sweat-points`. They
name no crank arm nut, no 90% figure and no 14 mm wrench.

## The crank arm nut is literal on a bike

A bike has a crank arm and its nut can work loose. The same sentence appears in Spirit treadmill
manuals as a carry-over, where it is not — `ct800-2016-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking` holds
the identical three figures for the Spirit treadmills and commercial ellipticals. The two cards
state the same figures for different product lines; neither supersedes the other.

## What the owner's manuals do not add

Chapter 7 of the **CU900ENT service manual** adds mechanical answers no owner's manual carries: a
flywheel sound when pedalling backwards is normal, a whole-machine shake is a levelling problem,
and the pedals tighten in opposite directions. See `cu900ent-noise-and-feet-feeling`,
`cu900ent-shaking` and `cu900ent-check-pedals`. **None of the twelve owner's manuals prints the
wear-parts warning** either (`cu900ent-wear-parts-inspection`) — checked for "susceptible",
"damage and wear" and "PU wheel" in all thirteen; the only PU wheel hits are parts-list rows.
