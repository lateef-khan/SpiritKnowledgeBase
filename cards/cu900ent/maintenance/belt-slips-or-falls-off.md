Section 7.1 as printed: "If you find the belt slip and off the phenomenon, please refer to 6.5,
6.7.1, 6.7.2, 6.7.3, the nut can be adjusted to adjust the tension."

Those four references are, in order:

| Ref | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 6.5 | Left and right chain cover removal — how to get at the belt |
| 6.7.1 | Remove the nut and hook screw to release the spring |
| 6.7.2 | Routing the belt |
| 6.7.3 | The idler: loosen the three screws; the idler must be installed **counterclockwise to the end of the lock** |

## The tension figure

Section 6.7.2 gives the value: **the belt is maintained in the middle of the belt during
installation, the belt tension is maintained at 180 to 210 N.**

That is the only belt tension figure this manual prints. Note it is stated in **newtons**, not as
a sonic-meter frequency — the CVC800 climber manual gives its drive belt as 190 Hz +/- 10 Hz and
the CE800ENT elliptical as about 185-210 kHz, so do not carry a figure between machines.

The removal steps themselves are part replacement procedures and are not repeated here.

## The CU900 2018 prints the same answer; the CU1000ENT prints only the figure

The **CU900 2018** service manual carries section 7.1 and section 6.7.2 word for word — the same
four references, the same **180 to 210 N**. The **CU1000ENT 2023** book has no chapter 7 at all; its
drive-system replacement (12.18) prints the same 180 to 210 N install sentence and nothing about a
belt that slips, so it is not listed on this card.

The recumbent **CR900 2018 and CR900ENT 2021** are a different mechanism and a different figure:
a spring-loaded idler set at a top nut and a bottom jam nut to **220 to 240 N (50–55 lbs)**
(`spirit-cr900-maintenance-belt-slip-jam-nut-220-to-240-newtons`). Do not quote either figure for
the other family.
