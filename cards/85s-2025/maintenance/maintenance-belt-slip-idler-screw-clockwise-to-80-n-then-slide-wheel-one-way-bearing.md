**One drive belt, one figure: 80 N.** The 7.5S sets two belts to 320 N and 240 N (`7-5s-med-maintenance-belt-slip-584-mm-belt-320-n-and-1032-mm-belt-240-n-then-cable-guide-wheel-one-way-bearing`), the CRS800S one belt to
380 N (`crs800s-2021-maintenance-belt-slippery-front-idle-pulley-allen-wrench-380-n-then-cable-drive-pulley-one-way-bearing`), and the CS800 and XS895 read theirs at 190 Hz on a sonic meter (`cvc800-drive-belt-slipping`).
The 8.5S owner's manual prints no belt figure at all (`spirit-85s-maintenance-machine-care-schedule`); this is the
only one for the machine.

7-1 Belt slip, as printed:

1. "If the drive belt is slipping, as shown in Figure 7.1.1, **tighten the screw clockwise** to adjust the drive belt
   tension."
2. "If the drive belt tension has been adjusted and slipping still occurs, the cause may be a **faulty
   unidirectional bearing inside the Steel Cable Slide Wheel**. In this case, the Steel Cable Slide Wheel needs to
   be replaced (refer to 6-8)."

The screw is the idler-pulley screw of 6-9: an **8 mm hex wrench** turns it counter-clockwise to loosen the idler
for a belt change, so clockwise tightens; and 6-9 step 3 gives the target - "adjust the tightness of the bolt to set
the drive belt tension. **The drive belt tension should be 80 N.**" No gauge is named.

The steel cable slide wheel (urethane) of step 2 carries the **one-way bearing** that lets the pedal cable pay out
and return; when it fails the drive slips however tight the belt is. Its replacement (6-8) is an assembly card, and
two things in it matter afterwards: the one-way bearing goes in the direction of figure 6.8.4, and the **optical
sensor board is set 7-9 mm** from the wheel's barcode sticker, or the step count goes wrong (7-4).

Nothing else in the book tensions a belt. The steel cable itself is set to 40-50 lb when it is replaced (6-5),
which is a cable, not a belt.
