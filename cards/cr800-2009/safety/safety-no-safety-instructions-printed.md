**The only manual on file for this machine prints no safety instructions, no electrical instructions,
no grounding page, no outlet or voltage requirement and no user weight limit.** The book is the
Dyaco `XR898 Service Manual`: one outline drawing, one skeleton drawing, eleven disassembly and
assembly procedures and three Q&A pages. That is the whole of it.

- **No safety chapter.** The word *safety* does not appear in the book, in the text layer, with
  letter-spacing allowed, or in the 300 dpi renders the OCR supplements were made from.
- **No power requirement.** The words *outlet*, *ground*, *GFCI*, *generator* and *adapter* do not
  appear. What the book does say is that the console is fed by a `9P computer cable (44)` and that the
  `Induction Brake (55)` has harnesses to unplug; the console Q&A asks the technician to `use
  multi-meter to check output voltage at each connect contact` and to check the hand-pulse cable
  continuity. **It never says what powers the controller** - no generator sentence, no adapter, no
  cord, no voltage figure.
- **No user weight limit** - the words *lb*, *lbs*, *kg*, *capacity* and *weight* appear nowhere
  (`xt-2015-safety-no-user-weight-limit-printed`).

**Do not fill any of this in from the later CR800 books.** The CR800-2021 and CR800-2023 owner's
manuals describe a generator-powered bike with a 450 lb limit and a full safety list
(`spirit-bike-safety-no-mains-outlet-needed`, `spirit-ct800-safety-user-weight-limit-450-lbs`,
`spirit-bike-safety-instructions-list`). The XR898 is the 2009 machine, with an induction brake and a
separate controller - a different design, and nothing in its book says those statements apply to
it. Say the manual does not state these things,
and get them from the machine's own labels or from Spirit.
