**No. The console has six program buttons - Manual, Hill, Fat Burn, Cardio, Strength and HIIT - and
none of them is a heart rate program.** See `cvc800-program-buttons`.

**The contents page says otherwise.** It lists `20 HEART RATE PROGRAM`, and the erratic-operation
caution on printed page 24 opens *"Do not use this Vertical Climber for Heart Rate programs unless a
steady, solid Actual Heart Rate value is being displayed."* Printed page 21 adds *"Entering your age
is used for the Heart Rate programs."*

**What that chapter actually contains is heart rate *training* material, not a program:** the
220-minus-age formula and the 60%/85% zone (printed page 20), the Target Heart Rate Training Chart
(page 21), the Rate of Perceived Exertion scale (page 22), how to wear the chest strap (page 23) and
the erratic-operation list (page 24). No procedure, no HR key, no percentage-named program.

Proved three ways before carding: a loose search of the whole book for **"HR 1"**, **"HR 2"**,
**"65%"**, **"80%"**, **"auto-pilot"** and **"heart rate program operation"** returns nothing;
printed pages 20-24 were read in full rather than sampled; and the console page names only six
program buttons.

**The Spirit steppers of the same generation do have the program** - two keys named 65% and 80% -
which is where this chapter's title and its cautions come from. See
`spirit-stepper-programs-heart-rate-keys-of-sixty-five-and-eighty-percent`, and do not answer a
CVC800 question from it.

The machine still **reads** heart rate, from contact grips or a Bluetooth chest strap; it just never
drives resistance from it. See `cvc800-bluetooth-app-or-chest-strap`.
