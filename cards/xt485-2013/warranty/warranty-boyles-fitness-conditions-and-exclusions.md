The Boyles Fitness warranty in the SPT0033 book is written as a paragraph rather than a numbered list, and the same conditions are printed twice - on the warranty page and again on the Australian Consumer Law page under *Conditions of Warranty*. It does not extend to:

- any damage caused by **abuse, improper or abnormal usage** (as detailed in the instruction manual), or **repairs not provided by BOYLES** (the second printing: "not provided by us or our Service Centre");
- **products used for commercial or rental purposes**;
- **ordinary wear, tear and weathering**, failure to follow directions, improper installation, improper maintenance, or **acts of God** - the page names storms, lightning, and snow or ice, and the second printing adds heavy winds.

Two conditions govern who can claim: coverage **extends only to the original retail purchaser from the date of purchase**, and the purchaser is told to keep the receipt, tax invoice or other proof of purchase. "No other Warranty beyond that specifically set forth above is authorised by BOYLES."

**Residential only, in effect.** The commercial-or-rental exclusion is the only use restriction printed; there is no dues-paying or light-commercial distinction and no hours-per-day figure.

**This is not the Spirit XT exclusion list.** The Spirit 2015 XT books print a numbered list with serial-number, transfer and registration items (`xt-2015-warranty-exclusions`); none of those appears here. Under the Australian Consumer Law statement in the same book, the statutory guarantees cannot be excluded by any of the above (`xt485-2013-warranty-australian-consumer-law-six-months-and-claims`).
