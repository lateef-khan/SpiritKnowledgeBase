If a squeak, thump, clicking or rough feeling develops, the manual says the main cause is most likely one of two things.

**I. The hardware was not sufficiently tightened during assembly.**
All bolts installed during assembly need to be tightened **as much as possible**. It may be necessary to **use a larger wrench than the one provided** if you cannot tighten the bolts sufficiently. The manual is emphatic here: **90% of calls to the service department for noise issues can be traced to loose hardware or the rear rails being dirty.**

**II. Dirt build-up on the rear rails and polyurethane wheels.**
Build-up on the rails can cause a **thumping sound that seems to come from inside the main body of the machine**, because noise travels and is amplified in the tubing of the frame.

1. Clean the rails and wheels with a **lint free cloth and rubbing alcohol**.
2. Remove stubborn build-up with **your thumbnail or a non-metallic scraper**, such as the back edge of a plastic knife.
3. After cleaning, apply **a small amount of lubricant on the rails** with your fingers or a lint free cloth. **A thin coat is enough; wipe off any excess.**

**III. If squeaks or other noises persist, check that the unit is properly levelled before calling the service department.**

The assembly pages add a cause this list does not mention: **a clicking or thumping sound during pedaling is usually caused by the pedals being too loose** (page 10). Check the pedals as well as the two causes above.

**The manual never names the lubricant** to use on the rails.

The service manuals for the earlier light commercial recumbents carry a different, part-level noise list - seat wheel adjustment plate, pedal wear, loose cranks, the drive pulley rubbing a front shroud. See `lcr-2023-noises`. That is a different machine, and neither list mentions the other's causes, so a technician should work through both.
