**The manual prints no weight-stack rating.** This total is the sum of the plate quantities its
assembly hardware table lists, and it is not a figure the manufacturer states anywhere in the book -
say so when you quote it. Why that is, and how it was checked, is at
`spirit-strength-specs-no-weight-stack-rating-printed`.

| No. | Description | Qty | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 117 | `20LB Weight Stack` | 12 | 240 lb |
| 075 | `10LB Weight Stack` | 5 | 50 lb |
| 074 | `10LB Weight Stack` | 2 | 20 lb |
| 073 | `10LB Weight Stack` | 1 | 10 lb |
| 076 | `10LB Top Weight Stack` | 1 | 10 lb |
| | **Total** | **21** | **330 lb** |

**This stack assists rather than resists.** On a pull-up and dip assist machine the selected weight
counterbalances the user, so a higher pin setting makes the exercise easier, not harder. A customer
who reads 330 lb as a resistance rating has it backwards.

**Three separate part numbers for the same 10 lb plate** - 073, 074 and 075, in quantities 1, 2 and
5. All eight are the same size; the split is a bill-of-materials artefact, not three different
plates.

The 330 lb here and the 330 lb on the seated leg press are unrelated figures -
`spirit-strength-specs-stack-of-330-lb-in-twenty-pound-plates`.

The optional 5 lb increment weight rides on top of the stack on the machines that have one -
