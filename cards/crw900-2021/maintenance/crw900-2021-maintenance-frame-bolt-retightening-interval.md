> *The rower frame bolts require periodic checking for tightness. Do this at the **end of the first
> month of use** and again **every 12 months**.*

That is the only torque-related interval in the book. No torque figure and no spanner size is given
anywhere for it.

**It is printed as a note in the assembly chapter**, beside step 4, not in GENERAL MAINTENANCE — and
the maintenance table on printed p. 51 has no hardware row at all
(`spirit-water-rower-maintenance-weekly-rails-and-hundred-hour-belt-check`). A customer with a
creaking frame at six weeks is exactly the case this note is for.

## The wood finish

> The rower frame can be **polished with any high grade furniture polish** to further highlight the
> rich luster of the wood finish.

**Any high grade furniture polish**, on the frame only. This is cosmetic, not lubrication — the
manual names no lubricant for any moving part
(`spirit-rower-maintenance-no-lubricant-named-anywhere`). For hygiene, Spirit's own sanitizing
procedure is `ct900-sanitizing-equipment`; do not put alcohol solution on the wood in place of the
polish, and do not put polish on the seat track.

## Where it stands

> Choose a **flat, level surface** on which to use your rower, to avoid rocking and potential
> premature wear.

**This machine has no levelling feet and no levelling instruction.** Unlike the Spirit air rowers,
which give 2 levelling pads on the rear stabilizer and a 14 mm wrench
(`ct800-2016-maintenance-squeak-thump-or-clicking`), the CRW900 answers a rocking machine by asking
you to move it. The words *leveling pads*, *crank arm* and *14mm* return zero hits across the whole
book including its OCR supplements, so do not quote the air rower's figures for it.
