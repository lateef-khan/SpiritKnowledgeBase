Required parts: **Heart Rate Receiver [29]**, **3 Cable Ties [30]**, **3 Cable Tie Mounts
[31]**.

1. Find the **flying lead cable on the Mainframe [1]**, just behind the upper **Footboard
   [21]**, and attach the harness.
2. **Run the wire under the footboard** to the side rail.
3. **Paste the receiver on with the double-sided tape**, at the position the indication
   label marks, **keeping the wire on the downside**. Mount it **low enough to clear the
   lower seat rollers**.
4. Use the **Cable Tie Mounts [31]** to secure the wiring clear of any moving parts, and
   trim the excess.

> **Do not affix it permanently until the signal has been tested in that position.**

Excess wire can be tucked behind the metal frame bracket.

## The manual expects the signal to be awkward, and says why

Maintaining a consistent signal on a rower is hard because **the distance between
transmitter and receiver changes through the stroke**. If it is unreliable:

- check the batteries in the chest belt or other device are fresh;
- check the transmitter frequency is **5.3 KHz**;
- allow for environmental conditions and nearby machines;
- **experiment with moving the receiver to different positions** for best performance.

That last line is why step 3 says not to stick it down permanently first.

## This is an assembly job, not a console setting

It is printed as the last two pages of the assembly chapter, before the operation
instructions, and it fits a physical part with parts numbers of its own. How the console
then reads and displays a pulse is a console question and is answered elsewhere.
