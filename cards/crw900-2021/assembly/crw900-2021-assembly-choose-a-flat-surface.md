The build's last word on standing the machine straight is one line of step four:

> **Choose a flat, level surface on which to use your rower, to avoid rocking and
> potential premature wear.**

**That is the only levelling instruction in the book.** There is no adjustment, no wrench
size, no pad count and no leveller.

## The machine has no levellers to adjust

Neither carton panel counts one, no step fits one, and no drawing labels one. What the
rower stands on is **four Elevated Legs [23]**, bolted on in step five with the arched
sides facing the middle, and **four rail bumpers** refitted underneath them. None of the
four is adjustable.

## Do not carry the range's usual sentence across to this machine

Most Spirit manuals answer this question with "there are 2 leveling pads on the bottom of
the rear stabilizer, use a 14mm wrench" - including both **XRW600** printings and the
**CRW800 2021** and **2024**. **The CRW900 does not print it**, and it has neither the
pads nor a 14mm wrench in the box. See
[that card](../../ct800-2016/assembly/ct800-2016-assembly-levelling-pads.md).

**The other water rower gives a third answer** - "adjust the rear feet if the machine is
out of balance", with no count and no tool. See
[that card](../../crw800h2o/assembly/crw800h2o-assembly-levelling-rear-feet.md).

## How the absence was checked

*Level* appears in this document only in the sentence above, in the safety instruction to
install the equipment on a flat level surface, and in the tank's "multi-level" resistance
copy. *Leveler*, *leveling pad* and *glide* appear nowhere. Every page of the assembly
chapter was rendered at 300 dpi and compared against the extraction; nothing is hidden in
a flattened image.
