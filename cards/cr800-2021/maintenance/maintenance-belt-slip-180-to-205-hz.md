**This is the CR800 2020-version recumbent: both front shrouds off, nut 88, 180 to 205 Hz.** The
CU800 upright of the same year reaches its belt from the right side only and turns nut 77
(`cu800-2021-maintenance-belt-slip-right-shroud-180-to-205-hz`); the 2009 CR800 reads 180 ±10 Hz
(`spirit-recumbent-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-180-hz`).

Q: Belt Slipping — as printed:

1. Take apart both left and right front shrouds (29, 30).
2. Use a **13 mm wrench** to turn the **M8 × 7T nylon nut (88) clockwise** until the sound wave
   frequency 'falls between **180~20HZ**'. Since the machine is belt driven, slippage is possible
   depending on the weight of the user or the way the user uses it; generally slippage is rare
   (figure 1).
3. Since the driving belt itself is malleable and expendable, it is normal for it to get loose as
   time passes.

## '180~20HZ' is a misprint for 180–205 Hz

A range of 180 to 20 Hz is impossible. The same book's belt replacement step tightens the same nut
(88) on the J-bolt with the same 13 mm wrench 'until the Belt (54) is with acceptable tension' and
checks it with an **audio tension gauge within the range of 180~205HZ**, then turns the drive
pulley (20) to make sure the belt is centred without falling out. The CU800 2021 prints 180~205HZ
in both places, and the Sole LCR 2016 — the same Dyaco book under the other brand — carries the
identical '180~20HZ' misprint (`lcr-2016-belt-slips-or-falls-off`). Set the belt to **180–205 Hz**.

The rest of the Q&A page — the belt falling off, adjusting it one notch against the direction it
falls, replacing the drive pulley (20), the idler wheel assembly (10) or the generator/brake (55),
and finally the main frame (1) — is a diagnosis and belongs with the Q&A cards.

The CR800ENT 2023 book, which shares this template, prints **no slipping-belt Q&A at all**; its
only belt figure is the same 180~205HZ inside its replacement step 8-5. The CR800 owner's manuals
print no belt work (`spirit-bike-maintenance-no-lubricant-belt-or-battery-service`).
