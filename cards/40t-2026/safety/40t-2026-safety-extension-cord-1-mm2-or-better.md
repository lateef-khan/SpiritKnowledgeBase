The 4.0T manual prints the rule in capitals, as item 3 of its safety instructions:

> DO NOT USE AN EXTENSION CORD UNLESS IT IS 1 mm2 OR BETTER, WITH ONLY ONE OUTLET ON THE END. The
> treadmill should be the only appliance in the circuit in which it is connected. DO NOT ATTEMPT
> TO DISABLE THE GROUNDED PLUG BY USING IMPROPER ADAPTERS, OR IN ANY WAY MODIFY THE CORD SET. A
> serious shock or fire hazard may result along with computer malfunctions.

- **1 mm2 minimum** - a conductor cross-section, not an AWG number.
- **One outlet on the end.** Not a multi-way lead and not a power strip.
- **Never defeat the grounding pin** with an adapter, and never modify the cord set. The hazard
  named is serious shock or fire, plus computer malfunctions.

**This figure is the 4.0T's alone, and it is the thinnest one Spirit prints.** Every other Spirit
manual states a wire gauge instead:

| Manual | Extension cord minimum |
|---|---|
| 4.0T 2026 | 1 mm2 |
| 7.0T and 8.0T 2026 | 12 awg (2.5mm) |
| CT850 2016, CVC800 | 16 AWG |
| CT850 2020, XT 2015 (most) | 14 AWG |
| XT385 2015 | 18 AWG |

1 mm2 is roughly 17 AWG, so the 4.0T's figure is thinner than the 12 awg its 7.0T and 8.0T
stablemates demand and thinner than the 16 AWG of the CT850 2016. The manuals never reconcile
these; quote the figure from the machine's own manual and do not carry the 12 awg answer of
`spirit-2026t-safety-extension-cord-12-awg-or-larger` onto a 4.0T.

**The 4.0T manual asks for a dedicated 20-amp circuit** for the machine whatever cord is used:
`spirit-2026t-safety-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`.

**The 2025 printing of the 4.0T manual prints this page word for word**, so it holds for the 40t-2025 as well. The February 2025 book (revision 02.04.25) and its May 2025 reprint (revision 05.19.25) differ only in the version and revision line; both were compared with the 2026 book page by page and no rule or figure on the safety, electrical, grounding or operation pages changed.

**The Australian XT485-2013 owner's manual prints the same rule a decade earlier**, in capitals -
`DO NOT USE AN EXTENSION CORD UNLESS IT IS A 1m㎡ OR BETTER, WITH ONLY ONE OUTLET ON THE END` - on a
230-volt, 10-amp machine (`xt485-2013-safety-instructions-list`). Same gauge, same one-outlet rule.

**The 4.0T service manuals say 14 AWG.** Both the ST8700-ST017 book and its ST8700A-ST026-01 revision
print the CT850 chapter's `Do not use an extension cord unless it is a 14 AWG or better with only one
outlet on the end` (`ct850-2020-extension-cord-14-awg-or-better`). 1 mm² is roughly 17 AWG, so the
service manual asks for a heavier cord than the owner's manual; neither book mentions the other.
Quote the gauge from the book in the customer's hand and say the other figure exists.
