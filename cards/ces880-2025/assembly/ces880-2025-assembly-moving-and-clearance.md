## Towing it

> Hold the **Base Frame (77)** up with two hands and tow the item to the desired place
> carefully.
>
> - **Make sure the floor is level while towing the item.**

The transport wheels are on the front, under the main frame's front stabilizer - step
one of the build tells you to fit the front levellers with the transportation wheels
on the front. So lifting the **rear** base frame puts the machine on its front wheels.

## Floor space

> Place the item in the area where it will be used.

| | Inches | Millimetres |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum usage dimensions of the item | approx. **74" L x 38" W** | **1876mm (L) x 970mm (W)** |
| Area required for safe operation | at least **87" L x 43" W** | **2200mm (L) x 1100mm (W)** |
| Free space in front, for access and passage | at least **12"** | **300mm** |

The manual prints both unit systems, one in each column of the same page, and the two
agree.

## This is the only Spirit elliptical with either instruction

**No other CE manual prints a floor-area requirement at all** - not the CE800, the
CE800ENT, the CE850 or the CE900. And the only other one that prints anything about
moving is the CE800 2013, which says the machine has two transport wheels that engage
when the rear is lifted, and nothing more. See
[that card](../../ce800-2013/assembly/ce800-2013-assembly-transport-wheels.md); do not
carry the base-frame instruction across, because those machines have no base frame.

**The manual gives no machine weight here** and no advice on how many people it takes
to tow it, although three of the assembly steps say a job could require two people.
