**Avoid AFCI/GFCI circuit breakers if possible.** They may trip occasionally during exercise because of the high
inrush currents of the treadmill drive electronics and motor. This affects all treadmill brands, not just Sole.

New laws in your area may require these breakers. If you have them and are getting nuisance tripping:

1. Check whether any other device is plugged into the same circuit. Fluorescent lights with electronic ballasts, a
   coffee maker, a space heater and a hair drier are named as examples.
2. Optimally the treadmill should be **the only device plugged into the circuit**.

Sole treadmills have **surge suppressors built in** to help avoid nuisance tripping. Sole tested these brands and
reports they do **not** trip when connected to a Sole treadmill, as long as no other device is on the same circuit:

| Brand | Series tested |
|---|---|
| Eaton | Cutler Hammer Series |
| Leviton | Smart lock pro |
| Schneider Electric | Canadian home series |

**This is softer than what earlier Sole documents say.** The F85 2023 service manual card states flatly "never use
a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill". This 2026 owner's manual says to avoid
them "if possible", names tested breakers that pass, and treats them as acceptable when nothing else shares the
circuit. Those are different machines and different documents; this card reports what the 2026 manual says and
picks no winner.
