The manual prints these two symptoms as one row with one answer:

> The user may be walking while favoring or putting more weight on either the left or right foot. If this walking
> pattern is natural, track the belt slightly off-center to the side opposite from the belt movement. See General
> Maintenance section on Tread-belt Tension. Adjust as necessary.

So the guide's answer is a **gait** answer first: if the user naturally favours one foot, deliberately track the
belt slightly off centre, to the side **opposite** from the belt movement.

**The cross-reference is to the wrong section.** The row sends you to "Tread-belt Tension", but centring the belt
is the **Tread-belt Tracking Adjustment** section on page 30. Tension, on page 29, is the separate adjustment for
a belt that slips. Both procedures are carded; a hesitating belt is a tension symptom and an off-centre belt is a
tracking symptom, so work through the one that matches what the belt is doing.
