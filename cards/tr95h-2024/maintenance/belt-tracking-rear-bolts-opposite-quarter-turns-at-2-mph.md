This book turns **both** rear bolts, in opposite directions - unlike the other Xterra books, which turn only the left one (`xterra-treadmill-maintenance-belt-tracking-6-mm-allen-wrench-left-bolt-3-mph`). Running style and a non-level surface are the two causes it names for drift.

1. Press the power switch (at the front of the treadmill) to ON, make sure the safety key is on the console, press START, then increase speed to **2 MPH**.
2. Stand right behind the treadmill to determine which side the belt is drifting to.
3. **Drifts to the right**: turn the **right** adjustment bolt one-quarter turn **clockwise** and the **left** adjustment bolt one-quarter turn **counter-clockwise** (Fig. A).
4. **Drifts to the left**: turn the **left** bolt one-quarter turn **clockwise** and the **right** bolt one-quarter turn **counter-clockwise** (Fig. B).
5. Observe the tracking of the belt for about two minutes. Repeat steps 3, 4 and 5 as needed.

**DO NOT OVERTIGHTEN** - over tightening will cause belt damage and premature bearing failure; if the belt still slips after a lot of tightening, the drive belt under the motor cover may be the problem and is a trained-service job.

The page was read from the render: the text layer also carries an unprinted "3 kph" and "Master Power Switch" that are not on the page. The book does not name the bolt size. The 42-second factory video *TR95H Track the belt demo* corroborates the location - a technician kneels at each rear corner in turn and turns the bolt at the end of the side rail with an L-shaped hex key while the belt runs - but shows neither the direction nor the amount. The TR75H book prints nearly the same page with the bolts "at the front" and a speed of 1.9 mph: `tr75h-2025-maintenance-belt-tracking-opposite-quarter-turns-at-1-9-mph`.
