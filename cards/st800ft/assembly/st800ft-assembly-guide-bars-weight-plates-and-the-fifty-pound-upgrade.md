Step four builds each stack, and its order matters because the guide bars are loaded while
they lean:

1. **Slide the 2 Guide Bars (No. 10) into the holes at the base**, letting them lean inward
   toward the centre of the machine so parts slide on easily.
2. **Slide the 2 Support Frames (No. 13) on first, then the 2 Weight Bumpers (No. 46) on
   top of them.** Those two carry the stack.
3. **Slide 15 Weight Plates (No. 51) on, then the Top Plate (No. 52).**
4. **Tilt the guide bars back to vertical** and secure them to the frame at the top with
   2 Mount Sleeves (No. 45), 2 Washers Φ9xΦ16x1.6 (No. 77), 2 Spring Washers Φ8 (No. 74)
   and 2 M8x25 socket head cap screws (No. 62).
5. **Attach the Rubber Pedestal (No. 54), then the Pulley Connecting Frame (No. 14) to the
   Top Plate**, then the Selector Pin with Coil (No. 53) to the bolt portion of the pulley
   connecting frame. Wrench tighten.

**Guide Bar (No. 10) needs to be lubricated** before the plates go on - the note is printed
against the step drawing. The left and right guide bars of step three (Nos. 3 and 4) need
lubricating too.

## The upgrade takes the support frames out, it does not add height

If **an additional 50 lbs of plates** is bought to take a stack **from 160 lbs to
210 lbs**, the two support frames come out to make room:

1. **Disassemble the inner shroud, the two guide bars, the top plate and the 15 weight
   plates.**
2. **Take away the two Support Frames (No. 13), add 5 Weight Plates to the left stand.
   Repeat on the right stand.**

So the stack ends at **20 plates a side**, and the support frames are not refitted.

**The heavy-stack step misprints two item numbers.** It calls the top plate No. 10 and the
weight plates No. 52, while the parts list and step four call them No. 52 and No. 51. Go
by the names, not the numbers.

**Selected weight is not actual resistance on this machine** - the manual's tables halve
it, 160 selected reading 80 actual and 210 reading 105. That is a specification and lives
on its own card.
