*"The Wingate test, sometimes called the Wingate anaerobic test, is a way of measuring the strength,
power, and endurance of a person's muscles. It uses a special stationary cycling device that can
measure how many watts of energy are generated by the person pedaling the cycle as hard as possible
for a period of time."*

## Wingate Test Inputs

- **Subject Body Mass (kg)** - crucial for determining resistance.
- **Resistance Load** - **generally 75 g or 90 g per kg of body weight (0.075-0.090 kp/kg)**.
- **Flywheel Revolutions** - data recorded per 5-second interval, or total.
- **Test Time** - usually **30 seconds** of maximal pedaling.

## Wingate Test Outputs

- **Peak Power (PP)** - the highest power output achieved, usually **within the first 5 seconds**,
  expressed in watts or W/kg.
- **Mean Power (MP)** - the average power produced over the entire 30 seconds, indicating anaerobic
  capacity.
- **Fatigue Index (FI)** - the rate of power decline, **calculated as the percentage difference
  between peak power and minimum power**.
- **Minimum Power (MinP)** - the lowest power output achieved, usually at the end of the test.

**This is a different description of the test from the 8.5S's.** The 8.5S sets the load at **5% of
body weight**, prints a five-minute warm-up with three sprints and a three-minute recovery, and
allows a 15-to-300 second test; it names none of these four outputs. The 8.5S-Fit gives the load as
**75-90 g/kg** - which is 7.5% to 9% of body weight, not 5% - and prints no warm-up procedure. See
`85s-2025-programs-wingate-test-thirty-seconds-at-five-percent-of-body-weight` and do not merge the
two.
