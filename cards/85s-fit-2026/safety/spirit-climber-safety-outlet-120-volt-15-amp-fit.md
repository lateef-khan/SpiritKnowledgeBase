**120 volt AC, 15 amp, grounded.** The 8.5S-Fit states it three times and all three agree.

| Where | Printed as |
|---|---|
| Safety instructions, item 2 | `a 120 Volt AC, 15-amp grounded outlet` |
| Grounding instructions | `This product is for use on a nominal 120-volt circuit` |
| Specifications | `120V/15AMP Cord/Outlet` |

- **Nothing else on the circuit.** `The 8.5S-Fit should be the only appliance in the electrical
  circuit.`
- **An extension cord must be 16awg or larger, with only one outlet on the end.**
- **Do not disable the grounded plug with an improper adapter** or modify the cord set - item 2 says
  a serious shock or fire hazard may result along with computer malfunctions.

**This is the one figure that separates the 8.5S-Fit from the 8.5S.** The two manuals are otherwise
the same 24-item safety page, and the 8.5S asks for **100 to 240-volt AC, 50/60 Hz**
(`spirit-climber-safety-outlet-100-to-240-volt-15-amp`). The 8.5S-Fit is a single-voltage North
American machine; never tell an 8.5S-Fit owner it will run on 230 volts.

**Its grounding page contradicts item 2 in one respect.** Item 2 forbids adapters outright, and the
grounding page then describes a temporary adapter for a two-pole receptacle and says it must be held
in place by a metal screw (`spirit-temporary-adapter-for-a-two-pole-receptacle`). The 8.5S, 7.0S and
7.5S grounding pages print the adapter illustration but not that paragraph. Both sentences are
printed and the manual never reconciles them.

**Its electrical page is the residential Spirit page, not the medical one.** It names AFCI/GFCI
breaker brands it has tested - Eaton (Cutler Hammer Series), Leviton (Smart lock pro) and Schneider
Electric (Canadian home series) - and says they do not trip as long as no other device shares the
circuit. The 8.5S, 7.0S, 7.5S and MS300 print the IEC 60601-1 climatic block there instead
(`spirit-climber-safety-operating-environment-iec-60601`).
