Row 1 of the mechanical `Troubleshooting guide`, three checks, and what each leads to:

| Check | How, as printed | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Check if there are interactions between chain cover and the flywheel | Turn the flywheel and check if there is gap between chain cover and the flywheel or if the flywheel swings or any other interactions. | If there are interactions, Step 1: remove the chain covers and check the screw on the bearing housing is loose; if not, check whether the covers are deformed, and **replace a deformed chain cover**. |
| 2. Check if there is any crack on the roller | Pull up the connecting arm, turn the rollers and see if there is crack or breakage on them. | **Replace the roller** (the wheel-cover, C-ring and wave-washer procedure of check 4-1). |
| 3. Check if the magnet system interacts with the flywheel | Release the bolt under the right handlebar assembly and the bolt securing the crank axle, remove the pedal arm assembly, the crank axle cover and the bolt on the crank axle, the two screws at the rear of the left chain cover and the right chain cover's screws, unplug the DC power cable, take the right chain cover off, and turn the flywheel to see if it interacts with the external magnet system. | If it does, Step 2: set the resistance to maximum so the magnet housing touches the stopper screw, and adjust the screw until the magnet-to-flywheel distance is **2mm**; if the control key then runs it wrongly, tighten the steel cable as far as possible while keeping the 2mm. |

**Two of the three end in a part** - a chain cover or a roller - and the third in the 2 mm magnet gap, which is an adjustment. That adjustment, and the belt replacement that follows it as Step 4, are care procedures the maintenance cards carry; this card is the diagnosis.
