The belt tension is set before shipping and **should not need adjusting when the bike is new**.
It may need a minor adjustment over time.

**When to adjust:** move the crank arms back and forth. If there is more than **1/4" (0.64 cm)**
of movement in the crank before the flywheel starts turning, the belt needs tightening.

1. Loosen the **two flanged nuts (A)**, one on either side of the flywheel.
2. Move the flywheel forward by **evenly tightening clockwise the 2 hex bolts (B)** that go through
   the frame tube, until there is approximately **1/4" (0.64 cm) of slack in the belt**.
3. Re-tighten the flanged nut on either side of the flywheel, then put the safety cover back on.

**Adjust both sides equally** — tighten or loosen together — so that the flywheel stays in
alignment with the frame.

The same 1/4" figure means two different things here: it is the *maximum acceptable crank play*
that triggers the job, and the *target slack in the belt* when you finish.

## The CIC850 job is a different procedure

The CIC850 2022 prints its own BELT TENSION ADJUSTMENT with the same opening paragraph and a
**completely different set of steps** — guards come off first and the adjustment is made at
numbered screws 55 and 17, with no crank-play figure and no slack figure at all:
`cic850-2022-maintenance-belt-tension-behind-the-top-and-right-guards`. **Neither procedure works
on the other machine.**

Do not carry the commercial-bike answer here either. Twelve Spirit commercial bike owner's manuals
prescribe no drive belt work of any kind
(`spirit-bike-maintenance-no-lubricant-belt-or-battery-service`), and the 180-210 N figure in the
CU900ENT service manual is a technician spec for that machine
(`cu900ent-belt-slips-or-falls-off`). The CIC800 owner's manual prints no tension force.
