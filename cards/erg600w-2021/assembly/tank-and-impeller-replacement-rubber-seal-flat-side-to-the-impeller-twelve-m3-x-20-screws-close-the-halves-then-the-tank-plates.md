1. Take the tank plate off after all the steps of the preceding pages, and take the tank out of the main frame.
2. Take out the tank assembly and remove all the screws from the middle of the upper and lower tank; the tank, impeller and rubber parts can then be replaced.
3. Prepare the new plastic impeller: put the **rubber seal through the shaft, flat side towards the impeller**. Turn the upper tank over and insert a **black rubber seal** in its middle hole; place the **big rubber sealing ring** properly in the upper tank (figure 1).
4. Insert the impeller shaft into the tank hole - "you may need a base to support the tank". Cover with the other tank half and secure with **12 M3 x 20 stainless screws** in the middle (figure 2). Insert a **white foam spacer** into the middle sunken area of the upper tank.
5. Assemble the tank onto the main frame. Put the tank plate on the tank; use the S4 Allen wrench to tighten **6 M6 x 15 screws** on the upper tank and the S5 wrench to tighten **4 M8 x 15 screws** (figure 3).
6. Put the magnet seat onto the tank plate, match the hole with the shaft, then drive in a pin to fix the shaft's position. Attach a large stainless washer and brush on some oil.
7. Refit the mesh belt wheel and handlebar as on their page.
8. Stand the main frame up and secure the lower tank plate with **6 M6 x 15 screws** in the bottom of the tank and **2 M8 x 15 screws** in the side frame.

The Q&A page on leaks: a little water from the middle of the upper tank when the rower is stood up is normal; leaks from the bottom mean a cracked lower tank, leaks from the join a bad sealing ring. The Spirit CRW800H2O's tank uses a mechanical sealing ring, graphite spacer and glue - a different seal set, so its card is a look-alike.
