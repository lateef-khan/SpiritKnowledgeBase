**Condition 1 - the user experiences slipping during pedaling.**

1. Remove the left and right front shrouds (A48 and A49).
2. Use an open-end wrench to adjust the **idler wheel assembly adjustment screw (A13)** until the belt no
   longer slips.

**No tension figure** is given - no newtons, no hertz, no deflection - only "until the belt no longer
slips". The Spirit XBR service manual sets the same job at 450 N on a gauge
(`spirit-xbr-maintenance-belt-slip-nyloc-nut-88-450-newtons`); that figure is not evidence for this machine.

**Condition 2 - resistance suddenly drops to zero, "similar to a bicycle chain coming off".** This is the
drive belt (A12) slipping off. As printed:

1. Reinstall the drive belt following the installation steps (the flywheel-and-belt replacement pages are
   assembly cards).
2. After installation and adjustment, rotate the drive belt forward and backward to test its stability.
3. If the belt slips off, **adjust the drive belt one notch in the opposite direction of the slipping** and
   test again.
4. If the belt continues to fall off, the machine has probably been dropped or struck and is out of
   dimension: replace the **Small Iron Tube (A84)** and the **Idler Wheel Assembly (A16/A17)**.
5. If the issue still persists, the Main Frame (A) may be deformed and the entire machine may need to be
   replaced - rare, the manual says, since every machine is tested before shipping.

The page ends: if these steps do not resolve the issue, contact the dealer.

The service manual has **no maintenance chapter** - its contents are parts, troubleshooting and
replacement procedures - and the owner's manual's care page is the post-workout wipe-down and sanitizing
routine (`xterra-treadmill-maintenance-post-workout-care`). The Sole R92 and the Spirit recumbents print
the same one-notch rule (`r92-2023-belt-slips-or-falls-off`,
`spirit-recumbent-errors-drive-belt-falls-off-one-notch-then-drive-pulley-idler-or-brake`). Other brands.
