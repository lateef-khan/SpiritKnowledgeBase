> **WARNING!** Use of a GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter) or RCD (Residual Current Device) wall
> outlet with this treadmill, as with any appliance with a large motor, may result in tripping the
> circuit breaker often. Route the power cord away from any moving part of the treadmill including
> the elevation mechanism and transport wheels.

**This is a warning about nuisance tripping, not a prohibition.** The sentence says a GFCI or RCD
outlet `may result in tripping the circuit breaker often`; it does not say never use one, and it
names no alternative. That is a third position in the Spirit range:

| Wording | Machines |
|---|---|
| `NEVER use a ground fault circuit interrupt (GFCI) wall outlet with this treadmill` - a ban | CT850 2016, 2018, 2020 and ENT-2022, the CT800 family, the 4.0T, the XT685-2010 and the XT485-2013 (`spirit-never-use-a-gfci-wall-outlet`) |
| `Avoid AFCI/GFCI circuit breakers if possible`, with tested breaker brands | the 2015 and 2023 XT manuals (`xt-2023-safety-afci-gfci-nuisance-tripping`) |
| **`may result in tripping the circuit breaker often`** - this card | **CT850-2013** |

- **RCD and GFCI are named together**, as the ENT-2022 and 2026 books do; the 2016, 2018 and 2020
  CT850 books say GFCI alone.
- **The reason is the same as the ban's** - a large motor - so an installer who fits a GFCI should
  expect trips; the manual just does not tell them not to.
- **The cord-routing sentence is unchanged** from the rest of the family
  (`spirit-route-the-power-cord-away-from-moving-parts`).

**The rest of the page is the CT850 family's electrical page.** The cover rule, the 10 % voltage
variation, 40 to 120 degrees Fahrenheit at 95 % humidity, the high-inrush breaker paragraph closing
`The electrical outlet used should have a dedicated 20 amp circuit breaker`, and a grounding page
naming `a 120-volt/20 amp dedicated circuit` (`spirit-house-breaker-needs-a-high-inrush-type`,
`spirit-ct850-outlet-and-circuit-requirement`, `spirit-product-must-be-grounded`).

**The book dates itself.** Its registration page stamps `CT850_20131015` and its warranty is
effective November 1, 2013; the folder and file name say 2012, and that is the file's date, not the
book's. Quote this wording only to a customer holding the 2013 book, and the ban to one holding the
2016 or later book.
