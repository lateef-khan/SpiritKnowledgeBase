1-12. Follow the twelve belt-replacement steps (chain covers, the M6 nyloc nuts, the M8 hex head bolt and M6 eye bolt, the two M5 bolts, the RPM sensor, the front stabilizer's four M10 bolts - two out, two loosened - rotated down 45 degrees, the two M12 nylon nuts with the 19 mm wrench, and the flywheel assembly out with a rag under it). **Set the belt tension to 100 to 110 pounds on refitting.**
13. Use a **14 mm open-end wrench** to remove **2 M8 hex head bolts (#139)** and the **lock washers (#122)**.
14. Remove the brake assembly.
15. Use diagonal pliers to cut the cable tie, then unplug the **computer cable**. Reverse the steps to refit.

**The brake assembly is the whole eddy-current resistance unit:** the book's electrical-configuration page describes it as neodymium magnets, a gear motor that sets the resistance level, and a closed-loop encoder for brake position. Its limit sensor board and DC-motor encoder board are drawn on the wiring pages, which are a separate section; this book prints no procedure for replacing them on their own.

The troubleshooting chapter's MOTOR ERROR answer ends "replace brake assembly" and "replace the limit sensor board & controller"; this is the brake replacement, and the lower control board has its own card. After replacing, the console's calibration offset number (written on the console mounting bracket) is what the maintenance-mode calibration uses; that is a console card.
