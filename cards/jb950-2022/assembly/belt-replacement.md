**Belt tension: 100 to 110 pounds.**

1. Use a Phillips head screwdriver to remove **four 12 mm Phillips head screws (#134)** and **two 16 mm sheet-metal screws (#131)** from both chain covers (R and L).
2. Move the crank arm to the horizontal position to make the chain cover easier to take off.
3. Remove both chain covers (R and L).
4. Use a **10 mm open-end wrench** to remove the **M6 nyloc nut**.
5. Use a **13 mm open-end wrench** to remove the **M8 hex head bolt (#139)** and the **M6 eye bolt (#129)**.
6. Use the 10 mm open-end wrench to remove the **M6 nyloc nut (#171)**.
7. Use an **M4 Allen wrench** to remove **two M5 bolts (#145)** and **flat washers (#120)**.
8. Use the Phillips head screwdriver to loosen the **M5 Phillips head screw (#134)** and remove the **RPM sensor (#86)**.
9. Stand the main frame up to make the front stabilizer assembly's bottom screws easy to reach.
10. There are **four M10 hex head bolts** in the **front stabilizer assembly**: **remove the two at the front and loosen the two at the back**.
11. **Rotate the front stabilizer assembly (#5) down about 45 degrees.**
12. Use a **19 mm open-end wrench** to remove **two M12 nylon nuts (#169)** and the M6 eye bolt (#129), then remove the **flywheel assembly**. Replace the belt. **Use a rag on the front stabilizer assembly (#5) to avoid scratching the flywheel.** Reverse the steps to refit. **Adjust the belt tension to between 100 and 110 pounds.**

**The front stabilizer pivots rather than coming off.** Two of its four M10 bolts are only loosened; the stabilizer swings down on them so the flywheel can come forward. Steps 1 to 12 are printed identically at the start of the brake assembly procedure, which continues with three more steps.

The tension is printed in pounds only, and no gauge is named. The 9 V adapter aside, the console is unpowered with the belt off, since the generator is belt-driven from the crank.
