**There is no power button and no batteries.** The console is powered by the rider. The maintenance
mode chapter states it plainly: **"Pedaling above 30RPM is all that is needed to illuminate the
console"**, and **"It will be necessary for light to moderate pedaling of bike to power on the
console"**.

**The wake sequence is fixed**, and it always lands you in Warm Up Mode:

1. **`JOHNNY G. METHOD`** displays.
2. **`THE RIDE OF TRUTH`** displays.
3. **`WARM UP`** flashes **3 times**.
4. **`PRESS PLAY KEY, WHEN READY TO GO`** displays.
5. The **Home screen** appears, showing **LEVEL, RPM, TIME, DISTANCE, WATTS, HEART RATE**.

**The console then stays in Warm Up Mode until the Play Key is pressed** - as long as the rider
wants. Warm Up Mode never shows a summary screen; only the program modes do.

**A blank console is normal on a bike nobody is riding.** Nothing is wrong; nothing has gone flat.
Pedal, and give it a few seconds.

**Which also means every menu needs a pedalling rider.** Maintenance Mode cannot be reached, entered
or changed without someone keeping the cranks turning, so a technician working alone has to pedal and
press at the same time.

**A cord can hold the console on without a rider**, but only for a trade show: Demo Mode needs a
plug-in cord that connects to the bike's electrical board and mains electricity. See
`jb950-2022-console-demo-mode`.

**The service manual adds a way to power the console without pedalling**: an optional **9 V power
adapter**, "available as a service part through your Spirit distributor", used to reach Maintenance
Mode and change settings. The owner's manual never mentions it; the same book's Demo Mode page still
says a plug-in adapter that connects to the bike's electrical board is required to keep the display
on.
