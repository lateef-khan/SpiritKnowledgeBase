Auto-pilot is a heart rate program that lets you enter Heart Rate Control **on the fly**.

**At any time, during any program**, press the **Auto-pilot** button and the console switches to
heart rate control mode and maintains your heart rate at the level it was at when you pressed the
button.

The manual's own example: if you are in Manual mode and your heart rate is at **150 beats per
minute**, pressing Auto-pilot switches the console to heart rate control and keeps you at 150 beats
per minute automatically.

**Only the XE550 has it.** The XE150/XE350/XE550 owner's manual heads the section "Auto-pilot
(**XE550 only**)" and repeats "(XE550 only. See Heart Rate section for details of this feature)"
beside every program's steps. The XE350 does not have it, and neither does the XE150, whose console
is a different design entirely.

**The XE100/XE200/XE300 manual repeats this page, and that is not evidence the XE300 has it.** That
book prints the same Auto-pilot paragraph at the end of its XE300 console chapter and refers to the
feature five times without a qualifier - but two of its own references still read "**(XE550 only.**
See Heart Rate section...)", naming a machine that book does not cover. The whole block is lifted
from the XE150/XE350/XE550 book. No manual states that an XE100, XE200 or XE300 has an Auto-pilot
button, so this card does not claim one.

**This is not the same feature as the 2026 treadmill's HR Auto Pilot**, despite the shared name and
the shared idea of holding the rate you are already at. On the Spirit 4.0T, auto pilot works **only**
in the Fitness programs (Manual, 5K, Hill, Plateau, Interval and Custom), **requires a heart rate
strap**, shows **NO HEART RATE** if none is detected, and ends with a workout summary. The XE550
manual states none of those four things and says instead that it works at any time in any program -
see `40t-2026-programs-hr-auto-pilot`. The two cards are deliberately kept apart because the
restrictions differ.
