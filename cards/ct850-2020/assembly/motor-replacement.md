1. Use a screwdriver to remove the **Motor Base Cap (L)** and **(R)**, then remove the
   **Motor Top Cover**.
2. Remove the motor grounding wire (**green yellow**) and the computer cables (**U, V, W**), and use
   a **14mm open end wrench** on the tension screw and an **M8 L-shape Allen wrench** to release the
   motor adjusting bolts.
3. Use an **M8 L Allen wrench** to remove the **4 motor locking bolts** under the treadmill.
4. Replace the motor with the new one after removing the motor belt, then resume parts in reverse
   order, including the motor belt. **Make sure that the belt is parallel to the frame, and do not
   tighten the 4 bolts yet.**
5. Use the **14mm open end wrench** to adjust the belt tension with a tension gauge until the tension
   reads in the **white range between 70~75 Lbs**. Then use the **M8 L-shape Allen wrench** to
   tighten the **4 motor adjusting bolts** to secure.
6. Connect the grounding wire and computer cable back.

The manual labels the three motor leads **U, V and W**.

The photograph beside step 5 shows the gauge being used: an **SPX OTC 6673 Belt Tension Gauge**, set
against the motor belt and read on its **LBS** scale. The text never names the gauge; the model
number is legible on the tool in the picture, which is the same photograph the 2016 manual prints.

The CT850 2016 manual prints the same six steps with the same **70~75 Lbs** and the same 14mm and M8
tools, but it also gives screw counts for the caps and the top cover, which this manual does not.
See `ct850-2016-motor-replacement`.

The roller procedure sends you back to step 5 for the belt tension, citing "step 11.3 (5)".

**Four books print these six steps word for word**, with the same 70~75 Lbs, 14mm open end wrench, M8 Allen
wrench and 4 bolts: the CT850 2020 and CT800 2020 as section 11-3 "Motor Replacement", the CT800ENT and
CT850ENT as section 9-3 "Drive Motor replacement". This card covers all four. The 4.0T 2026 book prints the
2016 version, with the screw counts, and shares the CT850 2016 card.
