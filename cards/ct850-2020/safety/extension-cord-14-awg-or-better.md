> Do not use an extension cord unless it is a 14 AWG or better with only one outlet on the end. Do
> not attempt to disable the grounded plug by using improper adapters or in any way modify the cord
> outlet.

- **14 AWG minimum.**
- **One outlet on the end.**
- **Never defeat the grounding pin** with an adapter, and never modify the cord or the outlet.

**This manual prints two gauges.** Its own troubleshooting table (p. 46, printed 45) says an
extension cord `should be as short as possible and heavy duty 16-gauge minimum`, which is thinner
than the 14 AWG the safety chapter demands. See `ct850-2020-only-reaches-7-mph`.

**The CT850 2016 manual and the CVC800 climber print 16 AWG in the same safety sentence**:
`spirit-extension-cord-16-awg-or-better`. A 16 AWG cord meets those manuals and not this one.

**The Spirit CT800ENT-2022 owner's manual prints both sentences**, in capitals, in the WARNING
header of its safety instructions:

> DO NOT USE AN EXTENSION CORD UNLESS IT IS 14 AWG OR LARGER, WITH ONLY ONE OUTLET ON THE END.

> DO NOT ATTEMPT TO DISABLE THE GROUNDED PLUG BY USING IMPROPER ADAPTERS, OR IN ANY WAY MODIFY THE
> CORD SET. A serious shock or fire hazard may result along with computer malfunctions.

It says `14 AWG or larger` where the CT850 manual says `a 14 AWG or better` - the same requirement,
since a larger conductor is a lower AWG number. One outlet on the end, in both.

**This manual prints two gauges too.** Its own troubleshooting section says an extension cord
`should be as short as possible and heavy duty, 16-gauge minimum`, thinner than the 14 AWG the
safety page demands - the same disagreement the CT850 2020 manual has, and neither manual
reconciles it.

**The CT800 2012, 2016 and 2020 manuals print no extension-cord requirement** on their safety
pages. Their only mention of one is the same troubleshooting line, which asks for a lead
`as short as possible and heavy duty 16 gauge minimum` - a different sentence for a different
purpose. Do not quote 14 AWG for those three.
