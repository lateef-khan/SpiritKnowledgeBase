| Component | Warranty period |
|---|---|
| **Frame Weldments** | **Lifetime** |
| **Parts** | **3 Years** |
| **Labor** | **1 Year** |
| **Cosmetic Items\*** | **90 Days** |

> **\* Cosmetic items including, but not limited to the following: grips, console overlays, and labels/decals.**

Every period above runs **from the date of retail sale, as determined by sale receipt**, or **in the absence of a sales receipt, eighteen (18) months from the original factory shipping date**. The table sits under a heading reading **"Effective February 1, 2023"**, printed exactly that way in a 2026 manual.

**The Labor row is stated in two places and the other half adds something.** This table says **Labor 1 Year**. The Service section on the same page sets the labour clock as **twelve (12) months from the date on the bill of sale** *or* **eighteen (18) months from the date of factory shipping**, as determined by the serial number - the same one year, plus an alternative start the table does not mention.

**The parts term differs from the E35 of the same year, and this is not a transcription slip.** The E35-2026 manual's equivalent table reads **"Effective February 1, 2026"** and gives **Parts 2 Years**; this E95 table gives **Parts 3 Years**. Frame Weldments, Labor and Cosmetic Items are identical across the two. So the longer E95 parts term is a real difference between the models - but it is printed on a page dated **2023** while the E35 page is dated **2026**. Both facts are stated here and neither is adjudicated: quote the table as printed and escalate if a customer's coverage turns on which date governs.
