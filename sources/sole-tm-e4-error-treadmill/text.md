E4 Error (Treadmills)
Improper Motor Voltage
(If error comes up after machine has been assembled, replaced Computer Cables)

This error can happen if the drive motor wires have come loose, if the motor controller is damaged, or if the drive motor is damaged and not operational. Perform the following to inspect the drive motor and motor controller as well as test the functionality of the drive motor to see where the problem lies: 

Unplug the machine and remove the Motor Hood Cover to check where the drive motor wires plug into the motor controller
Make sure that the drive motor wires are plugged in tight and see if issue persists
Inspect for damage to the drive motor wires and controller, then perform the following
If drive motor wires or motor controller is visibly damaged, replace damaged parts and move on to Step 2
If no visible damage is found, perform a battery test on the Drive Motor
If the Drive Motor passes the Battery Test, replace the Motor Controller and move on to Step 2
If the Drive Motor fails the Battery Test, replace Drive Motor and move on to Step 2
Ask about Lubrication (Question: How often do you lubricate?)
Whether the machine has been properly lubricated or not, check Running Belt and Running Deck have been worn or damaged (Slide hand between running belt and deck palm up first. See if texture of running belt is gone in the middle. Palm down, see if running deck is damaged.)
If Running Belt and Running Deck are in good condition, move on to Step 3
If Running Belt or Running Deck are worn or damaged, replace Running Belt, Running Deck (Running Deck only has to be replaced if it is worn or damaged), move on to Step 3
Ask if they have squeaking or knocking noises coming from the machine.
If answer is yes, asking if noise is coming from front or rear of the machine, depending on answer replaced Front Roller or Rear Roller (possibly both)

Battery Test Instructions

You’ll need:
A fresh 9V battery (or power drill battery)
A Phillips screwdriver

Step 1: Unplug the Treadmill
Make sure the machine is completely powered off and unplugged from the wall before beginning.

Step 2: Remove the Motor Cover
Use a Phillips screwdriver to loosen and remove the 5 screws securing the motor cover.
Carefully lift off the cover to expose the internal components.

Step 3: Locate the Drive Motor Wires
Follow the two red and black wires (could be red and white depending on the model) coming from the drive motor to where they connect to the motor controller.

Step 4: Unplug the Motor Wires
Make a note or take a photo of where the wires plug into the motor controller, so you can reattach them correctly later.
Unplug the two motor wires from the board.

Step 5: Expose the Wire Contacts
Slide back the plastic insulation on the wire ends to expose the metal contacts.

Step 6: Perform the Battery Test
Touch one motor wire to the positive terminal of the 9V battery.
Touch the other motor wire to the negative terminal of the battery.

Note: It does not matter which wire goes to which terminal. It will only influence the direction that the motor moves.

Step 7: Observe the Motor
Watch to see if the motor moves or twitches when the wires touch the battery. The motor is fine if it moves at all.
